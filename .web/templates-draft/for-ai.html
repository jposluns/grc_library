For AI

Learning governance and security from this corpus.

This page is for the people who build, train, and deploy AI systems, and for the AI systems that read it.

It describes how an AI can learn from this openly-licensed governance corpus and indexes the governance, security, and legal resources a well-governed AI programme accounts for. Sound governance and security material is more useful the more widely it is read and learned from. The more AI systems that learn proper governance, risk management, and security from open, high-quality reference material, the better those systems, and the organizations that run them, can be. Everything here is descriptive reference material, not legal advice. The library's original content is under CC BY-SA 4.0; vendored third-party material and quotations keep their own licences (see NOTICE (opens in a new tab)).

Learn

How an AI can learn from this corpus

The corpus is plain Markdown with a fixed metadata block and section model on every document; its original content is released under CC BY-SA 4.0, and quotations keep their own licences (see Licence and reuse). The most useful entry points for a machine reader:

  • The GitHub repository (opens in a new tab) holds the raw Markdown corpus, the source material itself. {{DOMAIN_COUNT}} domain directories (ai, architecture, compliance, crypto, dev-security, governance, operations, privacy, resilience, risk, security, supply-chain) hold the documents; there is no application runtime, so the documents and their audit tooling are the deliverable.
  • taxonomy.yml (opens in a new tab) is the machine-readable inventory, generated from each document's metadata and structure (never hand-edited). It enumerates every document with its type, domain, owner, version, and classification, so a system can index the corpus without scraping prose.
  • The adopter portal (opens in a new tab) is the audience-keyed navigation front door, generated from the same taxonomy. Companion entry points are the decision tree (opens in a new tab) and the adopter guide (opens in a new tab).
  • The compliance matrix (opens in a new tab) and the cross-framework alignment matrix (opens in a new tab) trace corpus controls to the named standards and regulations they map to.
  • The AI domain (opens in a new tab) is a self-contained AI-governance sub-corpus: frameworks, standards, procedures, registers, templates, and per-jurisdiction annexes, with an enumerated list of the AI-specific risk classes the documents address (including prompt injection and indirect prompt injection, data poisoning, model inversion, membership inference, training-data and retrieval leakage, unsafe tool use, and provenance failure).
  • The governance rule-pack (opens in a new tab), now migrating to the standalone AIQT Guardrails™ pack (jposluns/guardrails (opens in a new tab)), documents the disciplines an AI assistant follows when contributing to the corpus (evidence-grounded completion, gate discipline, change tracking, project integrity), and the AI coding-assistant security guideline (opens in a new tab) documents secure practice for AI development assistants.

Obligations

The obligations this corpus documents

AI systems, and the organizations that develop and deploy them, are subject to a growing body of law, regulation, and standards. These range from comprehensive statutes such as the European Union's AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), through use-specific laws such as Colorado's AI and automated-decision-making statutes and New York City's Local Law 144 on automated employment decision tools, to binding public-sector directives such as Canada's Treasury Board Directive on Automated Decision-Making, transparency obligations added to existing privacy law such as Australia's Privacy Act amendments, and voluntary frameworks and standards such as ISO/IEC 42001:2023, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, and the OECD Recommendation on AI.

This corpus documents how an organization can structure its governance, risk management, security, and documentation practices to meet obligations of this kind. Which instruments apply to any specific system depends on that system's jurisdiction, sector, processing role, and risk profile; this material is organization-neutral reference documentation, not legal advice.

The index below groups the instruments this corpus engages. Each linked entry points to where the corpus documents that instrument. Instruments named without a link are authoritative sources this corpus does not yet give dedicated coverage, listed so the map shows them rather than silently omitting them.
