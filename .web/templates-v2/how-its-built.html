The corpus is the system

§01

One source of truth; the site is a projection.

There is one corpus, and the site’s data is derived from it. This site’s generator reads an explicit allow-list of corpus inputs and recomputes every generated figure (document counts, area listings, the version) at build time, so no generated figure is hand-typed and a repository file outside the allow-list cannot leak onto the public site. The editorial prose and the curated executive framing are authored and reviewed by the maintainer. The site you are reading renders library version {{CALVER}}, and that number came from the corpus, not from an editor.

Quality, enforced

§02

A continuously expanding suite of quality gates.

Every change runs a suite of automated Python gates before it can land. Among the families: link integrity across the corpus; citation currency against the canonical register, so a reference to an edition the register already marks superseded is flagged; taxonomy drift, so the machine-readable inventory cannot fall out of step with the documents; web-to-corpus link resolution against a committed manifest, so a corpus file renamed or deleted without regenerating the site is caught; and safety screening for secrets and personal data.

You will find no gate count on this page, deliberately: the suite grows continuously, and any number printed here would understate it and go stale. The generator behind this site computes no such count for the same reason. What automation never does is decide content: gates enforce integrity; the accountable maintainer decides what the library says. That boundary is stated plainly on the trust page.

On every change, and every night

§03

Continuous integration, three layers.

The gates run in CI on every proposed change, so nothing merges ungated. A scheduled nightly sweep re-runs the full gate suite against the whole corpus, so time-dependent drift that no single change would trip (a citation whose recorded re-verification has fallen due, a reference to an edition the register already marks superseded) surfaces without waiting for the next edit. And the site generator has its own health check, which fails loudly if the corpus coupling it depends on ever breaks, rather than publishing wrong figures quietly.

Provenance on the record

§04

Citation registers, with inspectable upstream status.

External claims are anchored in registers, not in prose. The Canonical Citations Register↗ records every cited standard and regulation with its current edition, known superseded versions, the authoritative upstream page where currency is re-checked, and the date it was last verified, and a companion linter uses it to flag stale references across the corpus. Entries are additionally subject to publisher-source verification, recorded row by row; until an entry is verified, the register says so rather than implying provenance it does not have.

Never hand-maintained

§05

Indexes are generated, so they cannot silently drift.

The document inventory (taxonomy.yml↗), the adopter portal↗, the executive narrative registry, and this site’s own web-to-corpus link manifest are all generated artefacts, regenerated from the corpus and gated for drift. An index that is computed cannot quietly disagree with the documents it indexes: when the corpus changes, the indexes change in the same commit or the build fails.

Who gates the gates

§06

The toolchain is itself tested.

The linters and generators that enforce all of the above are covered by their own test suite in the repository, run in the same CI. A quality gate you cannot trust is worse than none; testing the tooling is how the gates earn the authority they exercise.

For AI-assisted teams

§07

The discipline behind the machine, open for reuse.

The corpus is maintained largely by an AI assistant working under an open pack of governance rules and skills (evidence before assertion, no gate weakened to force a pass) with every change approved by the accountable maintainer. The pack ships under the same CC BY-SA 4.0 licence, written to be lifted into any AI-assisted project.
