Concerns, not document types

§01

The decisions a governing body owns.

Each card below is a decision or exposure leadership is accountable for — not a folder of files. Behind each one sit published leadership pages (briefs, scenarios, journeys, decision narratives, and oversight question sets), and every claim in them resolves to the governing documents in the corpus. Where coverage does not exist yet, we say so.

Accountability & decision rights

Who owns this decision — and where is that written?

Every governance decision has an accountable owner. The corpus fixes where each authority sits, so a decision cannot fall between roles.

Cybersecurity & operational resilience

Could we take the hit and keep operating?

Protective controls plus the ability to withstand and recover from disruption — an identity-provider compromise, a compromised signing key, a failed critical service.

Data, privacy & the information lifecycle

Do we control our information from creation to destruction?

Personal and sensitive information governed across its whole life: classification, cross-border movement, retention, and evidenced destruction.

Supplier & cloud dependency

How exposed are we through the parties we depend on?

Concentration, exit, and assurance over third parties — including what happens the day a critical supplier fails.

Compliance & assurance

Can we show, with evidence, that our controls operate?

Controls mapped to obligations and tested so an assurer can rely on them — evidenced, not asserted.

Risk acceptance & exceptions

Which risks have we knowingly accepted — and who signed?

Defensible, time-bound risk acceptance with named owners, instead of silent exceptions that surface in the post-incident review.

Enterprise architecture & technology debt

What is our accumulated technology debt costing us?

Architecture decisions tied to governance, so deferred modernization is a visible, owned exposure rather than a surprise.

Workforce, conduct & operating model

Do the people running the controls have the mandate to run them?

People, roles, screening, conduct, and the operating model that actually runs the controls day to day.

AI & autonomous systems

What is AI doing on our behalf — and who approved it?

AI governed from intake through production approval, oversight, incidents, and retirement — one exposure among the ten, governed like the rest.

Programme establishment & maturity

Where does our programme stand — and what would better look like?

Leadership pages for this concern are not yet published: a declared coverage gap, not a hidden one. Coverage here is measured by concerns with supported routes, never padded by page count.

Qualified, deliberately

§02

What it does for you — and what it doesn’t.

Oversight

Walk into the board meeting with the questions — and the follow-ups

Oversight question sets and executive briefs give a governing body substance to probe with, each claim resolving to a governing document. Supports informed oversight; does not replace judgement or prove any control operates in your organization.

Programme

Document a programme in weeks, not quarters

Charters, policies, standards, registers, and templates, written to be adopted and adapted. Supports faster drafting and a coherent structure; adopting documents alone is not a functioning programme — implementation and approval remain yours.

Obligations

See where your obligations are covered — and where they aren’t

Crosswalks map controls to recognized frameworks and laws. Supports gap analysis and audit preparation; does not certify compliance with any framework, standard, or regulation.

Reuse

Adopt it freely, including commercially

Everything is CC BY-SA 4.0: read, adopt, adapt, and use internally or commercially, with attribution. Supports adoption at no licence cost; it is reference material, not legal, security, or compliance advice.

§03

Choose your starting situation.

New programme

Starting from nothing

No documented programme yet. Begin from the startup roadmap and quick-start templates and stand up the minimum viable structure first.

Undocumented programme

Running, but undocumented

Controls exist in practice but not on paper. Baseline what you already do against the reference set and close the gaps in priority order.

Regulated obligation

Facing a regulated obligation

An audit, a regulator, or a certification target with a date on it. Work backwards from the obligation through the compliance mappings to the documents that support it.

Multi-entity

Governing a group of entities

Subsidiaries, shared services, mixed jurisdictions. One coherent governance model, tailored at entity level without forking the programme.

§04

Why you could rely on it — and how to check.

Accountable stewardship

A named human maintainer approves and answers for every change. Nothing is published anonymously.

Transparent gaps

What is not covered is declared on the page it affects, never hidden behind page counts.

Primary-source alignment

External standards are cited against a canonical citations register and flagged when an edition is superseded.

Open licence

CC BY-SA 4.0 throughout: adopt, adapt, and reuse with attribution — commercially included.

Current & versioned

This is library version {{CALVER}}; every figure on this site is recomputed from the corpus on every change.

Automation checks, humans decide

Automated gates enforce integrity — links, citations, consistency. Content and judgement stay with the accountable maintainer.
