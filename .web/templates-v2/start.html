Situation one

§01

Starting from nothing.

No documented programme yet, perhaps a first security hire, a first board question, or a first customer questionnaire. The mistake to avoid is adopting everything at once; the corpus is built to be entered smallest-first.

  1. Fix the minimum structure first. The Minimum Viable Governance Structure Guideline↗ defines three tiers of governance structure, from minimum-viable through mid-market to enterprise/regulated. Pick your tier and name the seats before adopting a single control document.
  2. Adopt the minimum viable set. The Adopter Quickstart Template↗ defines the minimum viable adoption: the smallest coherent document set a new programme stands up first.
  3. Plan the build-out around what you actually do. The Adopter Startup Roadmap Template↗ stages a core baseline plus a module catalogue keyed to your activities (building software in-house, shipping external software, using AI in operations, and so on), so you sequence only the modules that apply.
  4. Choose your adoption mode. The Adopter guide↗ sets out three adoption modes (fork the whole repository, adopt the corpus only, or adopt the governance pack only) and how the library is meant to be used in each.

Situation two

§02

Running, but undocumented.

Controls exist in practice (backups run, access gets reviewed, incidents get handled) but little is written down, so none of it is provable or repeatable when the person who does it leaves. The work here is baselining, not building.

  1. Establish what applies to you. Work through the applicability decision tree↗ and the Adopter guide↗ to scope which parts of the corpus your organization actually needs.
  2. Map what you already do to the reference set. Use the adopter portal↗ (the audience-keyed navigation front door) and the on-site library to find the reference document for each practice you run informally: that comparison is your gap list.
  3. Score where you stand. The Maturity scorecard↗ turns the baseline into an honest current-state picture you can put in front of leadership.
  4. Close gaps in priority order. Where an obligation drives the priority, the GRC Library Compliance Alignment Matrix↗ shows which documents support which framework requirements, so the gap you close first is the one your obligations care about most.

Situation three

§03

Facing a regulated obligation.

An audit, a regulator, or a certification target with a date on it. Here you work backwards: from the obligation, through the mappings, to the documents that support it, not forwards from whatever is easiest to adopt.

  1. Fix the obligation precisely. Start from the GRC Library Compliance Alignment Matrix↗: find your framework or regulation and read off, domain by domain, which corpus documents align to its requirements.
  2. Check you are citing current editions. The Canonical Citations Register↗ records the current published version of every standard and regulation the library cites, with superseded editions flagged: use it so your programme documents name the edition your auditor will.
  3. Adopt the supporting documents and their jurisdiction overlays. Enter through the compliance area, and pick up the Annex documents: the jurisdiction- and sector-specific overlays that extend the parent documents for your regulatory context.
  4. Prepare the evidence, not just the paper. The mappings support gap analysis and audit preparation; they do not certify compliance. What an assurer tests is whether your controls operate: see the compliance & assurance concern for how the corpus frames evidence.

Situation four

§04

Governing a group of entities.

Subsidiaries, shared services, mixed jurisdictions, and the standing temptation to fork the programme per entity until no two entities can be compared. The aim is one coherent governance model, tailored at entity level.

  1. Choose an adoption topology. The multi-entity adopter guide↗ sets out three adoption topologies for groups, with the trade-offs of each: decide this before any entity adopts anything.
  2. Map roles per entity. Governance authorities differ across a group; the same guide covers role mapping when they do, and the Minimum Viable Governance Structure Guideline↗ supplies the seat names to map onto each entity’s real structure.
  3. Layer jurisdictions instead of forking. Follow the guide’s jurisdictional layering approach: shared core documents, with Annex overlays carrying the jurisdiction-specific obligations per entity.
  4. Keep versioning coherent. The guide defines how the library’s version surface behaves per topology, so entities can prove which corpus version they adopted even when they upgrade at different speeds.
