How it’s built About

Who answers for it

§01

Accountable human stewardship.

The library has a named maintainer of record (Jeff Posluns, CGEIT, CISSP) who approves and answers for every change. The corpus is authored with substantial AI assistance and is curated, reviewed, and edited by the maintainer; nothing is published anonymously and nothing is published without a human decision. Who he is, and the practitioners acknowledged for shaping the corpus, are on the about page.

Machines check, humans decide

§02

What automation checks, and what it never decides.

Every change runs a continuously expanding suite of automated quality gates: link integrity, citation currency, taxonomy consistency, secret and personal-data screening, and structural checks among them. We describe that suite qualitatively, deliberately: it grows continuously, and a hardcoded gate count would understate it and go stale.

The boundary matters more than the count: automation enforces integrity; it does not author judgement. What a control should require, how a risk should be framed, whether a document is ready: those calls are made by the accountable maintainer, never by a gate. The machinery is on How it’s built.

Honesty about the edges

§03

Declared gaps, and the route to correct us.

What is not covered is declared on the page it affects, never hidden behind page counts. The executive layer states it as policy: a concern with no supported pages yet is shown as a declared coverage gap, and coverage is measured by concerns with supported routes. You can see this practice live on the programme-maturity concern, which is currently such a gap.

Found an error, a stale claim, or a gap we have not declared? The correction route is public: open an issue or pull request per CONTRIBUTING.md↗, or report a security concern per SECURITY.md↗. Corrections land through the same reviewed, gated process as every other change.

Cited against the source

§04

Primary-source alignment you can audit.

Every standard, regulation, and framework the library cites is recorded in the Canonical Citations Register↗ with its current published edition, publication date, known superseded versions, and the authoritative upstream page where currency is re-checked. A companion linter parses the register and flags any corpus citation that uses an edition the register records as superseded.

The register is honest about its own limits: each row carries a last-verified date, and a row that has not been re-confirmed against its upstream this cycle says so explicitly rather than implying verification that has not happened. That is the standard the whole library aims for: the claim, its source, and the state of its checking, all inspectable.

Open by design

§05

Openly licensed, plainly limited.

Everything is released under CC BY-SA 4.0: read it, adopt it, adapt it, and use it internally or commercially, with attribution and share-alike. There is no paywall and no gated tier, so what you evaluate is exactly what you get.

The limits, equally plainly: this is organization-neutral reference material, not legal, security, or compliance advice. Reading or adopting it does not certify compliance with any framework or law, and does not by itself show that any control operates in your organization. Adapt it to your own obligations before relying on it.

Current, and provably so

§06

You can tell how fresh it is.

This site renders library version {{CALVER}}. Its generated figures (document counts, area counts, the listings) are recomputed from the corpus on every change, so those figures cannot silently go stale. Documents and executive pages carry their own versions and review records, and the executive pages record when they were last reviewed.
