Make a decision

What should leadership be deciding, and on what evidence?

Ten concerns cover what a governing body is accountable for. Open a concern to see the decision, who owns it, the evidence to request, and one next action.

The ten concerns

Each concern below is an exposure leadership owns whether or not anyone is watching it. The linked briefs and question sets read at source on GitHub today; on-site versions are coming.

Accountability: who owns this decision, and where is that written?

Decide: where each governance authority sits, recorded in charters and decision rules so no decision falls between roles.

Owner: the governing body, delegating through named charters.

Ask for: the charter and the role-authority register that name the owner of the decision in question.

Next: start from the governance area, or read the decision narrative on human review and oversight.

Resilience: could we take the hit and keep operating?

Decide: how much disruption the organization must be able to absorb, and what recovery it funds.

Owner: the governing body sets the appetite; the security and resilience leads run the plans.

Ask for: tested continuity and recovery evidence, not the plan document alone.

Next: start from the resilience area, or read the resilience and continuity brief.

Data and privacy: do we control information from creation to destruction?

Decide: what the organization keeps, where it may move, and when it must be provably destroyed.

Owner: the accountable executive for privacy, reporting to the governing body.

Ask for: the retention rule and the destruction evidence behind any class of record.

Next: start from the privacy area, or read the data-governance brief.

Suppliers and cloud: how exposed are we through the parties we depend on?

Decide: which dependencies are acceptable, and which concentrations need a rehearsed exit.

Owner: the executive who owns third-party risk, with the governing body reviewing concentration.

Ask for: the supplier register, the concentration picture, and the last exit rehearsal.

Next: start from the supply-chain area, or read the supply-chain oversight brief.

Compliance and assurance: can we show, with evidence, that our controls operate?

Decide: which obligations the organization answers to, and what evidence standard it holds itself to.

Owner: the compliance lead assembles the evidence; the governing body reviews the assurance picture.

Ask for: evidence that a control operated on the days it mattered, not the policy that requires it.

Next: start from the compliance area, or read the control-effectiveness outcome map.

Risk acceptance: which risks have we knowingly accepted, and who signed?

Decide: that risk is accepted on purpose: time-bound, owned, and revisited, never by silence.

Owner: the named acceptor of each risk, within the appetite the governing body sets.

Ask for: the exception register, with a name and an expiry date on every accepted risk.

Next: start from the risk area for the methodology and the acceptance procedure behind it.

Architecture debt: what is our accumulated technology debt costing us?

Decide: which deferred modernization the organization carries, and who owns each deferral.

Owner: the technology executive, with deferrals visible to the governing body.

Ask for: the decision records and the register of deferred work, with owners attached.

Next: start from the architecture area, or read the architecture-debt question set.

Workforce and conduct: do the people running the controls have the mandate to run them?

Decide: that responsibility sits with roles that hold the authority, the headcount, and the reporting line to act.

Owner: the executive team, against the operating model the governing body approved.

Ask for: the operating model, the screening and conduct rules, and the reporting routes that back them.

Next: start from the governance area, or read the workforce and conduct brief.

AI and autonomous systems: what is AI doing on our behalf, and who approved it?

Decide: what is deployed, on whose approval, under whose oversight, and with what authority.

Owner: the governing body approves production use; a named executive oversees each system.

Ask for: the AI inventory, the approval record for each production system, and its oversight arrangement.

Next: start from the AI area, or read the AI inventory and risk question set.

Programme maturity: where does our programme stand, and what would better look like?

Decide: the current state, the target state, and who owns the path between them.

Owner: the executive who owns the governance programme, reporting maturity honestly.

Ask for: a scored maturity picture, so investment follows the gaps rather than the last incident.

Next: no executive pages exist for this concern yet, a declared gap. Use the maturity scorecard and the govern-a-programme path.
