# Changelog

All notable changes to this repository are recorded in this file in two forms: while a week is current, one compact entry per change (a `date | version | PR` header followed by a short, plain-language summary a general reader can follow), or a daily roll-up summarizing a day's changes under a date and contiguous PR range; once a week completes, its entries are rolled up into a single weekly summary carrying the week's contiguous PR range, so the root file preserves complete PR coverage while staying scannable as the entry count grows. The full maintainer-grade detail for each change (the Added / Changed / Removed / Fixed / Security / Verification sections) is kept in the operational store and in git history; that mirror is how this project's maintainer tracks the full audit trail. The convention is project-specific; forks may keep their own working-state store and adopt their own approach to detailed change tracking. The mechanics are documented in the [`change-tracking` governance rule](guardrails/governance/change-tracking.md). Significant project milestones are recorded inline as dated `MILESTONE:` entries (`**YYYY-MM-DD | MILESTONE:** ...`) that float to the top of the entry stream, above that day's per-PR entries; those are never condensed by the daily or weekly roll-ups, and the roll-up tooling skips them by their `MILESTONE:` marker.
The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) loosely; individual document versions follow semantic versioning as defined in [`specification-ingestion.md`](specification-ingestion.md). The library as a whole carries a Calendar Versioning (CalVer) version of the form `YYYY.MM.patch`; see [`specification-master-project.md`](specification-master-project.md) section 4.5.

**Week of 2026-05-25 (PRs #1-#2)** This two-entry week marks the start of the library's public life. The Governance, Risk, and Compliance Documentation Library had its first release, under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0) licence, alongside an internal corpus-wide hyperlink sweep and backlog cleanup. The corpus itself is far older: it grew out of practitioner work in the early 2000s and was formalized around 2009 by the original maintainer together with a large group of friends and colleagues, a team of roughly ten regulars who wrote, edited, and peer-reviewed every document by hand. This week also closes a transition many months in the making: the shift from that hand-tended, peer-reviewed corpus to an AI-managed one in which a Human In The Loop (HITL) is required: a person who authorizes, verifies, and signs off on the machine's work.

The team's ambition extends past their own library. As they adopt AI tooling, they want the lessons from that move to help everyone else: the suffering through an AI that, left without guardrails, will lie, invent citations, and misrepresent its own finished work, and the discipline it takes to catch every such error before it reaches the corpus. Every discipline they learn is distilled into a portable [replicable pack of rules](guardrails/README.md), tools, and reference material, so that anyone facing the same task of keeping a governance corpus accurate, citable, and trustworthy can pick it up and build on their experience. They are excited to do this in the open, on GitHub, and expect the beginning to be messy. That is the point: to watch how unruly it starts, then use an ever-growing lattice of guardrails, audit gates, commit hooks, and linters working quietly in the background to make it steadily more standard, more methodical, and finally simple, shifting as much of the quality-assurance burden as possible onto machinery that never tires and never forgets.
