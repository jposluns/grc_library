# Changelog

All notable changes to this repository are recorded in this file as one compact entry per change: a `date | version | PR` header followed by a short, plain-language summary a general reader can follow. The full maintainer-grade detail for each change (the Added / Changed / Removed / Fixed / Security / Verification sections) is kept in [`.working/changelog-details/CHANGELOG-detailed.md`](.working/changelog-details/CHANGELOG-detailed.md) and in git history; that mirror is how this project's maintainer tracks the full audit trail. The convention is project-specific; forks may delete `.working/` and adopt their own approach to detailed change tracking. The mechanics are documented in the [`change-tracking` governance rule](dev-security/claude-rules/governance/change-tracking.md).
The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) loosely; individual document versions follow semantic versioning as defined in [`specification-ingestion.md`](specification-ingestion.md). The library as a whole carries a Calendar Versioning (CalVer) version of the form `YYYY.MM.patch`; see [`specification-master-project.md`](specification-master-project.md) section 4.5.

**Week of 2026-07-06 (PRs #667-#855)**

This was the corpus's heaviest AI-governance week: new AI framework and guideline documents were created (AI value-and-decision governance, synthetic-content provenance, an AI maturity model, an AI data-quality and readiness-validation standard, and a director-facing board-oversight guide), and existing AI documents on model risk, impact assessment, testing, transparency, and security were deepened against held ISO, NIST, OECD, UNESCO, ETSI, and OWASP sources. The EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice (its transparency, copyright, and safety-and-security chapters) was incorporated, and EU AI Act accuracy was sharpened across the European Commission definition and prohibited-practice guidelines, the serious-incident reporting deadlines, the proposal-era Title to enacted-Chapter renumbering, and a corrected penalty figure. Several jurisdiction and sector annexes were added (Canada AI, Australia AI, New York City Local Law 144, Colorado AI, Mexico privacy, a consolidated EU AI Act annex, eIDAS2 for the public sector, and a US HIPAA healthcare annex), alongside detailed Schrems II transfer guidance and a router across overlapping impact assessments. A large citation-accuracy stream ran in parallel, correcting CSA and ISO/IEC 42001 control and clause references, relabelling lapsed or rescinded instruments (Canada's Artificial Intelligence and Data Act and US Executive Order 14110), cascading the World Customs Organization Framework to its 2025 edition, and introducing a new Principle document type that ranks accuracy, integrity, quality, and trust above speed, and speed above cost.

**Week of 2026-06-29 (PRs #452-#666)**

The dominant workstream was a corpus-wide consistency pass: dozens of policy and standard documents were renumbered to the library's uniform section scheme (cross-references updated, no requirement changed), spelling was harmonized to Canadian orthography (about 2000 changes across roughly 330 documents), mandatory shall wording was converted to must, and the document-lifecycle vocabulary was standardized on Superseded. A large citation-accuracy stream relisted jointly issued standards to their ISO/IEC form across 31 files, corrected COBIT and CSA control codes, migrated PCI DSS 4.0 to 4.0.1, updated several standards to current editions, and added version-currency tracking plus new citation-existence audit checks. Cross-document conflicts were reconciled to the stricter or canonical value across retention periods, risk categories and residual-risk scoring, data-subject-rights clocks, committee naming, and breach-notification deadlines (including a fabricated Quebec 72-hour deadline), and a distinct Data Protection Officer role was separated from a composite CIO role across ten documents. New adopter instruments were also added, including a privacy-by-design framework, a sanctions and export-control standard, a mergers-and-acquisitions due-diligence procedure, an AI Ethics Review Panel charter, and several fill-in templates (transfer-impact assessment, legitimate-interest assessment, and a breach-regulator register).

**Week of 2026-06-22 (PRs #187-#451)**

The defining effort was a comprehensive expansion of the compliance alignment matrix, which grew domain by domain (architecture, risk, resilience, operations, compliance, governance, security, dev-security, supply-chain, privacy, and AI) from a curated subset toward the full document set, with many mis-fitting control codes corrected against their source catalogues. A corpus-wide citation and control-code accuracy stream ran alongside it: CSA Cloud Controls Matrix references were fixed (the superseded IVS domain updated, fabricated domains and a hallucinated version 5 removed, versions brought to 4.1), NIST Cybersecurity Framework codes were migrated from version 1.1 to 2.0, and new audit checks were added to validate cited codes. Cross-document conflicts were reconciled to canonical values across risk-scoring scales and treatment vocabularies, retention and log-retention periods, transport security (TLS raised to 1.3 corpus-wide), disaster-recovery backup cadences, maturity-ladder labels, and privacy-lead role naming (Chief Privacy Officer standardized to Data Protection Officer across seventy-three files). Fabricated or inaccurate legal content was corrected, notably Canada's never-enacted CPPA relisted as the in-force PIPEDA across twenty-nine documents and an invented 72-hour PIPEDA breach deadline removed, while new privacy instruments (joint-controller, data-processing-agreement, EU-representative, and China cross-border-transfer material) and substantial internal audit-tooling and AI-assistant governance work were added.

**Week of 2026-06-15 (PRs #38-#186)**

Much of the week was internal process and tooling work: the mechanical corpus-review capability was built up (deterministic pre-checks, principled stop conditions, structured finding output, and a nightly run), the changelog was split into a public summary and a maintainer detailed mirror, and many audit checks and AI-assistant governance rules were added (for example flagging a document body change with no version bump, and requiring a changelog entry per change). On the adopter-facing side, a planned set of templates was added, including a quickstart, a maturity self-assessment, an implementation roadmap, regulator-interaction templates, an audit-evidence package, and a GDPR Article 35 data-protection-impact-assessment template. Corpus content was reconciled and sharpened: the canonical five-level data-classification scheme was propagated into documents that had listed only four, an RFC 2119 requirement-language convention was documented and applied (shall converted to must), the Risk Owner role was defined and added to the role-authority register, and exception handling gained hard duration and renewal ceilings. Other accuracy work raised several retention periods and transport-security floors to their canonical values, stated breach-notification timing as a two-clock model, reconciled the adopter entry points around the portal as the front door, and added AI-agent production-authority controls requiring tested reversibility, auditability, and accountability.
