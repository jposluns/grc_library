## Purpose

This annex gives the adopter a single per-regime view of Illinois's AI-in-employment law, House Bill 3773 (Public Act 103-0804), effective 1 January 2026. Unlike the comprehensive Texas and Colorado state AI statutes, HB 3773 is a targeted amendment to an existing civil-rights statute: it changes two sections of the Illinois Human Rights Act (IHRA), 775 ILCS 5, to make discriminatory employer use of artificial intelligence, the use of zip codes as a proxy for protected classes, and the failure to notify an employee of AI use each a "civil rights violation" under the IHRA employment article. Its nearest corpus sibling in subject is the New York City automated-employment-decision-tool annex (employment decisions), not the Colorado or Texas comprehensive-statute annexes. This annex frames the amendment's provisions and cross-references the operational substance rather than restating it; the controlling text is Public Act 103-0804 as codified in 775 ILCS 5.

## Applicable law and regulatory authority

- **HB 3773, Public Act 103-0804**, amends the Illinois Human Rights Act, 775 ILCS 5, by changing Section 2-101 (definitions) and Section 2-102 (civil rights violations - employment). The held enrolled text has two operative sections: Section 5 (the IHRA amendment) and Section 99 (effective date).
- The amendment operates inside Article 2 of the IHRA: the new AI provisions are added to the enumeration under the heading "Sec. 2-102. Civil rights violations - employment. It is a civil rights violation:", and the Section 2-101 definitions it adds are, by that section's own terms, "applicable strictly in the context of this Article".
- **The rulemaking mandate.** The held text directs "the Department" to adopt any rules necessary for the implementation and enforcement of the AI subdivision (the statutory text is quoted in full under Core obligations); the corpus AI compliance policy identifies that department as the Illinois Department of Human Rights. The identity of "the Department", the enumerated protected classes, and the IHRA charge and adjudication machinery are not established within the two amended sections of the held text; this annex states them only at the level the corpus at [`ai/policy-ai-compliance.md`](../policy-ai-compliance.md) already establishes, and cross-references the canonical register, rather than re-deriving them from the held enrolled text.
- The controlling text is Public Act 103-0804 as codified. Version-sensitive facts (the citation form and effective date) are maintained in [`governance/register-canonical-citations.md`](../../governance/register-canonical-citations.md) (the Illinois HB 3773 row); this annex cross-references them rather than re-deriving them. A signing date is not present in the held text and is not asserted here.

## Transition timeline

- **Effective date:** the Act takes effect 1 January 2026 ("Section 99. Effective date. This Act takes effect January 1, 2026."), corroborated by the ilga.gov public-act footer. This is the same effective date as the Texas TRAIGA annex.
- **Rulemaking:** the Department is directed to "adopt any rules necessary" for implementation and enforcement; the held text sets no deadline for those rules, and none is asserted here. Whether the Department has proposed or adopted implementing rules as of the adopter's reading is an upstream question (the Illinois Register and the Department); the adopter confirms the current rulemaking status before committing to a compliance milestone.

## Scope: covered employers, employees, and employment decisions

The amendment plugs into the existing IHRA Article 2 definitions of employer and employee (which HB 3773 does not itself change) and adds two new AI definitions:

- **Employer (775 ILCS 5/2-101(B)):** the IHRA definition reaches, among others, any person employing one or more employees within Illinois during 20 or more calendar weeks within the calendar year of or preceding the alleged violation, the State and its political subdivisions and governmental units without regard to the number of employees, any party to a public contract, and a joint apprenticeship or training committee. The one-or-more-employee threshold makes coverage broad.
- **Employee (775 ILCS 5/2-101(A)):** the IHRA definition includes any individual performing services for remuneration within Illinois for an employer, an apprentice, and an applicant for any apprenticeship.
- **Covered employment decisions** (from the new subdivision): "recruitment, hiring, promotion, renewal of employment, selection for training or apprenticeship, discharge, discipline, tenure, or the terms, privileges, or conditions of employment".
- **Applicant coverage (stated, not resolved):** the operative prohibition speaks of AI use "that has the effect of subjecting employees to discrimination", while its decision list includes "recruitment, hiring". The IHRA Article 2 "Employee" definition reaches apprenticeship applicants but does not in terms name job applicants generally; the treatment of applicants is a point the Department's rules and enforcement practice may clarify, and the adopter confirms it against those rules rather than relying on this annex to resolve it.
- **Artificial intelligence (775 ILCS 5/2-101(M), new):** the amendment adds a definition: "Artificial intelligence" means a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments. "Artificial intelligence" includes generative artificial intelligence. This is materially the same machine-based-system formulation as the Texas TRAIGA definition.
- **Generative artificial intelligence (775 ILCS 5/2-101(N), new):** the amendment adds a definition: "Generative artificial intelligence" means an automated computing system that, when prompted with human prompts, descriptions, or queries, can produce outputs that simulate human-produced content; the held text enumerates textual, image, and multimedia outputs and other content that would otherwise be produced by human means.

## Core obligations

The amendment adds one new subdivision to the IHRA employment civil-rights-violation enumeration. Its operative content is short and is quoted here in substance:

- **Discriminatory-effect and proxy prohibition (subdivision (L)(1)):** with respect to the covered employment decisions listed above, it is a civil rights violation "for an employer to use artificial intelligence that has the effect of subjecting employees to discrimination on the basis of protected classes under this Article or to use zip codes as a proxy for protected classes under this Article." Two distinct prohibitions sit in this paragraph: AI use with a discriminatory effect, and the use of zip codes as a proxy for protected classes. The protected-class list is the IHRA's own (defined in the IHRA's general definitions, outside the held extract); this annex does not reproduce it and directs the adopter to the IHRA definitions.
- **Notice duty (subdivision (L)(2)):** it is a civil rights violation for "an employer to fail to provide notice to an employee that the employer is using artificial intelligence for the purposes described in paragraph (1)." The held text specifies no notice mechanics itself; the statute expressly delegates to the Department's rules the circumstances and conditions that require notice, the time period for providing notice, and the means for providing notice.
- **Rulemaking mandate (closing paragraph of the subdivision):** the subdivision closes by directing the Department to adopt any rules necessary for its implementation and enforcement, expressly including rules on the circumstances and conditions that require notice, the time period for providing notice, and the means for providing notice.
- **Effects test, contrasted.** The Illinois prohibition turns on AI use that "has the effect of" subjecting employees to discrimination, an effects-based test. This contrasts with the Texas TRAIGA discrimination prohibition, which turns on intent and expressly provides that a disparate impact is not by itself sufficient to demonstrate intent.
- **What the Act does not impose.** HB 3773 imposes no bias-audit duty (contrast the NYC automated-employment-decision-tool law), no impact-assessment or risk-management-programme duty, and no obligations on AI developers (the duty-bearer throughout the new subdivision is "an employer"); it creates no AI-specific penalty schedule and no consumer-facing surface. Its entire operative device is classifying the described employer conduct as an IHRA "civil rights violation", so the notice mechanics arrive through the Department's rules rather than the statute.

## Enforcement and rulemaking

- Because the new AI provisions are IHRA employment civil-rights violations, they ride the IHRA's generally applicable enforcement machinery rather than a bespoke AI enforcement regime. This annex states the enforcement mechanism only at the level the corpus already establishes in [`ai/policy-ai-compliance.md`](../policy-ai-compliance.md) ("enforced by the Illinois Department of Human Rights through the Human Rights Act charge process"); the specific charge process, adjudication route, and available remedies are not present within the two amended sections of the held text, and the adopter consults the IHRA's enforcement articles or the canonical register for those specifics rather than relying on this annex to reproduce them.
- This is a different enforcement shape from the Colorado and Texas comprehensive statutes, where the state Attorney General is the exclusive enforcer and there is no private right of action; the IHRA instead routes these violations through its standard Human Rights Act charge process.
- **Rulemaking is the operative layer for the notice duty.** The three rule topics the statute enumerates are all notice-mechanics ("the circumstances and conditions that require notice, the time period for providing notice, and the means for providing notice"), so an adopter deploying AI in Illinois employment decisions tracks the Illinois Department of Human Rights rules as the compliance-operative layer once they are adopted.

## Relationship to federal anti-discrimination law and the Illinois statutory layer

- **Federal anti-discrimination law.** HB 3773 overlays a state civil-rights prohibition on the same employment-decision surface that federal anti-discrimination law also governs; it does not displace an employer's federal obligations. The federal specifics are outside the held Illinois text and are not asserted here; an adopter treats compliance with HB 3773 as additive to, not a substitute for, its obligations under applicable federal law.
- **Illinois biometric privacy (BIPA).** The Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (740 ILCS 14) governs the collection and handling of biometric identifiers that an employer's AI tooling may process; this annex cross-references the Illinois BIPA coverage in [`privacy/jurisdictions/annex-privacy-united-states.md`](../../privacy/jurisdictions/annex-privacy-united-states.md) rather than duplicating it.
- **Illinois Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act (820 ILCS 42).** Illinois separately regulates AI analysis of applicant video interviews under this Act, which is a genuinely adjacent Illinois employment-AI statute. The reference base does not hold its text, so this annex names it only and describes none of its obligations; an adopter subject to it consults the Act directly.

## Limitations

- This annex is a consolidating per-regime view, not a substitute for the enacted text or for legal advice; the controlling text is Public Act 103-0804 as codified in 775 ILCS 5.
- **Version-sensitivity.** The Illinois Department of Human Rights implementing rules post-date the held enrolled text and carry the notice mechanics (the circumstances and conditions requiring notice, the time period, and the means for providing it); the adopter confirms the current rulemaking status upstream before committing to a compliance milestone. A signing date is not present in the held text.
- **Definitions and enforcement boundary.** The enumerated protected classes, the identification of "the Department", and the IHRA charge, adjudication, and remedy mechanics live in IHRA articles outside the two amended sections; this annex states them only as cross-references to the canonical register and the corpus policy, and does not reproduce them from the held enrolled text.
