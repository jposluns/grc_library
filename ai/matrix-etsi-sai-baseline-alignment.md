## Purpose

This matrix maps the ETSI EN 304 223 V2.1.1 (2025-12) Securing-AI (SAI) baseline, its 13 principles across five secure-lifecycle phases, to the library's AI security carrier documents, and records where coverage is partial or absent. It gives a single auditable surface for baseline coverage and for the gaps and partial-coverage areas that further AI-assurance content would fill. It is an original CC BY-SA 4.0 crosswalk: it references ETSI provision identifiers and the corpus carriers that implement them, without reproducing third-party standard text.

The baseline is normative (EN 304 223 states `shall` and `should` provisions). The ETSI TR 104 128 V1.1.1 (2025-05) implementation guide and the ETSI GR SAI group-report family are informative companions (see the note at the end); a mapping to an informative companion is an orientation, never an obligation, and is marked as such.

## Baseline structure

EN 304 223 organizes its provisions under 13 principles grouped into five secure-lifecycle phases, and names five stakeholder roles (Developer, System Operator, Data Custodian, End-user, Affected Entity). Each provision carries a `shall` (mandatory) or `should` (recommended) modality.

| Phase | Principles |
| --- | --- |
| 5.1 Secure Design | P1 Raise awareness; P2 Design for security; P3 Evaluate the threats and manage the risks; P4 Enable human responsibility |
| 5.2 Secure Development | P5 Identify, track and protect assets; P6 Secure the infrastructure; P7 Secure the supply chain; P8 Document data, models and prompts; P9 Conduct appropriate testing and evaluation |
| 5.3 Secure Deployment | P10 Communicate with End-users and Affected Entities |
| 5.4 Secure Maintenance | P11 Maintain security updates and mitigations; P12 Monitor the system's behaviour |
| 5.5 Secure End of Life | P13 Proper data and model disposal |

## Provision-level coverage (deep map)

This section refines the principle-level map above to the individual EN 304 223 provision (72 separately numbered provisions across clauses 5.1 to 5.5), each rated against the held source text and the corpus carrier. Ratings: **Covered (deep)** (the carrier explicitly and completely carries the provision's actors, qualifiers, sub-elements, and handoffs); **Covered** (the substantive outcome is carried, generally through a composite or less provision-specific control); **Partial** (a substantive carrier exists but at least one material element, actor, condition, or handoff is absent); **Gap** (no substantive carrier in the full `ai/` and `supply-chain/` corpus). A `&dagger;` on a provision id marks a held-source ambiguity discussed in the notes after the table. Carriers are cited at document granularity; the provision-precise carrier lines are maintained in the review record.

### Held-source ambiguities

Ten provisions carry a `&dagger;` because the held (auto-extracted) source text is ambiguous; each is rated on the reading stated here, to be reconfirmed against the authoritative published EN 304 223 before any conformance use:

- **5.1.2-7, 5.1.3-1, 5.1.3-2, 5.2.4-1** each mix a `shall` sentence with a `should` sentence; the mandatory sentence is treated as controlling and the recommended detail is tested separately.
- **5.2.1-4.1** cross-references clause 5.2.2 "for relevant provisions for open source," but 5.2.2 (infrastructure) carries no clearly identified open-source sub-provision; the cross-reference may be incomplete in the extract.
- **5.2.3-3** ("re-run evaluations") does not identify which prior evaluations, the minimum scope, independence, or the acceptance criteria.
- **5.2.5-4.1** ("unintended influence over the system") is undefined; it is read as output-mediated manipulation of downstream behaviour, tools, actions, or configuration.
- **5.4.1-1** ("where possible") leaves the extent of the developer patch duty uncertain, while the operator-delivery sentence is unqualified.
- **5.4.2-3** ("internal states") is read in the narrower sense of model internals or equivalent internal-state telemetry, not ordinary request, response, tool, or workflow logs.
- **5.5.1-1** requires secure disposal of assets whose ownership is being transferred or shared, which is logically unclear; the likely intended duty is secure disposal of relinquished or residual copies.

## Gap register

The provision-level deep map above is the authoritative coverage record; this register summarizes its four genuine gaps and the material partial-coverage residuals, ranked by normative weight (mandatory `shall` first). Coverage is judged at the provision level, so a principle can be covered on its core provisions and still carry a gap or a thin residual on a specific sub-provision.

### Genuine gaps (no corpus carrier)

1. **All-staff AI-threat awareness** (5.1.1-2, P1, `shall`). Generic AI training exists, but no carrier requires all staff to maintain current awareness of AI-security threats, vulnerabilities, and mitigations.
2. **Share the insecure-component justification with End-users** (5.2.3-2.2, P7, `shall`). High-risk-artefact justification, risk acceptance, and compensating controls are carried internally; disclosing that rationale to End-users is not required.
3. **Multi-channel threat-update distribution** (5.1.1-2.1, P1, `should`). No carrier establishes multi-channel distribution of AI-security threat updates.
4. **Developer verification of Data-Custodian-specified controls** (5.1.4-4, P4, `should`). Data and Control Owners are assigned and general security testing runs, but no carrier requires the developer to verify the controls the Data Custodian specified.

### Reclassified from a coarse reading (Partial, not Gap)

The provision-level analysis found a thin or adjacent carrier for several areas an earlier principle-level reading treated as absent; each is Partial in the provision table with the missing element named: the AI vulnerability-disclosure policy (5.2.2-4, a provider process is elicited but the organization's own published policy is not required); provide-and-notify security updates (5.4.1-1, 5.4.1-1.1, supplier patch and recovery contingency without the notify-and-deliver chain); the proactive End-user security-update notice (5.3.1-2.2, advance update notices not scoped to security); the unresolved-threat developer-to-End-user handoff (5.1.3-2, limitations and incident communication without the defined handoff); and model internal-state monitoring (5.4.2-3, representation and latent-state review without ongoing telemetry).

### Material partial-coverage residuals (candidate scope for further content)

The 35 Partial provisions are listed in full in the provision table; the highest-weight residuals include AI-security-specific training content (5.1.1-1, 5.1.1-2.2), AI-tailored disaster-recovery planning (5.2.1-3), developer-side release of model-component hashes (5.2.4-1.2), Data-Custodian involvement in decommissioning (5.5.1-1, 5.5.1-2), re-running evaluations on adopted released models before adoption (5.2.3-3), independent security testers and the findings-transfer step (5.2.5-2.1, 5.2.5-3), and the prompt-and-configuration change-audit log (5.2.4-3).

The library is notably deeper than the baseline on adversarial testing and on access and agent-permission granularity; those are strengths, not gaps.

## Framework alignment notes

- **Normative weight.** EN 304 223 is a European Norm; its `shall` and `should` provisions are citable as a normative anchor. The TR 104 128 guide and the ETSI GR SAI reports are informative; a mapping to them is an orientation, and this matrix marks informative sources as see-also rather than as an obligation.
- **Distributed traceability.** Several carrier documents already cite specific EN 304 223 provisions in their own `Framework alignment` tables (for example [`ai/standard-ai-security-and-risk.md`](standard-ai-security-and-risk.md) and [`ai/standard-ai-access-and-agent-permissions.md`](standard-ai-access-and-agent-permissions.md)). This matrix is the index across those carriers; the per-document tables are the distributed record.
- **Informative companion reports.** The ETSI GR SAI group-report family (Threat Ontology, Data Supply Chain Security, Mitigation Strategy, Role of Hardware, Explicability and Transparency) provides informative threat and mitigation taxonomies. Their themes are substantially reflected in the corpus AI-security content surveyed above; hardware and confidential-computing for AI is the thinnest theme. These reports are informative see-also, not baseline requirements.
