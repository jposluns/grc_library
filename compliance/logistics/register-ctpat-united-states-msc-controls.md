## Purpose

This register documents the organization's implementation status against the CTPAT Minimum Security Criteria, grouped in this register's eight domains: corporate security, physical security, access controls, personnel security, procedural security (cargo handling), business partner security, information technology, and conveyance and cargo unit security. The library holds no complete CBP criteria text (its only held CBP CTPAT source is a cybersecurity checklist job aid, which reflects only the cybersecurity criteria), so each CTPAT Criterion cell is the library's own summary, unverified against CBP wording, and coverage of every criterion CBP applies to the organization's entity type is not confirmed. It complements [`compliance/logistics/register-ctpat-united-states-it-controls.md`](register-ctpat-united-states-it-controls.md), which focuses specifically on IT and cybersecurity criteria.

**Programme authority:** U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)\
**UK parallel programme:** Authorized Economic Operator Security and Safety (AEO-S): see [`compliance/logistics/annex-aeo-united-kingdom-cybersecurity.md`](annex-aeo-united-kingdom-cybersecurity.md)\
**Canada parallel programme:** Partners in Protection (PIP): see [`compliance/logistics/register-pip-canada-controls.md`](register-pip-canada-controls.md)\
**Mutual recognition:** The concluded CTPAT arrangements the WCO AEO Compendium (2020) records include EU-US (2012, with AEO (EU)) and Canada-US (June 2008, with Canada PIP); confirm the facilitation each provides against the arrangement itself. A CTPAT and UK AEO-S arrangement is not confirmed by a held source: the Compendium lists US-United Kingdom only among MRAs being negotiated.

## Programme overview

The WCO AEO Compendium (2020) records CTPAT as a voluntary United States programme open to importers, exporters, customs brokers, warehouse keepers, manufacturers and other stakeholders, including carriers (air, sea, highway and rail) and third party logistics providers, and says it requires partners to update their security profile and risk assessments annually. Confirm the Minimum Security Criteria (MSC) for the organization's entity type against CBP's published criteria. The only held source that dates the criteria is the BASC International Norm (2022), which refers to "the C-TPAT minimum security criteria adopted in 2020". No held source gives CBP's domain structure: the eight domains below are this register's own grouping, and domain 8 and the rows that name a held source are drawn from the WCO SAFE Framework of Standards (2025) and BASC texts, not from CBP wording.

**Status rule:** a Status of Implemented is the organization's own assertion and stands only when the Evidence Location names organization-specific evidence and Last Reviewed carries a date. Not assessed means no implementation is confirmed; record the evidence and the review date before changing it.

*PE-1 and PE-5 in the United States: where screening uses a consumer report for employment purposes, the Fair Credit Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. 1681b(b)(2)(A)) provides that a person may not procure the report, or cause the report to be procured, unless a clear and conspicuous written disclosure, in a document that consists solely of the disclosure, that a consumer report may be obtained for employment purposes, has been made to the consumer beforehand and the consumer has authorized the procurement by that person in writing. The authorization may be made on the same document as the disclosure. The exception in subparagraph (B) applies only to a consumer described in subparagraph (C): one applying for a position over which the Secretary of Transportation has the power to establish qualifications and maximum hours of service under section 31502 of title 49, or a position subject to safety regulation by a State transportation agency, whose only interaction with the person in connection with the application, when the report is procured or caused to be procured, has been by mail, telephone, computer or other similar means. When such a consumer applies for employment by mail, telephone, computer or other similar means, at any time before the report is procured or caused to be procured in connection with that application, subparagraph (B) requires that the person who procures it provide to the consumer, by oral, written or electronic means, notice that a consumer report may be obtained for employment purposes, together with a summary of the consumer's rights under section 1681m(a)(3), and that the consumer have consented, orally, in writing or electronically, to the procurement of the report by that person. In every other case, including an ordinary online application, the stand-alone written disclosure and written authorization in subparagraph (A) apply.*

## UK AEO-S parallel controls

For each of the eight domains in this register, the organization maps a corresponding AEO-S requirement area; that mapping is the organization's own and is to be checked against the UK HMRC AEO security and safety criteria handbook (section 11). A CTPAT and AEO-S mutual recognition arrangement is not confirmed by a held source (the WCO AEO Compendium (2020) lists US-United Kingdom only among MRAs being negotiated). Independently of any arrangement, evidence collected for CTPAT compliance is a candidate overlap for AEO-S, subject to HMRC acceptance requirement by requirement, with the following jurisdiction-specific supplements:

| CTPAT Domain | AEO-S Supplement Required |
|---|---|
| Corporate Security | HMRC AEO application documentation, including a security and safety risk and threat assessment covering all premises relevant to customs-related activities and a security plan (handbook §11.2: failure to produce either during the customs authorities' visit may result in an automatic recommendation that the application be rejected); an appointed contact person responsible for supply chain security (§11.16); UK legal entity compliance (this register's own item, not drawn from §11.1); separately, the handbook's controls and measures are in addition to legal health and safety requirements as well as any specific legal requirements that may be applicable to the business (§11.1) |
| Physical Security | UK site-specific evidence; HMRC criteria reference |
| Access Controls | Supplement not yet assessed: HMRC's administration of keys criterion (handbook §11.10) expects procedures limiting key access to authorized personnel, the place where keys are kept, the person responsible for key security, a record of who takes keys, when, why and their return, and the handling of lost or unreturned keys, and its access to business premises criterion (§11.9) requires a list of all access points, preferably referenced to the site plan, including fire escapes and access stairways, that marks those for cargo loading and unloading, utilities, public counters and drivers' rest areas; confirm the CTPAT access-control evidence covers these before relying on it |
| Personnel Security | Pre-employment screening of staff in sensitive areas that, at minimum, verifies identity, confirms the right to work in the UK, confirms the employee has declared any unspent criminal records and checks previous employment history; a documented employment history of staff covering the previous 5 years, a period HMRC says is not mandatory, although a Regulated Agent or Known Consignor should have a 5-year employment history check; the AEO applicant should have all the employment contracts, including for temporary personnel and outsourcing contracts, available to present to the visiting officer; a business is not required to screen staff it obtains through an employment agency, but should only use agencies that abide by The Conduct of Employment Agencies and Employment Businesses Regulations 2003 (Statutory Instrument 2003 No 3319), and any agency used should be aware of the security and safety policy and, where appropriate, carry out any additional checks the business requires for the sensitive nature of the post; if, exceptionally, temporary staff are employed without screening, there must be measures in place (body searches, supervision or restricted access, for example) to manage the risk (handbook §11.7). Criminal-record data is governed by data protection law: UK GDPR Article 10 and Data Protection Act 2018 section 10(5), which requires a condition in Part 1, 2 or 3 of Schedule 1. The ICO's employment guidance says it is unlikely to be necessary and proportionate to ask all candidates for criminal-record information and that in most cases criminal records checks can take place as part of pre-employment vetting; it also says to do pre-employment vetting only where the employer is under a legal obligation or can identify significant and particular risks, and not to routinely vet all candidates unless legally required to do so |
| Procedural Security | UK customs procedure codes; HMRC authorization references |
| Business Partner Security | Verify partner UK AEO-S status via HMRC; EU AEO for EU-routed shipments |
| Information Technology | Supplement not yet assessed: the HMRC criteria name no incident notification to HMRC (handbook §11.4 expects security procedures to be reviewed and amended after an incident, with evidence of how the changes were communicated to staff and visitors and each amendment recorded as a revision with its date and the parts revised; §11.8, on building and boundary security, asks how security incidents are reported and dealt with and names no recipient, and §11.12, on cargo security, expects procedures for cargo units that say how incidents are reported and recorded and what action is to be taken, including reporting to law enforcement or senior management); UK data protection law compliance |
| Conveyance and Cargo Unit Security | HMRC's cargo security criterion covers cargo units, which include containers, tankers, vans, lorries, vehicles and pipelines: procedures to examine the integrity of the cargo unit before loading, and control measures for checking cargo units, for example, a 7-point inspection, depending on the cargo unit used, that includes the tractor unit; routine maintenance, with an integrity inspection of the cargo unit on its return to the premises when maintenance is done externally or outside the supervision of the organization's staff; and incident procedures (handbook §11.12, on cargo security, expects procedures for cargo units that name the responsible person to whom incidents are reported and say how incidents are reported and recorded and what action is to be taken, including reporting to law enforcement or senior management, and §11.8, on building and boundary security, asks how security incidents are reported and dealt with and names no recipient) |

See [`compliance/logistics/annex-aeo-united-kingdom-cybersecurity.md`](annex-aeo-united-kingdom-cybersecurity.md) and [`compliance/logistics/procedure-aeo-united-kingdom-self-assessment.md`](procedure-aeo-united-kingdom-self-assessment.md) for AEO-S operational procedures.
