## 1. Purpose

This standard sets the requirements for the reserve of assets backing a stable-value crypto-asset, the holder's right of redemption against that reserve, the issuer's own funds, the service provider's prudential safeguards, the liquidity management of the reserve, and the recovery and redemption plans that govern the token's stressed and end-of-life states. It is a requirements document under the Crypto-Asset Governance Framework ([`crypto/framework-crypto-asset-governance.md`](framework-crypto-asset-governance.md)) and supplies the reusable core that the MiCA implementation annex ([`compliance/financial-services/annex-mica-implementation.md`](../compliance/financial-services/annex-mica-implementation.md)) maps to; the annex holds the regime navigation and this standard holds the requirements.

Where a requirement is grounded in the EU Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, MiCA), the provision is named at the point of use. The reserve custody requirements here govern the issuer's reserve assets under MiCA Article 37 and are distinct from the client-asset custody duties of the [Digital Asset Custody Standard](standard-digital-asset-custody.md), which carries the MiCA Articles 70 and 75 obligations of a provider holding clients' crypto-assets; an organization in both positions applies both.

## 2. Applicability

This standard applies at three scopes. First, an issuer of an asset-referenced token (an ART, MiCA Title III): the reserve, redemption, own-funds, liquidity, and plan requirements of sections 3.1 to 3.11 attach, with the significant-token uplifts named at the point of use. Second, an issuer of an e-money token (an EMT, MiCA Title IV), which is necessarily an authorized credit institution or electronic money institution (MiCA Article 48(1)): the parallel obligations of section 3.12 attach, and the ART reserve regime attaches only where MiCA Article 58 brings it in. Third, a crypto-asset service provider: the prudential safeguards of section 3.9 attach.

Two carve-outs bound the scope. A credit institution that issues asset-referenced tokens is not subject to the MiCA own-funds requirement, among other authorization provisions (MiCA Article 17(4) disapplies Articles 16, 18, 20, 21, 24, 35, 41 and 42); its prudential base is its banking regime, and the reserve, redemption, and plan requirements still apply. A financial entity providing crypto-asset services under the MiCA Article 60 notification route is not subject to the Article 67 prudential safeguards (Article 60(10)); its own sectoral prudential regime governs.

An organization outside the European Union applies the same control structure against its own governing law; the quantitative values named below are MiCA-regime values, not universal constants.

## 3. Requirements

### 3.1 Reserve constitution, matching, and valuation

The issuer constitutes and at all times maintains a reserve of assets, composed and managed so that the risks associated with the assets referenced by the token are covered and the liquidity risks associated with the holders' permanent rights of redemption are addressed (MiCA Article 36(1)). The management body ensures the effective and prudent management of the reserve, and issuance and redemption of the token are always matched by a corresponding increase or decrease in the reserve (Article 36(6)). The aggregate value of the reserve, determined by using market prices, is at all times at least equal to the aggregate value of the claims of the holders of the token in circulation (Article 36(7)).

Valuation uses mark-to-market, as defined in Article 2, point (8), of Regulation (EU) 2017/1131, whenever possible; the reserve asset is valued at the more prudent side of the bid and offer unless it can be closed out at mid-market, and only market data of good quality is used (MiCA Article 36(11)). Where mark-to-market is not possible or the market data is not of sufficiently good quality, the reserve asset is valued conservatively by mark-to-model as defined in Article 2, point (9), of Regulation (EU) 2017/1131, and the amortized cost method is not used (MiCA Article 36(12)).

An issuer that offers two or more such tokens operates and maintains a segregated pool of reserve assets for each token, managed separately; where different issuers offer the same token, they operate and maintain one reserve of assets for that token (Article 36(5)).

### 3.2 Legal and operational segregation

The reserve of assets is legally segregated from the issuer's estate and from the reserve of assets of other tokens, in the interests of the holders, so that the issuer's creditors have no recourse to the reserve, in particular in the event of insolvency (MiCA Article 36(2)), and the reserve is operationally segregated from the issuer's estate and from the reserves of other tokens (Article 36(3)). Segregation is evidenced, not asserted: the legal basis (the applicable-law analysis and any trust, escrow, or equivalent construction) and the operational account and wallet structures are documented and retained as evidence.

### 3.3 Reserve policy (the stabilization mechanism)

The issuer maintains a clear and detailed policy describing the token's stabilization mechanism, which in particular: lists the referenced assets and their composition; describes the type and precise allocation of the assets included in the reserve; contains a detailed assessment of the credit, market, concentration, and liquidity risks resulting from the reserve; describes the issuance and redemption procedures and how they produce the corresponding increase and decrease of the reserve; states whether a part of the reserve is invested; where it is, describes the investment policy in detail and assesses how it can affect the reserve's value; and describes the procedure to purchase and redeem the token against the reserve, listing the persons or categories of persons entitled to do so (MiCA Article 36(8)).

### 3.4 Independent reserve audit

The issuer mandates an independent audit of the reserve of assets every six months, assessing compliance with the reserve rules, from the date of its authorization or white-paper approval (MiCA Article 36(9)); for an electronic money institution issuing a significant e-money token, the six-monthly cadence runs from the date of the significance-classification decision (Article 58(1), second subparagraph). The issuer notifies the audit result to the competent authority without delay and at the latest within six weeks of the reference date of the valuation, and publishes the result within two weeks of the date of notification, save where the authority instructs a delay on the grounds MiCA lists (Article 36(10)). The six-monthly cadence, the six-week notification clock, and the two-week publication clock are MiCA-regime values.

### 3.5 Custody of reserve assets

Custody policies, procedures, and contractual arrangements ensure at all times that: the reserve assets are neither encumbered nor pledged as a financial collateral arrangement as defined in Article 2(1), point (a), of Directive 2002/47/EC; the issuer has prompt access to the reserve assets to meet redemption requests; and concentrations of custodians, and concentration of the reserve assets themselves, are avoided (MiCA Article 37(1)). An issuer of two or more tokens maintains a custody policy for each reserve pool (Article 37(2)).

The reserve assets are held in custody no later than five working days after the date of issuance of the token (a MiCA-regime clock), by one or more of: a crypto-asset service provider providing custody and administration of crypto-assets, where the reserve assets take the form of crypto-assets; a credit institution, for all types of reserve assets; or an investment firm providing the safekeeping ancillary service, where the reserve assets take the form of financial instruments (Article 37(3)).

The custodian is a legal person different from the issuer, selected, appointed, and reviewed with all due skill, care, and diligence, with the necessary expertise and market reputation, and the contractual arrangements ensure that the reserve assets held in custody are protected against claims of the custodian's creditors (Article 37(4)); the custody policy sets the selection criteria and the review procedure, and the issuer reviews the appointment regularly, evaluates its exposures to each custodian, and monitors the custodian's financial condition on an ongoing basis (Article 37(5)). Custody is effected per asset type in segregated, identifiable accounts opened in the issuer's name (funds and custodiable financial instruments on the custodian's books; crypto-assets through a register of positions opened in the issuer's name; other assets through verified ownership records), so that the assets held in custody are clearly identified as belonging to each reserve of assets (Article 37(6)). A custodian that loses a financial instrument or crypto-asset held in custody compensates or makes restitution with an identical type or corresponding value without undue delay, unless it proves the loss arose from an external event beyond its reasonable control whose consequences were unavoidable despite all reasonable efforts (Article 37(10)). A reserve custodian is additionally assessed through the [Crypto-Asset Service Provider Vetting Standard](standard-crypto-asset-service-provider-vetting.md) and the supplier-assurance controls; the Article 37 duties above are the regime floor that assessment evidences.

### 3.6 Investment of the reserve

Where a part of the reserve is invested, it is invested only in highly liquid financial instruments with minimal market risk, credit risk, and concentration risk, capable of being liquidated rapidly with minimal adverse price effect (MiCA Article 38(1)); units in a UCITS are deemed such assets under the conditions of Article 38(2). The financial instruments in which the reserve is invested are themselves held in custody in accordance with section 3.5 (Article 38(3)). All profits and losses, including value fluctuations, and any counterparty or operational risks resulting from the investment, are borne by the issuer (Article 38(4)). The technical standard specifying the eligible instruments and the concentration limits is mandated by Article 38(5); the issuer applies the adopted instrument applicable to it, and this standard does not restate its content.

### 3.7 Right of redemption

Holders have a right of redemption at all times against the issuer, and in respect of the reserve assets when the issuer is not able to meet its obligations under the recovery-and-redemption-plan chapter, and the issuer maintains clear and detailed policies and procedures for that permanent right (MiCA Article 39(1)). On a holder's request, the issuer redeems either by paying an amount in funds, other than electronic money, equivalent to the market value of the assets referenced by the token held, or by delivering the referenced assets; the redemption policy sets out the conditions, including thresholds, periods, and timeframes, for exercising the right; the mechanisms and procedures that ensure redemption including in stressed market circumstances and under the recovery or redemption plan; the valuation principles on exercise, including the section 3.1 valuation methodology; the settlement conditions; and the measures managing reserve increases and decreases to avoid adverse market impacts (Article 39(2)). Where the issuer accepted payment in funds other than electronic money denominated in an official currency when selling the token, it always provides the option to redeem in funds denominated in the same official currency (Article 39(2), second subparagraph). Redemption is not subject to a fee, without prejudice to the recovery-plan options of section 3.10 (Article 39(3)).

### 3.8 Own funds of the issuer

The issuer at all times has own funds equal to at least the highest of: EUR 350 000; 2 % of the average amount of the reserve of assets (the average of the end-of-calendar-day reserve amounts over the preceding six months, summed across tokens where more than one is offered); and a quarter of the fixed overheads of the preceding year, reviewed annually and calculated in accordance with the fixed-overheads method of section 3.9 (MiCA Article 35(1)). The own funds consist of the Common Equity Tier 1 items and instruments referred to in Articles 26 to 30 of Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 after the deductions in full pursuant to Article 36 of that Regulation, without the threshold exemptions of its Articles 46 and 48 (MiCA Article 35(2)).

The competent authority may require own funds up to 20 % higher than the 2 % amount on the higher-risk indicators MiCA lists (Article 35(3)), and may require a non-significant issuer to comply with the significant-token obligations where necessary to address such risks (Article 35(4)). The issuer conducts, on a regular basis, stress testing covering severe but plausible financial stress scenarios, such as interest rate shocks, and non-financial stress scenarios, such as operational risk; based on the outcome, the competent authority requires own funds between 20 % and 40 % higher than the 2 % amount in certain circumstances (Article 35(5)). For an issuer of a significant asset-referenced token, the 2 % percentage is set at 3 % of the average amount of the reserve assets (Article 45(5)); where an issuer offers two or more tokens and at least one is significant, the significant-token obligations apply to that issuer (Article 45(6)).

The percentages, the EUR 350 000 floor, and the 20 % to 40 % uplift band are MiCA-regime values. A credit institution issuing the token is not subject to this section's own-funds requirement (Article 17(4)); its banking prudential regime governs, and the remainder of this standard still applies.

### 3.9 Prudential safeguards of the service provider

A crypto-asset service provider at all times has prudential safeguards equal to at least the higher of: the permanent minimum capital requirement of MiCA Annex IV for the class of services provided (EUR 50 000 for class 1, EUR 125 000 for class 2, which adds custody, exchange for funds, or exchange for other crypto-assets, and EUR 150 000 for class 3, which adds operation of a trading platform); and one quarter of the fixed overheads of the preceding year, reviewed annually (MiCA Article 67(1) and Annex IV). A provider not yet in business for one year uses the projected fixed overheads from its authorization application (Article 67(2)). Fixed overheads are calculated from the applicable accounting framework by subtracting from total expenses, after distribution of profits, the fully discretionary and profit-dependent items MiCA lists: profit-dependent staff bonuses and remuneration, employees', directors', and partners' profit shares, other discretionary profit appropriations and variable remuneration, and non-recurring expenses from non-ordinary activities (Article 67(3)).

The safeguards take the form of own funds (Common Equity Tier 1 items and instruments per Articles 26 to 30 of Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 after the deductions in full pursuant to Article 36 of that Regulation, without the threshold exemptions of its Articles 46 and 48), or an insurance policy covering the Union territories where services are provided, or a comparable guarantee, or a combination (Article 67(4)). An insurance policy so used is publicly disclosed on the provider's website, has an initial term of at least one year and a cancellation notice period of at least 90 days, is provided by an authorized third-party insurer (Article 67(5)), and covers the risk classes MiCA lists, including loss of documents, misrepresentation, breaches of legal and conduct obligations, conflicts-of-interest procedure failures, business-disruption and system-failure losses, where applicable gross negligence in safeguarding clients' crypto-assets and funds, and the provider's client liability for attributable custody losses (Article 67(6)). A financial entity providing crypto-asset services under the Article 60 notification route is not subject to these safeguards (Article 60(10)); its sectoral prudential regime governs.

### 3.10 Liquidity management of the reserve

An issuer of a significant asset-referenced token assesses and monitors its liquidity needs to meet redemption requests, and establishes, maintains, and implements a liquidity management policy and procedures that give the reserve assets a resilient liquidity profile enabling normal operation including under liquidity stress (MiCA Article 45(3)); it conducts liquidity stress testing on a regular basis, covering all tokens and services comprehensively where it offers more than one (Article 45(4)). The Level 1 mandates set floor parameters for the technical standards: the reserve-liquidity standard under Article 36(4) carries minimum credit-institution deposits per referenced official currency that cannot be lower than 30 % of the amount referenced in each official currency, and the significant-token standard under Article 45(7) carries a minimum that cannot be lower than 60 %; the adopted instruments applicable to the issuer govern the operative figures.

The minimum contents of the liquidity management policy are specified in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1264, which applies to issuers of significant asset-referenced tokens, electronic money institutions issuing significant e-money tokens, and non-significant issuers where the competent authority so requires (its Article 1). Under it, the policy and procedures: identify, measure, manage, monitor, and internally report liquidity risk over appropriate time horizons, maintain reserve levels adequate to meet redemption at any moment including under stress, are proportionate to the issuer's complexity, risk profile, and scope, are approved by the management body with risk tolerance levels set per token, and monitor liquidity risk on an ongoing basis, including intraday, with overcollateralization assessed where the referenced assets are highly volatile or sit outside the reserve, custodian appointments monitored, and internal limits set against custodian concentration (its Article 2). The issuer calibrates early warning signals for maximum deviations between the market value of the reserve and of the referenced assets and between the market value of the token and of the referenced assets, maintains and reviews liquidity risk mitigation tools including diversified funding access, adjusts strategies and limits from stress-test outcomes, and maintains a liquidity contingency plan with documented responsibilities, shortfall strategies, and escalation tools (its Article 3). The requirements are applied separately for each token, and the policy is separate in content and form from the issuer's other liquidity policies (its Article 4). The stress-testing process documents the risks covered, the parameters and their calibration under stress, the historical data and assumptions used, and the outcomes and remedies, and includes a reverse stress test assessing the limit of resilience of each reserve's liquidity profile (its Article 5).

### 3.11 Recovery plan and redemption plan

The issuer draws up and maintains a recovery plan providing the measures it will take to restore compliance with the reserve requirements where it fails to comply, including the preservation of its token-related services, the timely recovery of operations, and the fulfilment of its obligations on events posing a significant risk of disrupting operations; the plan includes conditions and procedures for timely implementation and a wide range of recovery options, including liquidity fees on redemptions, limits on the amount of the token that can be redeemed on any working day, and suspension of redemptions (MiCA Article 46(1)). The issuer also draws up and maintains an operational redemption plan supporting the orderly redemption of each token, implemented on the competent authority's decision that the issuer is unable or likely to be unable to fulfil its obligations, including on insolvency, resolution, or authorization withdrawal; the plan demonstrates the ability to redeem the outstanding tokens without undue economic harm to holders or to the stability of the reserve-asset markets, includes contractual arrangements, procedures, and systems, including the designation of a temporary administrator in accordance with applicable law, ensures equitable treatment of all holders and timely payment from the proceeds of the sale of the remaining reserve assets, and ensures continuity of the critical activities needed for the orderly redemption (Article 47(1) and (2)).

Each plan is notified to the competent authority within six months of authorization or white-paper approval (for an e-money token issuer, within six months of the offer to the public or admission to trading, Article 55); the authority may require amendments within 40 working days of notification, the issuer implements a required amendment within 40 working days of that decision, and the issuer regularly reviews and updates both plans (Articles 46(2) and 47(3)). Where the issuer fails, or is likely in the near future to fail, to comply with the reserve requirements, the competent authority can require implementation or update of the recovery plan and can temporarily suspend redemption where justified by the holders' interests and financial stability (Article 46(3) and (4)). The plans build on the organization's continuity and recovery discipline ([`resilience/framework-business-continuity-and-resilience.md`](../resilience/framework-business-continuity-and-resilience.md)) but are distinct supervisory artefacts with their own content, notification, and trigger requirements.

### 3.12 E-money token parallels

An e-money token is issued only by an authorized credit institution or electronic money institution (MiCA Article 48(1)), and its holders have a claim against the issuer (Article 49(2)). The issuer issues the token at par value on the receipt of funds, and, on a holder's request, redeems it at any time and at par value, in funds other than electronic money (Article 49(3) and (4)); redemption is not subject to a fee, without prejudice to the recovery-plan options (Article 49(6)). Par-value redemption is the EMT rule; market-value redemption (section 3.7) is the ART rule, and the two are not interchangeable.

Funds received in exchange for e-money tokens and safeguarded under the e-money regime comply with the following: at least 30 % is always deposited in separate accounts in credit institutions, and the remainder is invested in secure, low-risk assets qualifying as highly liquid financial instruments with minimal market, credit, and concentration risk in accordance with section 3.6's eligibility rule, denominated in the same official currency as the one referenced (Article 54). The recovery-and-redemption-plan requirements of section 3.11 apply mutatis mutandis (Article 55). An electronic money institution issuing a significant e-money token is subject to the reserve, custody, and investment requirements of sections 3.1 to 3.6 and the non-prudential significant-token obligations, instead of the e-money safeguarding regime, and to the own-funds composition, supervisory-uplift, and stress-testing provisions of section 3.8 with the 3 % significant-token percentage, instead of the e-money own-funds regime (Article 58(1)); the competent authority may impose those requirements on an electronic money institution issuing a non-significant e-money token where necessary to address the risks those provisions target (Article 58(2)).

## 4. Evidence requirements

Evidence for this standard comprises the reserve composition and daily valuation records and the market-price or model valuation basis; the legal-segregation analysis and the operational account and wallet structures; the reserve policy describing the stabilization mechanism; the six-monthly independent audit reports with their notification and publication records; the reserve custody policy, custodian contracts, due-diligence, review, and exposure-monitoring records; the investment policy and instrument-eligibility evidence; the redemption policy and redemption execution records; the own-funds or prudential-safeguards calculation, its composition evidence, and any insurance policy and its disclosure; the liquidity management policy, the stress-test and reverse-stress-test records, the early-warning-signal calibration, and the liquidity contingency plan; and the recovery plan and redemption plan with their notification, amendment, review, and update records.

## 6. Limitations

This is original library content and reproduces no external control text; MiCA-grounded requirements and values are named at the point of use, and an organization under another regime applies its own governing law. The quantitative values here (the EUR 350 000 floor, the 2 % and 3 % reserve percentages, the 20 % to 40 % uplift band, the Annex IV class minima, the 30 % and 60 % deposit floors, the five-working-day custody clock, the six-monthly audit cadence with its six-week and two-week clocks, and the 40-working-day plan-amendment loop) are MiCA-regime values, not universal constants, and this standard does not compute any organization's capital position or certify its adequacy. The technical standards mandated by MiCA Articles 35(6), 36(4), and 38(5), and the EBA guidelines on recovery and redemption plans, are not restated here; the adopted instruments and guidance applicable to the organization govern their detail. Authorization and white-paper mechanics, the significance-classification procedure, interest prohibitions, and the supervisory architecture remain with the MiCA implementation annex; client-asset custody remains with the [Digital Asset Custody Standard](standard-digital-asset-custody.md). This standard is not legal advice and does not establish compliance.
