## 1. Purpose

This standard states the organization-neutral controls that produce a compliant crypto-asset white paper and govern its content, mandatory statements, notification or approval, publication, modification, and the disclosure-linked rights and liabilities that attach to it. It is the disclosure-governance control layer for the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, MiCA) white-paper regime, covering the three asset classes MiCA distinguishes: crypto-assets other than asset-referenced tokens or e-money tokens (Title II), asset-referenced tokens (ARTs, Title III), and e-money tokens (EMTs, Title IV).

The standard governs the disclosure DOCUMENT and its lifecycle. The operational reserve, prudential, custody, redemption, and recovery controls that an ART or significant-EMT issuer must run are carried by [`crypto/standard-crypto-asset-reserve-and-prudential-requirements.md`](standard-crypto-asset-reserve-and-prudential-requirements.md); the reserve-disclosure items of an ART white paper (MiCA Annex II Part G) are the disclosure counterpart of those operational controls and are cross-referenced at section 3.3. The regulation-to-library navigation map is [`compliance/financial-services/annex-mica-implementation.md`](../compliance/financial-services/annex-mica-implementation.md); this standard is the normative control layer that map points to.

This standard does not reproduce the MiCA white-paper schemas verbatim as a fill-in template; it states the controls that yield a compliant white paper and cites the governing article and annex part at the point of use.

## 2. Applicability

This standard applies to an organization that offers a crypto-asset to the public in the Union or seeks its admission to trading, and to a trading-platform operator that draws up a white paper. It applies across the three classes, with the class determining which content schema and lifecycle path govern:

- Crypto-assets other than ARTs or EMTs (Title II): the notified-not-approved path (sections 3.2, 3.5, 3.7 to 3.10).
- Asset-referenced tokens (Title III): the approved path (sections 3.3, 3.5, 3.7, 3.8, and 3.10), where the white paper is deemed approved on the issuer's authorization (MiCA Article 21(1)), except that an issuer whose offer is exempt from authorization under Article 16(2) notifies its white paper instead.
- E-money tokens (Title IV): the notified-not-approved path with the EMT schema (sections 3.4, 3.5, 3.7, 3.8, and 3.10).

The Title II white-paper obligation does not apply to an offer that meets a MiCA Article 4(2) small-offer exemption (fewer than 150 persons per Member State acting on own account; total consideration of 1 000 000 EUR or less over 12 months; or an offer solely to qualified investors), and the whole Title is disapplied for the Article 4(3) cases (free offers, mining or validation rewards, utility tokens for a good or service that exists or is in operation, and limited-network use); an offer is not free where purchasers provide personal data or other benefits, and a limited-network activity whose consideration exceeds 1 000 000 EUR over 12 months requires a notification to the competent authority. A utility token for a good or service not yet in operation may be offered for no longer than 12 months from white-paper publication (Article 4(6)). A voluntarily drawn-up white paper triggers the full Title (Article 4(8)). For ARTs, the parallel offer and authorization preconditions are in MiCA Articles 16 and 17, with the Article 16(2) small-offer and qualified-investor exemptions.

## 3. Requirements

### 3.1 White-paper obligation and offer or admission preconditions

The organization does not offer a Title II crypto-asset to the public, or seek its admission to trading, unless it is a legal person and has drawn up the white paper (MiCA Article 6), notified it (Article 8), published it (Article 9), drafted and published any marketing communications (Articles 7 and 9), and complies with the offeror conduct duties (Article 14). The same preconditions apply to a person seeking admission to trading (Article 5(1)). Where a platform operator initiates admission with no published white paper, the operator satisfies those preconditions (Article 5(2)); a written agreement may assign them to the operator and states that the applicant provides all necessary information (Article 5(3)). Preconditions (b), (c), and (d) are disapplied where the crypto-asset is already admitted on another Union platform and the paper is drawn up under Article 6, kept updated under Article 12, and the person responsible for drawing up the white paper consents in writing (Article 5(4)).

For an ART, the white paper is drawn up under Article 19 and forms part of the authorization application (Article 18); the issuer is a legal person or other undertaking authorized under Article 21, or a credit institution that complies with Article 17; an issuer whose asset-referenced-token offer is exempt from authorization under Article 16(2) instead draws up the Article 19 white paper and notifies it to the competent authority. Except where a provision states otherwise, the asset-referenced-token requirements in sections 3.5 to 3.8 describe the authorization path; for an Article 16(2)-exempt asset-referenced token the issuer's obligation is limited to drawing up and notifying the Article 19 white paper, and it is not subject to the Article 18 authorization, Article 20 assessment, or Article 25 modification-approval procedures. For an EMT, the issuer is an authorized credit institution or electronic money institution (Article 48(1)), save for the Article 48(4) small-issuer exemption and the Article 48(5) case (a token exempt under Article 1(4) or (5) of Directive 2009/110/EC, for which Article 51 still requires a white paper), and the white paper is drawn up under Article 51.

### 3.2 Content of a white paper for a crypto-asset other than an ART or EMT

The white paper carries the information heads of MiCA Article 6(1), as further specified in Annex I: information about the offeror or person seeking admission (Part A); the issuer where different (Part B, only where the issuer differs); the trading-platform operator where it draws up the paper (Part C, only in that case); the crypto-asset project (Part D); the offer to the public or admission to trading (Part E); the crypto-asset (Part F); the rights and obligations attached (Part G); the underlying technology (Part H); the risks (Part I); and the principal adverse impacts on the climate and other environment-related adverse impacts of the consensus mechanism used to issue the crypto-asset (Article 6(1)(j)). Where a person other than those in Article 6(1)(a) to (c) draws up the paper, it also carries that person's identity and the reason for drawing it up.

Parts A and D to I always apply; Part B applies only where the issuer differs from the offeror; Part C applies only where the platform operator draws up the paper. The Part A and Part D to I item sets include the offeror's legal-entity identifier and its fair and balanced financial condition over the preceding three years, or since registration where it has not existed for three years, the project's past and future milestones and planned use of funds, the offer's reimbursement and refund mechanics, the withdrawal information required by section 3.9, the transferability restrictions and supply protocols, the protection or compensation schemes, and the applicable law and competent court. The organization does not invent template field detail beyond the Annex I item list; the European Securities and Markets Authority implementing and regulatory technical standards for the Title II schema (Article 6(11) and (12)) are consulted where held, and this standard rests on the Level 1 item list where they are not.

### 3.3 Content of an asset-referenced-token white paper

The ART white paper carries the information heads of MiCA Article 19(1), as further specified in Annex II: the issuer (Part A); the ART (Part B); the offer to the public or admission to trading (Part C); the rights and obligations attached (Part D); the underlying technology (Part E); the risks (Part F); and the reserve of assets (Part G), the ART-distinctive head; together with the consensus-mechanism environmental head. Where an offeror or drafter other than the issuer is involved, the paper carries that person's identity and the reason.

Part A carries the issuer's detailed governance-arrangements description and its authorization details and granting competent authority (except an Article 17-exempt issuer). Part D is the rights-heavy schema: it carries the nature and enforceability of holder rights including the permanent redemption right and the claim in insolvency, a detailed description of the claim the ART represents (each referenced asset and its proportion, the relation between referenced-asset value, claim amount and reserve, and a fair and transparent valuation identifying, where relevant, independent parties), the complaints-handling and redress route, the rights under the recovery plan and the redemption plan, and the detailed redemption mechanics including whether the holder may choose the form and currency. Part G discloses the mechanism aligning reserve value with the claim, the reserve composition, the issuance and redemption mechanisms, the investment policy where the reserve is invested, and the custody arrangements including segregation and the named custodians. The operational controls behind Part G, reserve constitution and valuation, segregation, custody, investment, redemption, recovery, and own funds, are carried by [`crypto/standard-crypto-asset-reserve-and-prudential-requirements.md`](standard-crypto-asset-reserve-and-prudential-requirements.md); Part G is the disclosure side of the same reserve, and the two are kept consistent.

### 3.4 Content of an e-money-token white paper

The EMT white paper carries the information heads of MiCA Article 51(1), as further specified in Annex III: the issuer (Part A); the EMT (Part B); the offer to the public or admission to trading (Part C); the rights and obligations attached (Part D); the underlying technology (Part E); the risks (Part F); and the consensus-mechanism environmental head. There is no reserve head: EMT funds are safeguarded under MiCA Article 54 and Directive 2009/110/EC rather than an ART-style reserve; a significant e-money token issued by an electronic money institution is, however, additionally subject to the Article 36 to 38 reserve regime under Article 58, whose operational controls are in the reserve and prudential standard. Part D carries the detailed rights and obligations including the right of redemption at par value and its exercise, the modification conditions, the rights where the issuer cannot fulfil its obligations including in insolvency, the rights under the recovery and redemption plans, the complaints-handling and redress route, and the protection or compensation schemes. Part A carries the issuer's potential conflicts of interest, its financial condition over the preceding three years, or since registration where it has not existed for three years, and its authorization details and granting competent authority (except an Article 48(4) or 48(5) exempt issuer).

### 3.5 Form and mandatory statements

All white-paper information is fair, clear, and not misleading, contains no material omission, and is presented in a concise and comprehensible form (MiCA Articles 6(2), 19(2), 51(2)). For a Title II crypto-asset or an asset-referenced token, the paper makes no assertion as to the crypto-asset's future value beyond the mandated warning statement (Articles 6(4), 19(3)).

**First-page statement.** A Title II white paper carries, on its first page, the verbatim statement: "This crypto-asset white paper has not been approved by any competent authority in any Member State of the European Union. The offeror of the crypto-asset is solely responsible for the content of this crypto-asset white paper." (MiCA Article 6(3)), substituting "person seeking admission to trading" or "operator of the trading platform" for "offeror" as applicable. An EMT white paper carries the same statement with "The issuer" in place of "The offeror" (Article 51(3)). An ART white paper carries no such statement: on the authorization path it is deemed approved on the issuer's authorization (Article 21(1)), while an Article 16(2)-exempt ART white paper is notified rather than approved. Article 19 provides no first-page statement in either case, so importing a "not approved" statement into an ART white paper is incorrect.

**Warning statements.** A Title II white paper carries the six warnings of MiCA Article 6(5) (the asset may lose value in whole or part; may not always be transferable; may not be liquid; a utility token may not be exchangeable against the promised good or service, especially in the case of a failure or discontinuation of the crypto-asset project; is not covered by investor-compensation schemes under Directive 97/9/EC; and is not covered by deposit-guarantee schemes under Directive 2014/49/EU). An ART white paper carries the five warnings of Article 19(4), which omit the utility-token limb. An EMT white paper carries only the two warnings of Article 51(4), the investor-compensation and deposit-guarantee statements, because an EMT is redeemable at par.

**Management-body statement.** The white paper carries a statement from the management body confirming that the paper complies with the relevant Title and that, to the best of the management body's knowledge, the information is fair, clear, and not misleading and the paper makes no omission likely to affect its import; in a Title II or EMT white paper it is inserted after the first-page statement (MiCA Articles 6(6), 19(5), 51(5); the EMT statement adds that the information is complete).

**Summary.** After the management-body statement, the white paper carries a summary in brief and non-technical language that is easily understandable, carrying the four warnings that it is an introduction to be read as such, that a decision is based on the whole paper rather than the summary, that it is not an offer or solicitation of financial instruments, and that it is not a prospectus under Regulation (EU) 2017/1129 (MiCA Articles 6(7), 19(6), 51(6)). An ART summary additionally states that holders have a right of redemption at any time and the conditions for it (Article 19(6)); an EMT summary states that holders have a right of redemption at any time and at par value and the conditions for it (Article 51(6)).

### 3.6 Language, format, and machine-readability

The white paper carries its date of notification and a table of contents; it is drawn up in an official language of the home Member State or a language customary in international finance, and, where the crypto-asset is offered in a host Member State, in an official language of that State or a language customary in international finance; and it is provided in a machine-readable format (MiCA Articles 6(8) to (10), 19(7) to (9), 51(7) to (9)).

### 3.7 Notification, approval, and publication

For a Title II crypto-asset and an EMT, the organization notifies the white paper to the home Member State competent authority at least 20 working days before the date of publication (MiCA Articles 8(5), 51(11)); the competent authority does not require prior approval before publication (Articles 8(3), 51(11)). A Title II notification is accompanied by an explanation of why the asset is not an excluded asset, an EMT, or an ART (Article 8(4)), and a host-Member-State list with the intended start date (Article 8(6)). For an authorization-path ART, the white paper is assessed as part of the authorization procedure (Articles 18 and 20) and is deemed approved on authorization (Article 21(1)); an Article 16(2)-exempt ART issuer instead notifies its white paper to its competent authority (Article 16(2)). Neither follows the Title II notification path of Article 8.

For a Title II crypto-asset, the organization publishes the white paper on a publicly accessible website at a reasonable time in advance of, and in any event before, the starting date of the offer or admission, and keeps it available for as long as the crypto-assets are held by the public (MiCA Article 9(1)); an authorization-path asset-referenced-token issuer publishes the approved white paper, publicly accessible by the offer or admission start and available while the token is held (Article 28), and an e-money-token issuer publishes it before offering or admission (Article 51(13)). For a Title II crypto-asset, the published version is identical to the notified version, or to a version modified under section 3.8 (Article 9(2)).

### 3.8 Modification of a published white paper

Where a significant new factor, material mistake, or material inaccuracy capable of affecting the assessment of the crypto-asset arises during the offer period or while the asset is admitted to trading, the organization modifies the white paper.

For a Title II crypto-asset, it notifies the modified paper, the reasons, and the intended publication date to the home Member State competent authority at least seven working days before publication, informs the public on its website, time-stamps the versions, and keeps each superseded version publicly available for at least 10 years with a prominent statement that the version is no longer valid and a hyperlink to the current version (MiCA Article 12). For an authorization-path ART, the issuer notifies any intended change to its business model likely to significantly influence a purchase decision, including material modifications to governance and risk management, reserve assets and their custody, holders' rights, the issuance or redemption mechanism, validation protocols, proprietary-DLT functioning, liquidity mechanisms, third-party arrangements, complaints-handling, and the money-laundering and terrorist-financing risk assessment, at least 30 working days before the changes take effect; the competent authority grants or refuses approval of the draft modified paper within 30 working days of acknowledgement (MiCA Article 25). For an EMT, the issuer draws up a modified white paper describing the new factor, mistake, or inaccuracy, notifies it to the competent authority, and publishes it on its website (MiCA Article 51(12)).

### 3.9 Right of withdrawal

Where retail holders acquire a Title II crypto-asset directly from an offeror or a placing crypto-asset service provider, they have 14 calendar days within which to withdraw from the agreement to purchase, without incurring any fee or cost and without giving reasons, the period beginning from the date of the agreement (MiCA Article 13(1)). All payments are reimbursed no later than 14 days after the holder informs the organization of the withdrawal, by the same means of payment unless the holder expressly agrees otherwise and incurs no fee or cost (Article 13(2)). The white paper carries the withdrawal information (Article 13(3)). The right does not apply where the crypto-assets were admitted to trading before the purchase (Article 13(4)), so it is a right of the offer-to-public path rather than the admission-to-trading path, and it is not exercisable after a time-limited offer's subscription period ends (Article 13(5)).

### 3.10 Liability for the white paper

The offeror, the person seeking admission to trading, the operator that draws up the paper, or the issuer, together with the members of its administrative, management, or supervisory body, are liable to a holder for loss caused by an infringement of the applicable content article through information that is not complete, fair, or clear or that is misleading (MiCA Articles 15, 26, 52). A contractual clause excluding or limiting that civil liability is deprived of legal effect (Articles 15(2), 26(2), 52(2)). The holder bears the burden of showing the infringement and that it affected the decision to acquire (Articles 15(4), 26(3), 52(3)). Liability does not attach on the basis of the summary alone unless the summary is misleading, inaccurate, or inconsistent when read with the rest of the paper, or omits key information (Articles 15(5), 26(4), 52(4)). These liabilities are without prejudice to further civil liability under national law.

## 4. Evidence requirements

Evidence for this standard comprises the drawn-up white paper for each class offered, carrying the correct content schema and mandatory statements for its class; the notification record and competent-authority acknowledgement, or, for an ART, the authorization decision that deems the paper approved (or, for an Article 16(2)-exempt ART, the notification record); the publication record showing the website, the timing relative to the offer or admission start, and version identity; the modification records including notifications, time-stamps, and the retained superseded versions with their validity warnings and current-version hyperlinks; the withdrawal information carried in the paper and the reimbursement records for exercised withdrawals; and the management-body statement and the record of the persons liable for the paper's content.

## 5. Limitations

This standard states organization-neutral disclosure-governance controls grounded in MiCA Level 1 (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114) and its Annexes I, II, and III; it is not legal advice, and an adopter confirms the current consolidated text and any amending or delegated acts against the authoritative source before relying on a specific article. The European Securities and Markets Authority implementing and regulatory technical standards specifying the white-paper forms, formats, and templates and the sustainability-indicator methodology (MiCA Articles 6(11) and (12), 19(10) and (11), 51(10) and (15)) are consulted where held; this standard rests on the Level 1 item lists and does not reproduce a fill-in white-paper template. This standard governs the disclosure document and its lifecycle; the operational reserve, prudential, custody, and service-provider controls are carried by the related crypto-asset standards.
