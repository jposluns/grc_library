## Why this matters

Crypto-asset and blockchain activity concentrates several governance exposures at once: assets that can be irreversibly lost if keys are mishandled, stable-value tokens whose holders rely on a backing the organization must actually hold, disclosures that carry legal liability, and third-party providers and smart-contract code the organization does not fully control. The governing body's oversight question is not whether the organization uses crypto-assets, but whether it can require, and see in evidence, that each activity was classified and approved before it began, that client and reserve assets are safeguarded, that holders are properly disclosed to, that providers and code dependencies are vetted, and that all of it is inventoried and kept current. Each of those expectations maps to a specific control in the crypto corpus that an executive can ask to see.

## What the corpus establishes

- The [crypto-asset governance framework](../../crypto/framework-crypto-asset-governance.md) is the **dependency** of the rest of the domain: it sets classification-before-activity and an activity-approval gate as the precondition for the other controls, requiring a crypto-asset to be classified and the activity approved before it is issued, offered, custodied, traded, or otherwise used.
- The [digital-asset custody standard](../../crypto/standard-digital-asset-custody.md) makes a **contribution** to safekeeping: segregation of client from proprietary assets, wallet tiering, split-knowledge and dual-control key custody, and a register of positions.
- The [reserve and prudential standard](../../crypto/standard-crypto-asset-reserve-and-prudential-requirements.md) makes a **contribution** to holder protection where the organization issues an asset-referenced token, or a significant e-money token under Article 58: reserve constitution and coverage of the claim, legal and operational segregation, the redemption right, and the recovery and redemption plans.
- The [white-paper content and disclosure standard](../../crypto/standard-crypto-asset-white-paper-disclosure.md) makes a **contribution** to holder disclosure: it governs the white-paper document across the asset classes and the disclosure-linked rights and liabilities.
- The [service-provider vetting standard](../../crypto/standard-crypto-asset-service-provider-vetting.md) is a **prevention** control against engaging an unauthorized or unfit provider: its legal-identity-and-authorization gate is a mandatory stop where a required authorization is missing or an identity does not match.
- The [crypto-asset inventory register](../../crypto/register-crypto-asset-inventory.md) is the **evidence** for the classification, approval, and inventory controls: the authoritative record of assets held, wallets and custody, platforms, and deployed contracts.

Two further controls round out the domain: the [smart-contract and oracle risk standard](../../crypto/standard-smart-contract-and-oracle-risk.md) is a **prevention** control for smart-contract deployment and oracle risk, and the [regulatory-submission standard](../../crypto/standard-crypto-asset-regulatory-submission.md) makes a **contribution** to the organization's authorization and ongoing regulatory submissions. For an organization in scope of the EU regime, the [MiCA implementation annex](../../compliance/financial-services/annex-mica-implementation.md) makes a **contribution** to the applicability determination, as a navigation aid rather than a required layer.

## What this means for the organization

- **Require classification and approval before activity, not after.** The corpus makes classification the first control; oversight that accepts "we will classify it later" stops before the corpus starts.
- **Expect evidence, not assurances.** The inventory register, the reserve audit result, the register of positions, and the provider authorization record are evidence classes; a control the organization cannot evidence is not demonstrably operating.
- **Third-party custody does not remove the organization's controls.** Engaging a third-party custodian does not lift the provider-vetting gate or the custody standard's requirements; both still apply.
- **Asset-referenced tokens carry a distinct prudential burden.** For an asset-referenced token, or a significant e-money token under Article 58, reserve backing, redemption, and the recovery and redemption plans are a separate control set from client-asset custody; an organization in both positions runs both.
- **Regime status is fact-dependent and the organization's own.** The classification and which obligations attach are the organization's determination; the annex is navigation, not a legal conclusion.

## Evidence to request

- The crypto-asset inventory, recording each asset's classification and activity-approval status and date.
- The register of positions and the custody-arrangement evidence, showing client-from-proprietary segregation and key-custody controls.
- For any asset-referenced token issued (or significant e-money token under Article 58), the reserve composition and the independent reserve-audit result, and the redemption and recovery/redemption-plan evidence.
- The white-paper record, where one is required for the asset, and its notification, approval, or publication evidence.
- The provider authorization and vetting record for each engaged crypto-asset service provider, and the smart-contract deployment and change-governance record for each deployed contract.
