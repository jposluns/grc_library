## Why this matters

When an organization builds AI into its services, the model service, the retrieval store, and the model provider become operational dependencies of the business, alongside the applications and suppliers the organization already plans around. The [continuity and disaster recovery policy](../../resilience/policy-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md) places AI systems that support critical operations expressly inside its scope, so a disruption to such an AI dependency is assessed for continuity impact rather than treated as a novelty outside the plan.

The corpus does not treat AI resilience as a separate programme. It runs AI through the organization's existing continuity discipline (impact analysis, recovery objectives, backup, testing) and adds AI-specific controls where AI fails differently: a model can degrade rather than stop, a provider can retire a model version, and the safest containment can be to switch a capability off. The governing body's role is not to operate any of this; it is to require that AI dependencies are inside the continuity programme and to receive the evidence that they are. That single-programme reading is synthesized across the sources below; see the limitations.

## What the corpus establishes

- **AI continuity is a policy requirement.** The [continuity and disaster recovery policy](../../resilience/policy-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md) requires that AI system continuity address matters including model service availability, retrieval store availability, data restoration, emergency disablement, fallback processes, monitoring records, and supplier-operated AI dependencies (its section 3). It likewise requires supplier and cloud dependencies to be assessed for matters including continuity commitments, concentration risk, and exit feasibility.
- **Proportionate controls and documented recovery objectives.** The [continuity and disaster recovery standard](../../resilience/standard-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md) requires AI systems to have continuity controls proportionate to risk (its section 2.8), requires documented recovery objectives for each critical service (its section 2.2), and requires dependency maps to include AI systems and retrieval stores (its section 2.3). The specific objective values, schedules, and service tiers are set by the adopting organization; the standard's own limitations say so, and this brief does not restate any value.
- **AI dependencies enter through the impact analysis.** The [business impact analysis procedure](../../resilience/procedure-business-impact-analysis.md) requires the assessment to consider AI matters including model service availability, supplier dependency, emergency disablement, and the fallback decision process where the assessed process depends on AI (its "AI and data considerations"), and it defines the recovery objectives at its Step 5. The analysis is a dependency of the downstream continuity decisions: it is where the organization learns which services stop when an AI dependency fails. That reading is composite; see the limitations.
- **AI artefacts are inside backup and recovery.** The [backup and recovery procedure](../../resilience/procedure-backup-and-recovery.md) places AI prompts, outputs, embeddings, retrieval stores, model configuration, evaluation data, and monitoring records inside documented backup scope where applicable (its requirement 1), and requires AI system recovery to consider retrieval stores, model configuration, tool permissions, and data lineage (its requirement 6).
- **Plans are tested, and AI has a named test type.** The [continuity and disaster recovery standard](../../resilience/standard-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md) requires testing at defined intervals and after material changes (its section 2.7), and the [continuity and recovery testing procedure](../../resilience/procedure-continuity-and-recovery-testing.md) defines an AI resilience test that validates AI service fallback, retrieval store recovery, emergency disablement, monitoring, and data restoration (its test types). The [resilience framework](../../resilience/framework-business-continuity-and-resilience.md) names AI and data resilience among its core components and lists, among its recommended metrics, a coverage metric for AI systems with a documented fallback or emergency disablement method (its metrics).
- **AI failure has a defined response path.** The [AI incident response plan](../../ai/plan-ai-incident-response.md) defines the AI incident classes, which include model degradation and supplier model incidents, the containment actions available (including disabling a specific capability or suspending a model version), and a staged recovery. Its severity structure and escalation bodies are defined in the plan itself; this brief routes to them. The [cross-domain coordination procedure](../../resilience/procedure-cross-domain-incident-coordination.md) defines a hand-off into the AI stream where a security incident implicates an AI system, and a hand-off into the resilience stream from any domain on crisis activation (its hand-off checklists).
- **Supplier dependence is assessed, managed, and exitable.** The [AI security and risk standard](../../ai/standard-ai-security-and-risk.md) requires external AI services to be assessed for matters including service continuity, incident notification, deletion and export capability, and exit support (its section 4.9). The [foundation model lifecycle procedure](../../ai/procedure-foundation-model-lifecycle.md) manages provider version changes through a defined transition window and escalates an involuntary deprecation as supplier and concentration risk, to the escalation body it names (its Step 6). The [concentration risk register](../../supply-chain/register-concentration-risk.md) tracks single-provider reliance for AI features as a concentration finding with defined treatment options (its service-class dimension). The [cloud exit and data portability standard](../../supply-chain/standard-cloud-exit-and-data-portability.md) sets contract provisions that every new cloud and SaaS contract must carry before signature, scopes a further exit-assistance provision to the higher service tiers it names, and requires a documented exit plan for cloud services hosting assets in those tiers (its sections 4 and 5).
- **The governing body's instrument is defined.** The [board oversight guide](../../ai/guide-ai-board-oversight.md) establishes that the governing body sets appetite and policy direction while management operates within it (its section 4), provides the question set for structured interrogation (its section 5), and describes what the governing body should expect to receive on a regular cadence, including significant AI incidents and their resolution (its section 7).

## What this means for the organization

For the governing body and accountable executive leadership, the corpus position resolves into what to require of management, and one answer pattern to watch for. This reading is composite; see the limitations.

- **Require inclusion, not a parallel programme.** The corpus treats AI as one more class of critical dependency inside the existing continuity discipline. The question for management is therefore not "do we have an AI continuity plan" but "do our impact analyses, dependency maps, recovery objectives, backups, and tests cover our AI dependencies". Coverage in each of those artefacts is a dependency of the outcome the governing body wants: services that degrade in a controlled way when an AI dependency fails.
- **Require the fallback and disablement pair.** The corpus repeatedly names two AI-specific capabilities: a fallback process, so the service continues without the AI, and emergency disablement, so the organization can switch a capability off deliberately. Both appear in the [policy](../../resilience/policy-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md), the [impact analysis procedure](../../resilience/procedure-business-impact-analysis.md), and the [testing procedure](../../resilience/procedure-continuity-and-recovery-testing.md). A documented, tested fallback is a prevention against an AI outage becoming a business outage; disablement without a fallback is only half the pair.
- **Require supplier failure to be planned, not assumed away.** Provider outage, provider model retirement, and provider concentration are failure modes the corpus plans for through assessment ([AI security and risk standard](../../ai/standard-ai-security-and-risk.md), its section 4.9), lifecycle management ([foundation model lifecycle procedure](../../ai/procedure-foundation-model-lifecycle.md)), the [concentration risk register](../../supply-chain/register-concentration-risk.md), and exit planning ([cloud exit standard](../../supply-chain/standard-cloud-exit-and-data-portability.md)). "Our provider is resilient" is a weak answer: a supplier attestation is evidence for the supplier's own controls, and it is not a continuity plan for the organization's use of the supplier.
- **Some answers are weak answers.** "We can turn it off" without a fallback answers half the question. "The plan exists" without a test record answers none of it: a test record is the evidence of whether the plan met its success criteria, including where it did not and what corrective action followed, and the [testing procedure](../../resilience/procedure-continuity-and-recovery-testing.md) defines what that record contains. And a recovery objective that has not been reviewed against the impact analysis is a number, not a decision; the [impact analysis procedure](../../resilience/procedure-business-impact-analysis.md) requires the recovery objectives to carry an accountable approval at its Step 7.

## Evidence to request

Each item below is an evidence class the governing body can call for from management, and each is evidence for a named control or outcome. The [board oversight guide](../../ai/guide-ai-board-oversight.md) frames the questioning (its section 5); this list frames what a strong answer contains.

- **Impact analysis records for AI-dependent critical services**, as evidence that the AI considerations in the [business impact analysis procedure](../../resilience/procedure-business-impact-analysis.md) were applied and that recovery objectives were approved at its Step 7.
- **Dependency-map entries covering AI systems and retrieval stores, and recovery-objective register entries for the critical services those dependencies support**, as evidence for the mapping and objective requirements of the [continuity and disaster recovery standard](../../resilience/standard-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md) (its sections 2.2 and 2.3); the standard's minimum evidence set (its section 3) names the artefacts.
- **AI resilience test results with corrective actions**, as evidence of whether fallback, disablement, and recovery met their success criteria, per the test type and required record in the [testing procedure](../../resilience/procedure-continuity-and-recovery-testing.md), and the fallback-or-disablement coverage metric in the [resilience framework](../../resilience/framework-business-continuity-and-resilience.md).
- **The backup inventory covering applicable AI artefacts, and restoration logs for the restoration activities performed**, as evidence for the AI backup scope and recovery requirements in the [backup and recovery procedure](../../resilience/procedure-backup-and-recovery.md).
- **The AI incident response linkage**, as evidence that model degradation and supplier model incidents are covered incident classes with containment and staged recovery paths, per the [AI incident response plan](../../ai/plan-ai-incident-response.md), and that the hand-offs the [cross-domain coordination procedure](../../resilience/procedure-cross-domain-incident-coordination.md) defines, into the AI stream from security and into the resilience stream on crisis activation, are in place.
- **Supplier assessments for external AI services covering service continuity and exit support**, as evidence for the supplier controls in the [AI security and risk standard](../../ai/standard-ai-security-and-risk.md) (its section 4.9).
- **The documented rollback plan and version-transition records for foundation models, plus, where a rollback occurred, the records of it**, as evidence that provider changes are managed per the [foundation model lifecycle procedure](../../ai/procedure-foundation-model-lifecycle.md) (its Step 6 and its rollback-plan requirement).
- **Any concentration findings concerning AI model providers, evidence that the concentration assessment was performed, and the exit plans for the cloud services in the tiers the standard names**, as evidence that single-provider reliance is assessed and treatable, per the [concentration risk register](../../supply-chain/register-concentration-risk.md) and the [cloud exit and data portability standard](../../supply-chain/standard-cloud-exit-and-data-portability.md).

## Limitations

- This page creates no compliance and imposes no requirement. The linked corpus documents govern; where this page differs from a corpus document, the corpus document prevails.
- This page carries composite claims. The single-programme reading (that the corpus governs AI resilience through the general continuity discipline plus AI-specific controls), the "three things to require" framing, the reading that the impact analysis is a dependency of the downstream continuity decisions, and the fallback-and-disablement pairing are synthesized across several corpus sources, and the adopting organization validates that reading against its own structure.
- The corpus documents this page points to carry the specific recovery objective definitions, test cadences, service tiers, severity classes, contract periods, escalation bodies, and approval authorities. This page routes to them and does not reproduce them, so the corpus remains the single source for every value.
- This page makes no claim about how likely any disruption is, and none of its statements should be read as a probability or a frequency.
