## Outcome statement

The corpus states the shape of resilience assurance in one sentence: its [business continuity and resilience framework](../../resilience/framework-business-continuity-and-resilience.md) connects business impact, service dependency, recovery objectives, supplier exposure, technology recovery, communication, test evidence, corrective action, and governance reporting (its Purpose section). This page maps the corpus documents along that connected path to a single outcome:

**Leadership can state, with evidence, whether the organization's critical services could continue and recover within their approved objectives after a disruption, and can see where that readiness rests on untested assumptions.**

The first half of the outcome concerns the recovery capability itself. The second half concerns the coverage of the proof, which the corpus records deliberately: the [resilience metrics and testing log register](../../resilience/register-resilience-metrics-and-testing-log.md) records the coverage of critical services with current plans and approved recovery objectives and the count of recovery tests meeting their applicable objective (its Section 2, as counts or percentages), and the framework's recommended metrics include the share of critical services tested within the required cadence (its Metrics section). The assembly of the corpus documents below into one path, and the reading order, are a composite synthesis across the sources this page lists; the limitations say so.

## Contribution map

Each row relates one corpus control to the outcome using exactly one of the narrative layer's four relationship words. A **contribution** materially advances the outcome without being sufficient for it; a **dependency** is a condition the outcome requires; a **prevention** reduces the opportunity for a named failure mode; **evidence** demonstrates that a control exists or operates. No stronger relationship word appears on this page, by design. Where a row's justification is the cited document's own stated purpose, the row paraphrases it closely; the assignment of exactly one word per row is a composite reading, and the limitations carry that caveat.

|---|---|---|
| Business impact analysis, per the [BIA procedure](../../resilience/procedure-business-impact-analysis.md) | dependency | The procedure's stated purpose is to identify critical processes, dependencies, impact tolerances, recovery priorities, and the evidence required to support continuity and disaster recovery planning. Its step 5 defines each assessed service's recovery objectives and its step 7 has accountable roles review and approve them; use its Minimum BIA fields section for the record's required fields. Without this layer, the outcome has no definition of which services are critical or what recovery they require. |
| The standing requirements, per the [business continuity and disaster recovery policy](../../resilience/policy-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md) and the [business continuity and disaster recovery standard](../../resilience/standard-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md) | contribution | The policy requires critical services to be identified through business impact analysis, recovery objectives to be defined, and continuity and recovery plans to be maintained proportionate to impact (its section 3); the standard sets the minimum requirements for continuity, recovery, backup, dependency mapping, testing, validation, and evidence capture, and its section 3 names the minimum evidence set an adopting organization maintains. This requirement layer materially advances the outcome by mandating the analysis, objectives, and plans that the layers below produce; it structures the work without being, by itself, the knowledge the outcome states. |
| Continuity and recovery plans, per the [business continuity and crisis management plan](../../resilience/plan-business-continuity-and-crisis-management.md) and the [IT disaster recovery plan](../../resilience/plan-it-disaster-recovery.md) | contribution | The continuity plan establishes activation criteria, command structure, communication flow, recovery coordination, evidence expectations, and return-to-normal decision points (its Purpose section). The disaster recovery plan defines the system-specific recovery procedures, recovery time and recovery point targets, and runbook references for IT recovery execution (its Purpose section). Documented, approved plans materially advance the outcome without being sufficient for it: a plan is a stated capability, and the testing layer below is what shows whether it operates. |
| Test execution and its record, per the [continuity and recovery testing procedure](../../resilience/procedure-continuity-and-recovery-testing.md) | evidence | The procedure's stated purpose is a reusable method for planning, executing, documenting, evaluating, and improving continuity and recovery tests. Its step 2 defines measurable success criteria, including recovery time and recovery point, and its required test record pairs the tested objectives with the actual recovery time and actual recovery point achieved; use its Test types and Required test record sections for the exercise forms and the record fields. A completed test record demonstrates that the recovery capability was exercised and states what it actually achieved. |
| The testing log and coverage metrics, per the [resilience metrics and testing log register](../../resilience/register-resilience-metrics-and-testing-log.md) | contribution | The register records each test with its recovery target and its recovery actual (its Section 1), tracks coverage metrics such as critical services with current continuity plans, services with approved recovery objectives, and recovery tests meeting their applicable objective (its Section 2), and logs corrective actions to closure (its Section 3). These aggregate coverage metrics materially advance the second half of the outcome by making the untested remainder visible as counts or percentages (critical services with current plans, with approved recovery objectives, and recovery tests meeting their objective); the register records these aggregates, not a per-service cadence tracker, and the share-of-services-tested-within-cadence metric is the framework's Metrics section, not the register's. |
| Lessons learned and corrective action, per the [lessons-learned template](../../resilience/template-lessons-learned.md) | contribution | The template's stated purpose names the lessons-learned report as the durable record that drives systemic improvement after a significant event (a real incident, a tabletop exercise, a recovery test among them) and states that it feeds the corrective action register; the [standard](../../resilience/standard-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md) requires failed tests and missed recovery objectives to be recorded, assigned, remediated, and validated (its section 2.9). The contribution is that what a test finds changes the plans and objectives, rather than resting in the report that found it. |
| Reporting to oversight, per the [board risk report template](../../risk/template-board-risk-report.md) | evidence | The template carries a dedicated resilience and continuity section whose elements are the recovery posture, exercise results, status against severe-but-plausible scenario testing, and the material gaps that warrant board attention (its Section 10); the [framework](../../resilience/framework-business-continuity-and-resilience.md)'s resilience objectives include reporting resilience posture to accountable governance roles (its Resilience objectives section). The completed report, with its gaps stated, is evidence that the reporting control operates. |

## Gaps and dependencies

- The corpus states the path's couplings document by document: the [testing procedure](../../resilience/procedure-continuity-and-recovery-testing.md)'s step 8 updates continuity plans, recovery plans, BIA outputs, risk records, and recovery objectives from test results; the [register](../../resilience/register-resilience-metrics-and-testing-log.md)'s use requirements link testing evidence to business impact analysis, recovery objectives, supplier assessments, AI system continuity records, and risk registers; and the [board risk report template](../../risk/template-board-risk-report.md) carries exercise results into oversight (its Section 10). The reading of the seven rows as one ordered path is this page's synthesis, not any single document's statement; the limitations flag it as composite.
- The corpus separates a documented plan from proof that it operates: the [policy](../../resilience/policy-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md) requires recovery tests to record actual outcomes, failed assumptions, corrective actions, residual risk, and business acceptance (its section 3), and the test record pairs targets with actuals (the [testing procedure](../../resilience/procedure-continuity-and-recovery-testing.md)'s Required test record section; the [register](../../resilience/register-resilience-metrics-and-testing-log.md)'s Section 1).
- The corpus deliberately does not define an organization's critical services or recovery tolerances: the [framework](../../resilience/framework-business-continuity-and-resilience.md)'s Limitations state that an adopting organization completes its own business impact analysis, validates technical recovery capability, tests assumptions, and formally accepts residual risk. The [IT disaster recovery plan](../../resilience/plan-it-disaster-recovery.md) states that the infrastructure it names is an illustrative reference deployment, not a description of any particular organization's environment (its Purpose section).
- Supplier, cloud, and AI dependencies enter the path as assessed dependencies: the [policy](../../resilience/policy-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md) requires supplier and cloud dependencies to be assessed for continuity commitments, recovery capability, and exit feasibility, and AI system continuity to address model availability, fallback, and emergency disablement (its section 3). The supply-chain and AI corpus documents that operationalize those assessments sit outside this map's scope; the framework's Related Documents route to them.

## Evidence to request

Each item below is an evidence class leadership can ask management for, for a sampled critical service. Each is evidence for a named layer of the map operating.

- **The current business impact analysis and approved recovery objectives for the sampled service**, with the approving role, as evidence for the analysis layer in the [BIA procedure](../../resilience/procedure-business-impact-analysis.md).
- **The continuity plan and the recovery plan covering the sampled service**, with their activation criteria and restoration sequence, as evidence for the planning layer in the [business continuity and crisis management plan](../../resilience/plan-business-continuity-and-crisis-management.md) and the [IT disaster recovery plan](../../resilience/plan-it-disaster-recovery.md).
- **The sampled service's most recent test record**, with its recovery target, actual recovery time, actual recovery point, and whether the success criteria were met, as evidence for the testing layer in the [continuity and recovery testing procedure](../../resilience/procedure-continuity-and-recovery-testing.md).
- **The period's resilience metrics**, including critical services with a current business impact analysis, services tested within the required cadence, tests meeting their recovery objectives, and open corrective actions, as evidence for the coverage layer in the [resilience metrics and testing log register](../../resilience/register-resilience-metrics-and-testing-log.md) and the [framework](../../resilience/framework-business-continuity-and-resilience.md).
- **The lessons-learned report from the most recent significant event or exercise**, with the corrective actions it fed and their closure status, as evidence for the improvement layer in the [lessons-learned template](../../resilience/template-lessons-learned.md).
- **The resilience and continuity section of the latest board risk report**, with its material gaps, as evidence for the reporting layer in the [board risk report template](../../risk/template-board-risk-report.md).
- **Any approved exception to a continuity or recovery requirement touching the sampled service**, risk assessed, time bound, and tracked to closure per the [policy](../../resilience/policy-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md)'s section 3, as evidence for the accepted-deviation route in that policy.

## Limitations

- This page creates no compliance and imposes no requirement. The linked corpus documents govern; where this page differs from a corpus document, the corpus document prevails.
- This page carries composite claims. The assembly of the listed corpus documents into a single path, the reading order, the two-part outcome framing, and the assignment of exactly one relationship word per row are synthesized across the sources named above; a control can serve the outcome in more than one way, and the map records the primary relationship. The adopting organization validates that reading against its own resilience model.
- The specific values live with the adopting organization, inside the corpus structures this page links: recovery time and recovery point objectives, maximum tolerable disruption, test cadences, and metric thresholds are the organization's own, recorded in the fields the [standard](../../resilience/standard-business-continuity-and-disaster-recovery.md), the [BIA procedure](../../resilience/procedure-business-impact-analysis.md), and the [register](../../resilience/register-resilience-metrics-and-testing-log.md) define. This page routes to those documents and reproduces no value.
- Adoption is not presented as effectiveness. Holding the plans and templates this page links, or requesting the artefacts it names, is not evidence that any service can recover in an adopting organization; the [framework](../../resilience/framework-business-continuity-and-resilience.md)'s own Limitations require validated technical recovery capability, tested assumptions, and formally accepted residual risk, and only the organization's own test records speak to what recovery actually achieved.
- The role names on this page (resilience owner, service owner, governing body, accountable executive leadership) are the corpus's reference vocabulary; an adopting organization maps them onto its own structure.
- This page makes no claim about the likelihood, occurrence frequency, or magnitude of a disruption, a recovery failure, or an outcome, and none of its statements should be read as a probability; the scenario types and testing cadences it points to are the cited sources' own, routed and not reproduced.
