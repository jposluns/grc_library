## Scenario premise

This scenario is a composite illustration. It describes no identifiable organization, no actual service, and no real legal change, and it makes no claim about how likely such an event is. It also names no law, jurisdiction, regulator, deadline, or transfer mechanism. The legal change at its centre is deliberately generic, because the corpus routes it walks are the same whichever regime moves.

An organization runs a customer-data service on a platform operated in one region, serving customers in several others. One of the cross-border data flows behind that service is recorded in the organization's transfer register and has operated without incident for years. Then the conditions governing that flow change. Legal review concludes that the flow cannot continue under its current arrangement, and the service that depends on it must change too: where its data sits, how the data moves, or who operates it. The [scenario risk catalogue](../../risk/register-scenario-risk-catalogue.md) anticipates this class of event as a reference scenario, member `SCN-REG-002` in its Regulatory section; the surrounding regulatory scenario set stays in the catalogue.

Leadership now holds three questions at once: which flows and services are affected, what operating arrangement remains lawful, and how the redesign reaches production without breaking the service or the evidence trail. The first question is a records question before it is a legal one. The organization either holds a current register of its cross-border flows to answer from, or it starts the redesign by reconstructing its own data map under pressure.

## How the event unfolds

**Applicability is resolved.** The change enters through the organization's watch, not through a customer or a regulator. Section 5 of the [legal and regulatory compliance policy](../../compliance/policy-legal-and-regulatory-compliance.md), Regulatory horizon scanning and change management, requires continuous monitoring of regulatory updates, and it routes every logged update onward through the organization's change process; the monitoring sources and routing detail stay in that section. Horizon scanning operating as the policy requires is a contribution to the organization meeting the change on its own schedule rather than on an enforcement schedule. What the change means for this organization is then resolved as a recorded assessment, not a hallway conclusion: the [global regulatory applicability register](../../compliance/register-global-regulatory-applicability.md) provides the organization-neutral structure for that assessment. Its Purpose section states that applicability depends on the organization's own facts, its Applicability assessment fields section defines the record (the Limitation field appears among its members), and its Maintenance rules section governs how the record is kept, including its rule on recording uncertainty explicitly. The full field set and the rules stay in the register. The scenario does not interpret law, and neither does the register: a recorded applicability basis, with its uncertainty visible, is a prevention against redesigning the service around an unexamined legal assumption.

**The affected flow is identified.** The organization's answer to which flows are affected resolves against the [cross-border data flow register](../../privacy/register-cross-border-data-flow.md). Its Transfer record schema section defines what a transfer record holds (its Geography group appears among the schema's members), and its Connection to related registers section links the register to the subprocessor and applicability registers around it; the schema's fields stay in the register. A current flow register is a dependency of answering the impact question from records rather than from memory. Where the picture is incomplete, Step 2 of the [privacy impact and cross-border transfer procedure](../../privacy/procedure-privacy-impact-and-cross-border-transfer.md), Scoping and data mapping, owns the work of establishing it, including data flows that cross borders; the scoping detail stays in the step.

**The permitted transfer route is reassessed.** Whether any arrangement lets the flow continue, and under what controls, is owned by Step 4 of the same [procedure](../../privacy/procedure-privacy-impact-and-cross-border-transfer.md), Control selection and mitigation. That step carries the transfer-legality logic for both the originating and the destination side of a flow, and it routes jurisdiction-specific requirements through the [privacy jurisdiction index](../../privacy/annex-privacy-jurisdiction-index.md), whose Jurisdiction files section directs the reader to the applicable annex. The transfer mechanisms, sequences, tests, and jurisdiction content stay in the step and the annexes, and this page reproduces none of them. The outcome of this stage is narrower than a legal opinion: it is the set of operating arrangements under which the service could lawfully run, each with its control cost attached.

**Leadership chooses an operating path.** The organization now faces an architecture decision, not a compliance task: relocate where the data is processed, restructure the service so the restricted flow is no longer needed, or change who operates the platform. Each path is a material change, and each goes through the [architecture review procedure](../../architecture/procedure-architecture-review.md). Its Step 1, Identify a review-worthy change, names regulatory implications among the triggers in its Material risk row, so this change is review-worthy on its face; Step 4 provides the review forum, and Step 5 defines the reviewer dispositions a proposal can receive (Conditional endorsement appears among them). The decision itself is captured under Step 6, Recording the decision, as an architecture decision record; Step 7, Implementation oversight, keeps the build tied to what was decided, including routing drift back through the procedure. The trigger table, disposition set, and record elements stay in the procedure. The architecture decision record is evidence of what was decided, on which legal and operational assumptions, and under which conditions.

**The approved change is governed.** The chosen path becomes a production change under the [change management and configuration control procedure](../../operations/procedure-change-management-and-configuration-control.md). Its section 1, Change classification, requires every change to be classified before submission, with the change types, approval authorities, and review requirements held in its table; section 2, Change lifecycle, owns the path from request to post-implementation review. Section 6, Rollback planning, requires a documented rollback plan for a CAB-reviewed change, and the plan's required contents, testing expectations, and the compensating-control pathway stay in that section: a viable rollback plan is a dependency of undoing a data migration by design rather than by improvisation. Where the chosen path means leaving a hosting platform, the [cloud exit and data portability standard](../../supply-chain/standard-cloud-exit-and-data-portability.md) already governs the ground: its section 5, Cloud exit plan requirements, establishes which services carry a maintained exit plan (scoped by the asset tiers the standard names) and what the plan documents, and its section 7, Exit triggers, owns the boundary for activating one. Nothing in this scenario makes exit mandatory; whether the path leaves a platform is the architecture decision's outcome, and the standard governs the leaving when that path is taken.

**Evidence and obligations are reconciled.** After cutover, the records catch up to the new arrangement. Section 4 of the [legal and regulatory compliance policy](../../compliance/policy-legal-and-regulatory-compliance.md), Compliance management system, requires a centralized obligations register with an assigned owner for each obligation, and its section 7, Control design and evidence management, requires each obligation mapped to documented and tested controls with evidence retained; the mapping and retention detail stays in the policy. The [applicability register's](../../compliance/register-global-regulatory-applicability.md) Maintenance rules keep the applicability record honest as facts change, and the [cross-border data flow register's](../../privacy/register-cross-border-data-flow.md) Register governance section owns the update obligations for the flow records themselves (review following material regulatory change appears among them); frequencies and responsibilities stay in its table. The closed change record, the updated flow record, and the updated obligation mapping are, together, evidence that the redesign ended in a governed state rather than merely a shipped one.

## Where the corpus controls engage

- **Regulatory applicability.** Horizon scanning and obligation ownership in the [legal and regulatory compliance policy](../../compliance/policy-legal-and-regulatory-compliance.md) (its sections 4 and 5) are contributions to the change being seen early and owned by a named person. The recorded assessment structure in the [global regulatory applicability register](../../compliance/register-global-regulatory-applicability.md) (its Applicability assessment fields and Maintenance rules sections) is a prevention against acting on an unwritten legal assumption.
- **Data-flow truth and transfer assessment.** The [cross-border data flow register](../../privacy/register-cross-border-data-flow.md) (its Transfer record schema and Connection to related registers sections) is a dependency of knowing which flows the change touches. Steps 2 and 4 of the [privacy impact and cross-border transfer procedure](../../privacy/procedure-privacy-impact-and-cross-border-transfer.md), with the [privacy jurisdiction index](../../privacy/annex-privacy-jurisdiction-index.md) routing to the applicable annex, own the mapping of the flow and the selection of a permitted route.
- **Architecture decision.** The [architecture review procedure](../../architecture/procedure-architecture-review.md) (its Steps 1, 4, 5, 6, and 7) owns the review, decision, record, and oversight of the redesign; the resulting architecture decision record is evidence of the decision and its conditions.
- **Production change.** The [change management and configuration control procedure](../../operations/procedure-change-management-and-configuration-control.md) (its sections 1, 2, 6, and 7) owns classification, lifecycle, rollback planning, and the evidence trail; the tested rollback plan is a dependency of a reversible migration.
- **Supplier exit, where the path requires it.** The [cloud exit and data portability standard](../../supply-chain/standard-cloud-exit-and-data-portability.md) (its sections 5 and 7) establishes the exit plan and its activation boundary; a maintained exit plan is a contribution to the migration option being real when the decision needs it.

## What good looks like

The following reading is a composite of the sources above; see the limitations.

In a well-prepared organization, the redesign starts from records that already exist. The affected flow is traceable in the transfer register before anyone asks, and the register is kept in step with the subprocessor and applicability registers around it. The applicability assessment is written down, with its uncertainty recorded rather than smoothed over, and the obligation it surfaces has a named owner in the obligations register. The architecture decision identifies the legal and operational assumptions it rests on, so that when either moves, the organization knows the decision needs revisiting. The production change carries an approved record, a rollback plan that would actually work, and a post-implementation review; if the path left a platform, the exit ran along a plan that existed before the pressure did. None of these registers or records was created for this event. The event only reads from them, and that is the point.

Depicting these controls here is not evidence that they operate in any particular organization. The evidence classes below are how a governing body tests whether they do.

Each item below is an evidence class the governing body can call for from management, and each is evidence for a named control or outcome.

- **The applicability record for the changed regime, including its recorded uncertainty**, as evidence for the assessment structure and maintenance rules in the [global regulatory applicability register](../../compliance/register-global-regulatory-applicability.md) (its Applicability assessment fields and Maintenance rules sections).
- **The legal interpretation record distinguishing obligation from practice for the affected flow**, as evidence for the obligation-classification structure in the same register (its Legal obligation versus practice section).
- **The transfer register extract for the affected flow, and the register's connections to the related subprocessor and applicability registers**, as evidence for the flow-register control in the [cross-border data flow register](../../privacy/register-cross-border-data-flow.md) (its Transfer record schema and Connection to related registers sections).
- **The transfer assessment for the proposed new arrangement**, as evidence for the control-selection step in the [privacy impact and cross-border transfer procedure](../../privacy/procedure-privacy-impact-and-cross-border-transfer.md) (its Step 4).
- **The architecture decision record for the chosen operating path**, as evidence for the decision-recording control in the [architecture review procedure](../../architecture/procedure-architecture-review.md) (its Step 6).
- **The approved change record for the migration**, as evidence for the change lifecycle and records controls in the [change management and configuration control procedure](../../operations/procedure-change-management-and-configuration-control.md) (its sections 2 and 7).
- **The rollback plan for the migration and, where exercised, the rollback evidence**, as evidence for the rollback-planning control in the same procedure (its section 6).
- **Where the chosen path left a platform: the exit plan for the affected service**, as evidence for the exit-planning control in the [cloud exit and data portability standard](../../supply-chain/standard-cloud-exit-and-data-portability.md) (its section 5).
- **The post-implementation review record and the updated obligation and flow-register entries**, as evidence for the post-change validation step in the change procedure (its section 2), the obligation-to-control mapping in the [legal and regulatory compliance policy](../../compliance/policy-legal-and-regulatory-compliance.md) (its sections 4 and 7), and the register governance obligations in the [cross-border data flow register](../../privacy/register-cross-border-data-flow.md) (its Register governance section).
