## Purpose

This procedure defines the channels, protections, investigation processes, and reporting obligations for whistleblower disclosures and internal incident reporting. It ensures that concerns about misconduct, legal violations, ethical breaches, security incidents, or regulatory non-compliance are reported safely, investigated thoroughly, and resolved appropriately.

---

## Scope

Applies to all employees, contractors, temporary workers, and third parties who access organizational systems or resources and who have concerns about:
- Ethical violations or misconduct.
- Legal or regulatory breaches.
- Safety or security incidents.
- Financial irregularities or fraud.
- Bribery or corruption.
- Data protection violations.
- AI ethics concerns.

## Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **Chief Compliance Officer** | Owns whistleblower programme; receives and manages disclosures. |
| **Ethics and Compliance Committee (ECC)** | Reviews whistleblower reports; approves investigation outcomes. |
| **HR / People Operations** | Manages employment-related disciplinary outcomes. |
| **Legal Counsel** | Advises on legal obligations; for a personal data breach, determines the notification obligations by jurisdiction and approves notification content, while the Data Protection Officer decides and submits the personal data breach notifications; owns every other regulatory notification, unless the applicable domain procedure assigns the notification elsewhere (section 5). |
| **CISO** | Investigates security-related disclosures. |
| **Internal Audit** | Independently reviews whistleblower programme effectiveness annually. |

## 1. Reporting channels

Disclosures may be submitted through any of the following channels:

| Channel | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Direct Manager** | Appropriate for routine concerns without conflict of interest |
| **HR / People Operations** | Appropriate for employment, conduct, or HR-related concerns |
| **Chief Compliance Officer** | Appropriate for compliance, legal, or ethical concerns |
| **Anonymous Reporting Channel** | Available for sensitive disclosures; managed by the ECC |
| **External Regulator** | Available where the reporter believes internal channels are inadequate or compromised |

Anonymous disclosures are accepted and investigated to the extent possible given available information. Reporters are encouraged to provide sufficient detail to enable investigation.

---

## 2. Whistleblower protections

The organization prohibits any retaliation against individuals who make a disclosure in good faith. Protections include:

- Employment protection: no dismissal, demotion, suspension, or unfavourable treatment.
- Confidentiality: reporter identity is protected to the maximum extent possible.
- Data protection: the organization protects the personal data of all parties involved in a report and uses them solely for the purpose of appropriate internal procedures relating to the report and as required by law, or as may be required for subsequent judicial proceedings (informed by Council of Europe Recommendation CM/Rec(2015)5, principle 17.1).
- Support: reporters may request access to support services during the investigation period.

Retaliation against a whistleblower is itself a disciplinary offence subject to the Security Disciplinary Process.

Good faith is defined as a genuine belief that the reported concern may be true. Malicious or knowingly false reports are not protected.

---

## 3. Incident reporting

All staff must report actual or suspected:
- Information security incidents (data breaches, malware, phishing, unauthorized access).
- Privacy breaches or data loss events.
- Physical security incidents.
- AI ethics or safety concerns.
- Health and safety incidents.

Reports are made to the relevant team lead, IT/security helpdesk, or the CISO as appropriate for the incident type (points of contact per ISO/IEC 27035-1:2023 §4.5.3 b)). Security incident reporting follows the Security Incident Reporting and Escalation Procedure.

## 4. Investigation process

4.1 The Chief Compliance Officer acknowledges receipt of a disclosure within 5 business days.

4.2 A preliminary assessment determines:
- Whether the disclosure is within scope.
- Whether it requires immediate action (e.g., imminent legal risk, active breach).
- Whether it requires investigation by Internal Audit, Legal, or an external party.

4.3 An investigation is conducted proportionate to the severity of the concern:
- Minor concerns: resolved through conversation or management action.
- Significant concerns: formal investigation with documented findings.
- Criminal or regulatory matters: referred to Legal Counsel for guidance on external reporting.

4.4 The reporter is provided feedback within a reasonable timeframe not exceeding three months from the acknowledgment of receipt (or, where no acknowledgment was sent, three months from the expiry of the seven-day period after the report was made), and is informed of the outcome to the extent permitted by confidentiality and legal constraints. The three-month internal-channel ceiling follows the EU Whistleblowing Directive (Directive (EU) 2019/1937) Article 9(1)(f); an adopter operating external reporting channels applies the Directive's Article 11 timeframe, which permits up to six months in duly justified cases.

4.5 Investigations are documented and retained per the Data Retention Schedule.

## 5. Regulatory notification obligations

Where an investigation identifies, or the organization otherwise becomes aware of, a breach that may trigger regulatory notification obligations, Legal Counsel promptly assesses the obligations below (unless a row states otherwise, the statutory clock starts at awareness, not at confirmation; see the GDPR row below). For a personal data breach, the Data Protection Officer decides and submits the personal data breach notifications, and Legal Counsel determines the notification obligations by jurisdiction and approves notification content ([`privacy/procedure-data-protection-and-privacy-breach-response.md`](../privacy/procedure-data-protection-and-privacy-breach-response.md) §2.1). Legal Counsel owns every other regulatory notification, unless the applicable domain procedure assigns the notification elsewhere.

| Regulation | Notification Obligation |
| --- | --- |
| GDPR | Notify the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms (GDPR Art. 33(1)) |
| PIPEDA / Quebec Law 25 | Notify the OPC, and each affected individual unless otherwise prohibited by law, where it is reasonable in the circumstances to believe the breach creates a real risk of significant harm to that individual, as soon as feasible after the organization determines that the breach has occurred (PIPEDA s. 10.1); where the incident presents a risk of serious injury, promptly notify the CAI and notify any person whose personal information is concerned by the incident, except that such a person need not be notified so long as doing so could hamper an investigation conducted by a person or body responsible by law for the prevention, detection or repression of crime or statutory offences; keep a register of confidentiality incidents and send a copy to the CAI at its request (CQLR c. P-39.1, ss. 3.5 and 3.8, as amended by Law 25; consolidated text updated to 1 April 2026) |
| UK GDPR | Notify the Information Commission (to which the functions of the Information Commissioner transferred on 30 September 2026: Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 ss. 118 and 119, as enacted; S.I. 2026/1015, made 10 September 2026, reg. 2(b) and (c)) without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms; where the notification is not made within 72 hours, it is accompanied by reasons for the delay (UK GDPR Art. 33(1)); a processor notifies the controller without undue delay after becoming aware of a personal data breach (UK GDPR Art. 33(2); consolidated text retrieved 29 September 2026) |
| LGPD | Notify ANPD and affected data subjects within 3 business days of becoming aware that the incident affected personal data, where it may cause risk or relevant harm (Resolution CD/ANPD No. 15/2024, Arts. 6 and 9) |
| EU AI Act | A provider of a high-risk AI system placed on the Union market reports each serious incident (Article 3, point (49)) to the market surveillance authorities of the Member States where it occurred, within the Article 73(2) to (4) deadlines (Article 73(1)); by way of derogation from Article 73, a provider of a high-risk AI system subject to the AI Office's competence under Article 75(1) reports any serious incident to the AI Office, with Article 73(2) to (9) applying mutatis mutandis and the AI Office promptly transmitting the relevant information to the market surveillance authority of the Member State in whose territory the provider or its legal representative is situated (Article 75(1a), inserted by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026, Article 1, point (31)(c)). A deployer of a high-risk AI system that identifies a serious incident immediately informs first the provider, and then the importer or distributor and the relevant market surveillance authorities, and, if it cannot reach the provider, follows the Article 73 procedure mutatis mutandis and, as reasoning, applying Article 73(1) mutatis mutandis (Article 26(5)), reports, subject to the separate-entity case at the end of this row, to the market surveillance authorities of the Member States where the incident occurred; the duty does not cover sensitive operational data of deployers that are law enforcement authorities (Article 26(5)); whether this also limits a fallback report made under Article 73 is not settled by the held text, and Legal confirms it. For a high-risk AI system that is a safety component of a device, or is itself a device, covered by Regulations (EU) 2017/745 and (EU) 2017/746, the notification on the Article 73(1) route and, because Article 26(5) applies Article 73 mutatis mutandis without limiting it to particular paragraphs, on the deployer's fallback (not on the Article 75(1a) route, which applies only Article 73(2) to (9)) is limited to Article 3, point (49)(c) incidents and is made to the national competent authority chosen for that purpose by the Member States where the incident occurred (Article 73(10)); for a high-risk AI system referred to in Annex III that is placed on the market or put into service by a provider subject to Union legislative instruments laying down reporting obligations equivalent to those set out in the EU AI Act, the notification on the Article 73(1) route, on the Article 75(1a) route (because it applies Article 73(2) to (9) mutatis mutandis) and, for the same reason as for Article 73(10), on the deployer's fallback, where the condition concerns the provider, is limited to Article 3, point (49)(c) incidents and goes to the recipient of the route concerned, since Article 73(9) names none (Article 73(9)); for a deployer that is a separate entity in the provider's undertaking of a system within the AI Office's Article 75(1) competence, the held texts do not state the recipient of a fallback report, and Legal confirms it |
| NIS 2 Directive | Entities subject to Article 23 must report significant incidents to the CSIRT or, where applicable, the competent authority in stages (Article 23(4)), timed from becoming aware: early warning without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours of becoming aware of the significant incident, incident notification with an initial assessment without undue delay and in any event within 72 hours of becoming aware of the significant incident, except that a trust service provider must notify without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours of becoming aware of significant incidents that have an impact on the provision of its trust services (Article 23(4), second subparagraph), an intermediate report on request, final report within one month of that notification; where the incident is still ongoing at that point, a progress report then and the final report within one month of handling the incident (Article 23(4)(e)) |
| DORA | A financial entity reports major ICT-related incidents to the relevant competent authority (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 of 14 December 2022, Article 19(1)). The default time limits are: the initial notification as early as possible, but in any case within four hours from the classification of the ICT-related incident as a major ICT-related incident and no later than 24 hours from the moment it has become aware of the ICT-related incident; the intermediate report at the latest within 72 hours from the submission of the initial notification, even where the status or the handling of the incident have not changed, with an updated intermediate report without undue delay and in any case when the regular activities have been recovered; and the final report no later than one month after either the submission of the intermediate report or, where applicable, the latest updated intermediate report (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/301 of 23 October 2024, Article 5(1)). The remaining time-limit rules of Article 5 apply: a financial entity unable to submit the initial notification, intermediate report, or final report within the time limits set out in paragraph 1 of Article 5 informs the competent authority without undue delay, but no later than the respective time limit, and explains the reasons for the delay (Article 5(3)); and where a time limit falls on a weekend day or a bank holiday in its Member State, it may submit by noon of the next working day, except where Article 5(5) or (6) excludes this (Article 5(4) to (6)) |

## 6. Programme effectiveness review

The Chief Compliance Officer reviews the whistleblower programme annually and reports to the ERC on:
- Number and category of disclosures received.
- Investigation outcomes.
- Programme awareness levels.
- Any identified programme improvements.
