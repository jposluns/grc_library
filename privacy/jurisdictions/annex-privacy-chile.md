## Purpose

This annex defines the privacy regulatory requirements applicable to the processing of personal data (tratamiento de datos de carácter personal) in Chile under Ley 19.628 sobre protección de la vida privada. It supplements the Privacy and Data Governance Policy and the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure. Ley 19.628 governs processing in registros or bancos de datos by both public bodies and private parties, excluding processing done in the exercise of the freedoms of opinion and information [Art. 1].

IMPORTANT currency note for adopters: Chile has enacted **Ley 21.719**, a new comprehensive personal-data-protection law (published 2024-12-13, with vigencia deferred to 2026-12-01) that, once in force, establishes a dedicated data-protection authority (Agencia de Protección de Datos Personales), a modern lawful-basis and cross-border-transfer regime, breach-notification duties, and an administrative sanctions framework. **This annex is built on Ley 19.628, the law in force until Ley 21.719 fully takes effect; the consolidated Ley 19.628 text carries an end-of-validity date of 30 November 2026.** Ley 21.719 is enacted and held; this annex covers Ley 19.628, the law in force until the transition, and an organization processing Chilean personal data at or after 1 December 2026 applies Ley 21.719 and confirms its specific provisions against the primary source.

## Applicable law and regulatory authority

- **Ley 19.628 sobre protección de la vida privada**, published in the Diario Oficial on 28 August 1999, in the consolidated version in force from 9 May 2023 (last modification Ley 21.504, D.O. 10 November 2022). It governs the processing of personal data in registros or bancos de datos by organismos públicos and by particulares [Art. 1].
- **No dedicated data-protection authority exists under Ley 19.628.** Data-subject rights are enforced through the ordinary civil courts: where a controller does not respond to a request within two business days, or denies it for a cause other than national security or national interest, the data subject applies to the juez de letras en lo civil of the controller's domicile [Art. 16]. Civil liability for improper processing is pursued in the same courts [Art. 23]. (The dedicated authority is a Ley 21.719 feature; see the currency note above.)
- **There is no general registration obligation for private controllers.** The only statutory registry is the public-sector registry of data banks held by organismos públicos, maintained by the Servicio de Registro Civil e Identificación [Art. 22]; it does not apply to private-party data banks.
- Public-sector processing (Título IV) is largely out of scope for this private-party annex, which addresses processing by particulares under Ley 19.628.

The controlling text is Ley 19.628 itself; where this annex states an obligation, the article reference points to the enacted text, and an adopter confirms the current in-force version against the primary source (leychile.cl) before relying on a specific value, given the pending transition to Ley 21.719.

## Principles, lawful basis, and authorization

Ley 19.628 does not consolidate its principles into a single article (unlike some regimes); the principles are dispersed across the operative provisions:

- **Lawful basis and consent** [Art. 4]: processing may occur only where the law (or another legal provision) authorizes it, or where the data subject **expressly consents**. The authorization must be given by a person duly informed of the purpose of storage and its possible communication to the public, **must be in writing**, and may be revoked in writing (without retroactive effect). Article 4 sets out exceptions, including data from publicly accessible sources of an economic, financial, banking, or commercial character, category listings, direct-marketing communications, and processing by private legal persons for their own or their associates' exclusive statistical/pricing use.
- **Purpose limitation and data quality** [Art. 9]: personal data must be used only for the purposes for which it was collected (unless drawn from publicly accessible sources), and the information must be accurate, up to date, and truthful to the data subject's real situation.
- **Standing correction and deletion duty** [Art. 6]: the controller must, without a data-subject request, eliminate or cancel data whose storage lacks legal basis or that has lapsed (caducado), modify erroneous data, and block data whose accuracy cannot be established.
- **Confidentiality** [Art. 7]: persons working in data processing must keep secret personal data collected from non-public sources; the duty persists after their activities end.
- **Sensitive data** [Arts. 2(g), 10]: sensitive data (defined in Art. 2(g) to include health, racial origin, political ideology, religious belief, and sexual life) may not be processed except where the law authorizes it, the data subject consents, or the data are necessary to determine or grant health benefits.
- **Processing by mandate (processor)** [Art. 8]: where processing is carried out by mandate, the mandate must be in writing and record the conditions of use, which the mandatario must respect.
- **Opposition** [Art. 3]: the data subject may oppose the use of their personal data for advertising, market research, or opinion surveys.

Ley 19.628 has **no comprehensive "privacy notice" instrument** equivalent to a Mexican aviso de privacidad; the closest requirement is the informed-authorization duty of Article 4 (the data subject must be informed of the purpose of storage and its possible communication to the public) and the survey-collection information duty of Article 3.

## Data-subject rights

Ley 19.628 grants data subjects a set of rights exercised directly against the controller (the law does not use the "ARCO" label, which is a Mexican construct):

- **Access / information** [Art. 12]: the right to demand information on one's data, its origin and recipients, the purpose of storage, and the identity of persons or bodies to which the data are regularly transmitted.
- **Rectification / modification** [Art. 12]: the right to have erroneous, inexact, equivocal, or incomplete data modified.
- **Cancellation / elimination** [Art. 12]: the right to have data eliminated where storage lacks legal basis or the data have lapsed.
- **Blocking** [Art. 12]: the right to demand blocking (or elimination) where the data were voluntarily provided or are used for commercial communications and the subject no longer wishes to appear.
- **Opposition** [Art. 3]: to advertising, market-research, and opinion-survey use.

The information, rectification, and elimination of data are provided free of charge, with a copy of the altered register on request; a further free copy of the updated register is available only where new modifications or eliminations have since been made, at least six months have passed since the previous exercise, and the data subject exercises the right personally [Art. 12]. The rights **cannot be limited by any act or agreement** [Art. 13]. The statutory response window is short: a controller that does not respond within **two business days** (dos días hábiles), or denies the request for a cause other than national security or the national interest, exposes itself to a civil-court action by the data subject [Art. 16].

## Cross-border transfers

**Ley 19.628 has no general cross-border-transfer regime.** It does not condition international data transfers on adequacy, standard contractual clauses, or a consent-plus-notice mechanism of the kind found in newer regimes. Its only directly relevant provision is Article 5, which governs automated transmission procedures: where personal data are requested through an electronic network, the controller records the requester, the motive and purpose of the request, and the type of data transmitted; the recipient may use the data only for the purposes that motivated the transmission; and the article does not apply to publicly accessible data, nor to transmission of personal data to international organizations in compliance with treaties in force [Art. 5].

Because Ley 19.628 lacks a modern transfer framework, an organization transferring Chilean personal data abroad relies on the general lawful-basis and purpose rules (express written authorization and purpose limitation, [Arts. 4, 9]) rather than a dedicated transfer instrument. A comprehensive cross-border-transfer regime is a Ley 21.719 feature; adopters verify its mechanics at adoption (see the Purpose currency note) and, in the interim, apply the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure with the stricter-of-standards approach.

## Security and breach

- **Duty of care** [Art. 11]: the controller of the registers or databases must safeguard the data with due diligence and is liable for the resulting damage. Ley 19.628 states this as a diligence standard rather than an enumerated administrative/technical/physical controls requirement, so the organization's security control set is governed operationally by the corpus security standards, with Article 11 as the statutory anchor.
- **Breach notification: Ley 19.628 contains no personal-data breach-notification obligation.** There is no statutory duty to notify data subjects or an authority of a security breach. (Breach notification is a Ley 21.719 feature; see the currency note.) In the interim, breach handling follows the organization's incident-response and privacy procedures, and the absence of a statutory duty does not displace contractual or sectoral obligations.

## Enforcement and remedies

- **Habeas-data procedure** [Art. 16]: enforcement of the access/rectification/cancellation/blocking rights runs through the civil courts (juez de letras en lo civil). The court may order compliance and impose a fine of **one to ten Unidades Tributarias Mensuales (UTM)**, or **ten to fifty UTM** for infringements of Articles 17 and 18 (the credit-reporting rules), and **two to fifty UTM** where the information is not delivered in time, or a modification is not effected, in the form the court decrees.
- **Civil liability** [Art. 23]: a controller (private natural or legal person, or public body) must indemnify the patrimonial and moral damage caused by improper processing; the action follows the sumario procedure and the amount is set prudentially by the judge.
- **Credit-reporting limits** [Arts. 17, 18, 19]: adverse economic/financial/banking/commercial data may not be communicated after **five years** from when the obligation became due [Art. 18], nor after it is paid; numerous debt categories are excluded and reporting of defaults that arose during a period of unemployment (cesantía) is prohibited [Art. 17].

Fine amounts are expressed in UTM because the UTM is indexed monthly; an adopter converts to the current peso value using the UTM in force for the relevant period. Ley 19.628 provides **no administrative fines regime and no supervisory-authority sanctioning power**, sanctions are court-imposed. (An administrative sanctions framework is a Ley 21.719 feature; see the currency note.)

## Employment and workforce monitoring

Where the organization monitors the network and device activity of workers in Chile, the processing of their personal data is governed by Ley 19.628 and, operationally, by the Workforce Network Monitoring Policy ([`security/policy-workforce-network-monitoring.md`](../../security/policy-workforce-network-monitoring.md)) and its supporting suite. Ley 19.628 has no employment-specific privacy regime; workforce monitoring is subject to the law's general provisions.

- **Purpose limitation and data quality apply** [Arts. 9, 6]. Monitoring must be for the purposes for which the data were collected, and the data must be accurate and up to date; monitoring data is not repurposed beyond the disclosed purposes.
- **Authorization and information** [Arts. 4, 3]. Processing requires a legal authorization or the worker's express written consent, and the person authorizing must be informed of the purpose of storage and its possible communication to the public [Art. 4]. Ley 19.628 has no aviso-de-privacidad instrument; the Article 4 informed-authorization duty is the closest counterpart. The [Employee Monitoring Notice Template](../template-employee-monitoring-notice.md) provides content adapted to that informed-authorization form. Note the corpus's standing caution about the reliability of employment consent: where consent is not a sound footing, the operative basis is a legal authorization under Article 4 rather than "general principles", and the necessity/proportionality analysis in the [Legitimate Interest Assessment for Employment Monitoring Annex](../annex-legitimate-interest-employment-monitoring.md) is used only as an analytic aid, adapted to Ley 19.628's authorization-and-purpose rules rather than to GDPR Article 6(1)(f).
- **Sensitive data** [Art. 10]: any monitoring that would capture sensitive data (for example health or political-opinion data as defined in Art. 2(g)) is prohibited unless the law authorizes it, the worker consents, or the data are necessary to determine or grant health benefits.
- **Confidentiality and due care** [Arts. 7, 11]: those handling monitored data owe a secrecy duty, and the controller owes the Article 11 duty of care.
- **Consultation, telemetry, and presence.** Where any representative-body or collective duty applies to introducing monitoring, it is determined and evidenced through the [Works Council and Employee Representative Consultation Procedure](../procedure-works-council-and-employee-representative-consultation.md). Connection metadata is governed by the [Network Telemetry and DPI Controls Standard](../standard-network-telemetry-and-dpi-controls.md) and presence signals by the [Presence Inference Limitations Standard](../standard-presence-inference-limitations.md), with the scope stated to workers mirroring the parent policy's in-scope and out-of-scope boundaries.

## Limitations

- This annex is a consolidating per-regime view of Ley 19.628, not a substitute for the enacted law, its regulations, or legal advice; the controlling text is Ley 19.628 (consolidated version in force from 9 May 2023, Fin Vigencia 30 November 2026).
- **Currency and transition.** Ley 19.628 is being superseded by Ley 21.719, which takes effect from 1 December 2026 and materially changes the regime (dedicated authority, cross-border-transfer rules, breach notification, administrative sanctions). Ley 21.719 (published 2024-12-13, vigencia deferred to 2026-12-01) is the enacted successor regime; this annex covers Ley 19.628, and an organization processing Chilean personal data at or after 1 December 2026 applies Ley 21.719, confirming its specific provisions against the primary source.
- **Gaps in Ley 19.628 stated honestly.** Ley 19.628 has no general cross-border-transfer regime, no breach-notification duty, no administrative sanctions authority, and no comprehensive privacy-notice instrument; the annex marks each as a Ley 21.719 forthcoming feature rather than asserting a requirement that the held law does not contain.
- **Corpus cross-references.** On any divergence for Chile private-sector processing between this annex and another corpus document, this annex governs the per-regime framing; the [Latin America privacy annex](annex-privacy-latin-america.md) provides the regional overview.
- Public-sector processing (Título IV), the Servicio de Registro Civil public-body registry (Art. 22), and sectoral rules are out of scope for this private-party annex.
