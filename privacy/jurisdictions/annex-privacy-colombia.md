## Purpose

This annex defines the privacy regulatory requirements applicable to the tratamiento (processing) of personal data in Colombia under Ley Estatutaria 1581 de 2012 and its reglamentary stack, principally Decreto 1377 de 2013 as compiled into the Decreto Único Reglamentario 1074 de 2015 (Capítulo 25), together with the Registro Nacional de Bases de Datos rules (Capítulo 26, compiling Decreto 886 de 2014 as modified by Decreto 90 de 2018) and the Normas Corporativas Vinculantes rules (Sección 7, added by Decreto 255 de 2022). It supplements the Privacy and Data Governance Policy and the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure. Ley 1581 applies to personal data registered in any database that makes them susceptible to tratamiento by public- or private-nature entities, to tratamiento carried out in Colombian territory, and to a controller or processor not established in Colombia where Colombian law applies to it by virtue of international norms and treaties [Ley Art 2].

Unlike a gap-heavy regime, Colombia's framework is fully operational: a national data-protection authority with sanctioning power (the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio, through its Delegatura para la Protección de Datos Personales), a threshold-scoped database-registration regime (the Registro Nacional de Bases de Datos), an adequacy-based cross-border-transfer regime with enumerated exceptions and a Binding Corporate Rules pathway, a demonstrated-accountability (responsabilidad demostrada) duty, and a mandatory data-protection person or area [Ley Arts 19, 21, 23, 25, 26; D1377 Arts 23, 26]. Data-protection duties for the credit and financial-information regime sit in a separate law, Ley 1266 de 2008, which is expressly excluded from Ley 1581's scope [Ley Art 2.e] and is not covered here.

## Applicable law and regulatory authority

- **Ley Estatutaria 1581 de 2012**, "por la cual se dictan disposiciones generales para la protección de datos personales", sanctioned 17 October 2012, published in Diario Oficial 48587 of 18 October 2012, following the Constitutional Court's Sentencia C-748 de 2011 [Ley, header + closing note].
- **Decreto 1377 de 2013**, partial reglamento, published in Diario Oficial 48834 of 27 June 2013; partially derogated by Decreto 1081 de 2015 and compiled into the Decreto Único Reglamentario 1074 de 2015 (Sector Comercio, Industria y Turismo), Capítulo 25 (arts 2.2.2.25.x) [D1377 header; DUR Cap 25 provenance]. Citations below use the currently-compiled DUR article numbers alongside the originating Decreto 1377 article.
- **Registro Nacional de Bases de Datos**: reglamented by Decreto 886 de 2014, compiled at DUR Capítulo 26 (arts 2.2.2.26.x), with the registration-scope threshold modified by Decreto 90 de 2018 [DUR Art 2.2.2.26.1.2].
- **Normas Corporativas Vinculantes** (Binding Corporate Rules): reglamented by Decreto 255 de 2022, compiled at DUR Sección 7 (arts 2.2.2.25.7.x) [DUR Sección 7 heading].
- **Authority**: the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (SIC), exercising vigilance through a Delegatura para la Protección de Datos Personales [Ley Art 19]. The SIC issues declarations of conformity on international transfers, administers the national database registry, conducts investigations, and may order the temporary blocking of data [Ley Art 21]. A complaint (queja) may be raised before the SIC only after the internal consulta or reclamo procedure has been exhausted [Ley Art 16].
- **Scope and exclusions**: the regime does not apply to exclusively personal or domestic databases (which become subject to the law if data are supplied to third parties, after prior notice and authorization), to national-security/defence and anti-money-laundering/counter-terrorist-financing databases, to intelligence and counterintelligence databases, to journalistic and editorial databases, or to databases regulated by Ley 1266 de 2008 (credit/financial data) or Ley 79 de 1993 (statistics); the data-protection principles nonetheless apply to all databases, including the excepted ones, within the law's limits [Ley Art 2 and its Parágrafo].

## Principles, lawful basis, and authorization

- **Guiding principles** [Ley Art 4], applied harmonically and integrally: legality, purpose (a legitimate purpose informed to the data subject), freedom (tratamiento only with prior, express, and informed consent, or a legal or judicial mandate that releases consent), truthfulness/quality (data truthful, complete, exact, updated, verifiable, and comprehensible), transparency, restricted access and circulation, security, and confidentiality.
- **Authorization (consent) is the primary lawful basis** [Ley Art 9]: prior and informed authorization, obtained by any means that can be subsequently consulted [Ley Art 3.a, Art 9], and valid where expressed in writing, orally, or through the data subject's unequivocal conduct, with silence never treated as unequivocal conduct [D1377 Art 7 / DUR 2.2.2.25.2.4]. A fresh authorization is required when a substantial change concerns the purpose of tratamiento [D1377 Art 5 / DUR 2.2.2.25.2.2]. Authorization may be revoked, and suppression requested, through a reclamo, except where the data subject has a legal or contractual duty to remain in the database [Ley Art 8.e; D1377 Art 9 / DUR 2.2.2.25.2.6].
- **Exceptions to authorization** are enumerated and narrow: information required by a public or administrative entity in exercise of legal functions or by judicial order; public-nature data; medical or health-emergency cases; processing authorized by law for historical, statistical, or scientific purposes; and data related to the civil registry [Ley Art 10]. The regime has no controller legitimate-interest basis.
- **Information duty**: at the moment of requesting authorization the controller must clearly and expressly inform the data subject of the tratamiento and its purpose, the facultative character of answering questions bearing on sensitive data or on the data of children and adolescents, the rights that assist them, and the controller's identity and physical or electronic address and telephone; the controller keeps proof [Ley Art 12]. Where the treatment policies cannot be put at the data subject's disposal, an aviso de privacidad discloses their existence and how to access them, with a prescribed minimum content [D1377 Arts 14-15 / DUR 2.2.2.25.3.2-3.3].
- **Sensitive data** [Ley Art 5: data affecting intimacy or whose improper use may generate discrimination, including racial or ethnic origin, political orientation, religious or philosophical convictions, union or social-organization or human-rights-organization membership or data promoting the interests of any political party or guaranteeing the rights of opposition parties, and data relating to health, sexual life, and biometric data] may be processed only under the enumerated Article 6 exceptions (explicit authorization, vital interest of an incapacitated subject, legitimate activities of a qualifying non-profit, a right in a judicial process, or a historical/statistical/scientific purpose with identity suppression); the controller must additionally inform the subject that they are not obliged to authorize sensitive-data processing, and no activity may be conditioned on the subject providing sensitive data [Ley Art 6; D1377 Art 6 / DUR 2.2.2.25.2.3]. The tratamiento of children's and adolescents' data is proscribed except for public-nature data; where such data are processed, the processing must respect the child's best interests and fundamental rights and be authorized by the legal representative after the child has been heard, with the child's maturity considered [Ley Art 7; DUR 2.2.2.25.2.9].
- **Retention**: data are collected, stored, used, or circulated only for the time reasonable and necessary to the purposes that justified the tratamiento, then suppressed, unless a legal or contractual obligation requires their conservation; there is no fixed statutory retention period [D1377 Art 11 / DUR 2.2.2.25.2.8].

## Data-subject rights

Data subjects hold the rights to know, update, and rectify their data; to request proof of the authorization given (except where authorization is exempt under Article 10); to be informed, on request, of the use given to their data; to file complaints before the SIC; to revoke authorization and/or request suppression where the tratamiento does not respect the applicable principles, rights, and guarantees (revocation or suppression proceeding once the SIC has determined the controller or processor engaged in conduct contrary to the law and the Constitution); and to access their data free of charge [Ley Art 8]. Free consultation is available at least once each calendar month and whenever the treatment policies change substantially; for more frequent consultations only shipping, reproduction, and certification costs may be charged [D1377 Art 21 / DUR 2.2.2.25.4.2].

The procedures and their statutory deadlines: a consulta (query) is answered within a maximum of ten (10) business days from receipt, extendable by no more than five (5) further business days [Ley Art 14]; a reclamo (claim to correct, update, or suppress data, or on a presumed breach of duties) is filed with the controller or processor and answered within a maximum of fifteen (15) business days counted from the day after receipt, extendable by no more than eight (8) further business days on notice explaining the delay and stating the response date; where the claim is incomplete, the recipient must, within five days after receiving it, require the claimant to cure the deficiencies, and the claim is deemed abandoned two months after that requirement if the claimant does not respond; once a complete claim is received, a "reclamo en trámite" legend stating the reason is recorded within two business days and remains until the claim is resolved [Ley Art 15]. A complaint before the SIC is admissible only after the consulta or reclamo has been exhausted [Ley Art 16].

## Cross-border transfers

Colombia operates an adequacy-based transfer regime. The transfer of personal data of any kind to countries that do not provide adequate levels of protection is prohibited; a country is deemed adequate when it meets the standards fixed by the SIC, which in no case may be lower than those the law requires of its own addressees [Ley Art 26]. The prohibition does not apply to: a transfer for which the data subject has given express and unequivocal authorization; an exchange of medical data required by the subject's treatment for health or public-hygiene reasons; banking or stock-exchange transfers under applicable legislation; transfers agreed under international treaties to which Colombia is a party, on a reciprocity basis; transfers necessary to perform a contract between the subject and the controller, or pre-contractual measures, provided the subject's authorization; and transfers legally required to safeguard the public interest or a right in a judicial process [Ley Art 26]. For cases outside these exceptions, the SIC issues a declaración de conformidad on the international transfer [Ley Art 21.g, Art 26 Parágrafo 1]. These rules apply to all personal data, including data contemplated by Ley 1266 de 2008 [Ley Art 26 Parágrafo 2].

The reglamento distinguishes a transferencia (to a receiver who is itself a controller, in or outside the country) from a transmisión (to a processor treating data on the controller's behalf); an international transmisión between a controller and a processor need not be notified to, or consented by, the data subject where a data-transmission contract meeting the prescribed content exists [D1377 Arts 24-25 / DUR 2.2.2.25.5.1-5.2]. Binding Corporate Rules (Normas Corporativas Vinculantes) are an SIC-approved intra-group transfer mechanism guaranteeing the Article 4 principles, the special-category rules, the legality conditions, and the controller duties [DUR Sección 7; Ley Art 27]. Organizations apply the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure with the stricter-of-standards approach.

## Database registration and accountability

Colombia maintains the Registro Nacional de Bases de Datos (RNBD), the public directory of databases subject to tratamiento operating in the country, administered by the SIC and open to citizen consultation; registrants supply their treatment policies, which may in no case be inferior to the duties in the law [Ley Art 25]. Registration is threshold-scoped rather than universal: the databases subject to inscription are those treated by controllers that are companies or non-profit entities with total assets exceeding 100,000 Unidades de Valor Tributario (UVT), or public-law legal persons [DUR Art 2.2.2.26.1.2, per Decreto 886 de 2014 as modified by Decreto 90 de 2018]. A controller within scope registers each qualifying database independently [DUR Art 2.2.2.26.1.3], and the minimum registered information covers the controller's and processors' identity and contact details, the channels for exercising rights, the database name and purpose, the form of tratamiento (manual and/or automated), and the treatment policy [DUR Art 2.2.2.26.2.1].

Every controller and processor must designate a person or area assuming the personal-data-protection function, which processes data-subject requests [D1377 Art 23 / DUR 2.2.2.25.4.4]. Controllers must be able to demonstrate to the SIC, on request, that they have implemented appropriate and effective measures proportional to the controller's juridical nature and enterprise size, the nature of the data, the type of tratamiento, and the potential risks to data subjects (the responsabilidad demostrada / demonstrated-accountability duty), supported by effective internal policies; the SIC weighs the existence of such measures when evaluating sanctions [D1377 Arts 26-27 / DUR 2.2.2.25.6.1-6.2]. Controllers must also adopt an internal manual of policies and procedures [Ley Art 17.k].

## Security and breach

- **Security duty**: information subject to tratamiento must be handled with the technical, human, and administrative measures necessary to secure the records against adulteration, loss, and unauthorized or fraudulent consultation, use, or access [Ley Art 4.g, Art 17.d, Art 18.b]; confidentiality binds all who intervene in the tratamiento of non-public data and persists after the relationship ends [Ley Art 4.h]. The specific security controls are set by SIC instruction rather than enumerated in the statute [D1377 Art 19 / DUR 2.2.2.25.3.7]; the organization's security control set is therefore governed operationally by the corpus security standards, with these provisions as the statutory anchor.
- **Breach handling**: the controller and processor must inform the data-protection authority when violations of the security codes occur and there are risks in the administration of data subjects' information [Ley Art 17.n, Art 18.k]. The held statute states this as a duty to inform the authority without prescribing a numeric notification deadline; timing and form are set by SIC instruction (not in the held sources), so breach handling follows the organization's incident-response and privacy procedures with these provisions as the statutory anchor.

## Enforcement and penalties

- Once the SIC establishes non-compliance, it adopts measures or imposes sanctions, following the Código Contencioso Administrativo for matters the law does not regulate [Ley Art 22].
- **Sanctions** [Ley Art 23]: fines, of a personal and institutional character, of up to two thousand (2,000) monthly minimum legal wages in force (salarios mínimos mensuales legales vigentes, SMLMV) at the time of imposition, which may be successive while the non-compliance persists; suspension of processing activities for up to six months; temporary closure of operations where correctives are not adopted after the suspension period; and immediate and definitive closure of operations involving the processing of sensitive data. These sanctions apply only to private-nature persons; where a public authority is involved, the SIC refers the matter to the Procuraduría General de la Nación.
- **Graduation criteria** [Ley Art 24]: the dimension of the harm, the economic benefit obtained, recidivism, resistance or obstruction of the SIC's investigation, refusal to comply with SIC orders, and express acknowledgement of the infraction before the sanction is imposed.
- Fine amounts are expressed in SMLMV because the monthly minimum wage is set annually; an adopter converts to the current peso value using the SMLMV in force for the relevant year. (The RNBD registration-scope threshold, by contrast, is expressed in UVT, a distinct fiscal unit.)

## Employment and workforce monitoring

Where the organization monitors the network and device activity of workers in Colombia, the processing is governed by Ley 1581 de 2012 and its reglamentos and, operationally, by the Workforce Network Monitoring Policy and its supporting suite. The framework has no employment-specific regime; workforce monitoring is subject to the general rules.

- **Lawful footing**: authorization is the primary basis [Ley Arts 9, 12], but the corpus's standing caution about the reliability of employment consent applies; where consent is not a sound footing the operative basis is an applicable Article 10 exception or another legal mandate that releases consent [Ley Arts 4.c, 10], not a controller legitimate-interest basis, which the regime does not provide. The Legitimate Interest Assessment for Employment Monitoring Annex is used only as an analytic aid, adapted to Colombia's authorization-and-purpose scheme rather than to GDPR Article 6(1)(f).
- **Sensitive data**: monitoring that would capture sensitive data (broadly defined at Ley Art 5) is prohibited unless an Article 6 exception applies, and the worker must be told they are not obliged to authorize sensitive-data processing, with no activity conditioned on providing it [Ley Arts 5-6; D1377 Art 6 / DUR 2.2.2.25.2.3].
- **Purpose, quality, security, confidentiality** apply [Ley Art 4.b, 4.d, 4.g, 4.h; Art 17.d]. Consultation, telemetry, and presence signals are handled through the Works Council and Employee Representative Consultation Procedure, the Network Telemetry and DPI Controls Standard, and the Presence Inference Limitations Standard, with scope stated to workers mirroring the parent policy's boundaries; the Employee Monitoring Notice Template provides content adapted to the Article 12 information duty and the aviso de privacidad content [Ley Art 12; D1377 Art 15 / DUR 2.2.2.25.3.3].

## Limitations

- This annex is a consolidating per-regime view of Ley 1581 de 2012 and its reglamentos, not a substitute for the enacted law, its reglamentos, or legal advice; the controlling texts are Ley 1581 de 2012 and the Decreto Único Reglamentario 1074 de 2015 (Capítulos 25 and 26) with its amending decrees.
- **Currency.** Colombia's regime has been stable since 2012, with no comprehensive replacement law in force in the held sources; however, the SIC issues binding instructions (circulars) on security measures, adequacy standards, and RNBD registration deadlines that the held statutory sources do not contain. An adopter confirms the current SIC instructions, the current RNBD registration threshold and deadlines, and any legislative reform against the primary source before relying on a specific procedural detail.
- **Related but out-of-scope regimes.** The credit and financial-information regime (Ley 1266 de 2008, habeas data financiero) and the statistics regime (Ley 79 de 1993) are expressly excluded from Ley 1581's scope [Ley Art 2.e-f] and are out of scope for this annex; an organization processing credit or financial-reporting data applies Ley 1266 separately.
- **Corpus cross-references.** On any divergence for Colombia private-sector processing between this annex and another corpus document, this annex governs the per-regime framing; the Latin America privacy annex provides the regional overview.
- Public-authority processing (where the SIC refers matters to the Procuraduría), and sectoral rules, are out of scope for this private-party annex.
