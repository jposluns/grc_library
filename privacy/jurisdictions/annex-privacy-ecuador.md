## Purpose

This annex defines the privacy regulatory requirements applicable to the tratamiento (processing) of personal data in Ecuador under the Ley Orgánica de Protección de Datos Personales (LOPDP) and its Reglamento General, issued by Decreto Ejecutivo 904. It supplements the Privacy and Data Governance Policy and the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure. The LOPDP applies to the tratamiento of personal data held in any type of medium, automated or not, and to every later form of use [Ley Art 2]. Without prejudice to the rules established in the international instruments ratified by the Ecuadorian State that concern this matter, it applies where the tratamiento takes place anywhere in the national territory, where the controller (responsable) or processor (encargado) is domiciled there, where a controller or processor not established in Ecuador processes the data of data subjects (titulares) residing in Ecuador and the processing relates to offering them goods or services, whether or not payment is required, or to monitoring their behaviour in Ecuador, and where Ecuadorian law applies to a controller or processor not domiciled in Ecuador by contract or by public international law [Ley Art 3].

Ecuador's framework is comprehensive: an independent supervisory authority with sanctioning power (the Autoridad de Protección de Datos Personales, headed by a Superintendente de Protección de Datos Personales), eight lawful bases including a controller legitimate-interest basis, a national registry of databases and processing (the Registro Nacional de Protección de Datos Personales), an adequacy-plus-guarantees cross-border regime with an authorization route, a five-day breach-notification duty, mandatory impact assessments for defined high-risk processing, and a mandatory data protection officer (delegado de protección de datos personales) in defined cases [Ley Arts 4, 7, 42, 43, 48, 51, 56-60, 76-77; Reg Art 79]. The held texts are the Spanish originals, and the Spanish text is authoritative; the English statements below translate or closely paraphrase them, with the key Spanish terms kept in parentheses.

## Applicable law and regulatory authority

- **Ley Orgánica de Protección de Datos Personales**, issued by the Asamblea Nacional on 10 May 2021, sanctioned and promulgated on 21 May 2021, and published in the Registro Oficial, Quinto Suplemento No. 459, of 26 May 2021; it entered into force on publication [Ley, header + Disposición Final]. The corrective-measures and sanctioning provisions entered into force two years after publication, and processing that predated the law had two years from publication to conform [Ley Disposiciones Transitorias Primera and Segunda]; international transfers made before the law entered into force are legitimate but had the same two-year window to be adapted [Ley Disposición Transitoria Cuarta]. Data subjects may exercise their rights regardless of the entry into force of the sanctioning regime [Ley Disposición General Séptima].
- **Amendment to Article 43**: the Ley Orgánica para el Fortalecimiento de la Ciberseguridad (LOFC), published in the Registro Oficial, Quinto Suplemento No. 290, of 22 May 2026 and in force from publication, replaced the first paragraph of LOPDP Article 43 (breach notification to the authorities) [LOFC Art 14 + Disposición Final]. This annex states Article 43 as so amended. That 2026 law is otherwise a separate cybersecurity instrument and is not covered here.
- **Reglamento General a la Ley Orgánica de Protección de Datos Personales**, Decreto Ejecutivo 904, given on 6 November 2023 and published in the Registro Oficial, Suplemento 435, of 13 November 2023, in force on publication [Reg header + Disposición Final]. It develops the law's rules for application [Reg Art 1]. The held compilation records it as in force (Vigente) with no later modification. The Reglamento predates the 2026 amendment to Article 43, so its breach provisions still name the telecommunications regulator as a recipient (see Security and breach).
- **Authority**: the Autoridad de Protección de Datos Personales, defined as an independent public authority that supervises the application of the law, its reglamento, and its own resolutions [Ley Art 4], and constituted as the control and oversight body charged with guaranteeing the protection of personal data [Ley Art 76]. It enjoys administrative, technical, operational, and financial autonomy, is headed by the Superintendente de Protección de Datos Personales, and is seated in Quito [Reg Art 79]. Its functions include supervising controllers and processors, sanctioning controllers, delegados, processors, and third parties, hearing and resolving complaints (reclamos), conducting or delegating technical audits, issuing general and technical rules, running the Registro Nacional, issuing standard data-protection clauses, and authorizing international transfers [Ley Art 76.1-76.6, 76.8, 76.10]. The Superintendente is designated from a shortlist (terna) sent by the President of the Republic and serves a five-year term [Ley Art 77]. The implementation and operation of the Superintendencia are subject to budget availability, after a favourable opinion from the governing body for public finances [Reg Disposición Transitoria Primera].
- **Representative for non-established controllers**: controllers and processors not established in Ecuador to which Ecuadorian law applies must designate a special attorney (apoderado especial) resident in Ecuador with sufficient powers to appear before administrative and judicial bodies, except where the processing is occasional, does not include large-scale processing of special categories, and is unlikely to entail a risk to rights and freedoms [Reg Arts 2-3].
- **Scope and exclusions**: the law does not apply to natural persons using data in family or domestic activities; to deceased persons, without prejudice to what Article 28 provides [Ley Art 2.b]; to anonymized data while the data subject cannot be identified; to journalistic activities and other editorial content; to data whose processing is governed by specialized rules of equal or higher rank on natural-disaster risk management or State security and defence; to data or databases established for the prevention, investigation, detection, or prosecution of criminal offences or the enforcement of criminal penalties, by competent State bodies performing their legal functions; or to data that identify or make identifiable legal persons [Ley Art 2.a-2.g]. In the disaster-management, security and defence, and criminal-justice cases, the processing must still comply with international human-rights standards and the law's principles, and at a minimum with the criteria of legality, proportionality, and necessity [Ley Art 2.e-2.f]. The held text of Article 2(b) refers to Article 28, which governs credit data; the rules on deceased persons' data are in Article 27, and this annex states the cross-reference as the law has it. Under Article 27, the holders of succession rights (titulares de derechos sucesorios) may ask the controller for access to, rectification and updating of, or deletion of the deceased's data, provided the data subject did not, in life, indicate another use or destination for the data; persons or institutions the deceased expressly designated may also request access and, where applicable, rectification, updating, or deletion in line with the instructions received; and for a deceased child, adolescent, or person the law recognizes as incapable, the last legal representative may exercise these powers [Ley Art 27].
- **Publicly accessible contact data**: the contact data of professionals, and the data of merchants, representatives, partners, and shareholders of legal persons, and of public servants, are publicly accessible and may be processed provided they relate to the exercise of the person's profession, trade, line of business (giro de negocio), competences, powers, or office and consist of names and surnames, functions or positions held, postal or electronic address, and professional telephone number. For public servants, the historical and current asset declaration (declaración patrimonial) and remuneration are also publicly accessible and may be processed [Ley Art 2, final paragraph].

## Principles, lawful basis, and consent

- **Principles** [Ley Art 10]: juridicidad (legality), lealtad (fairness), transparencia (transparency), finalidad (purpose limitation, with further processing allowed only where compatible with the original purpose, judged by the context of collection, the information given to the data subject at collection, the data subject's reasonable expectations, the nature of the data, the consequences, and the safeguards), pertinencia y minimización (relevance and minimization), proporcionalidad (proportionality), confidencialidad (confidentiality), calidad y exactitud (quality and accuracy), conservación (storage limitation), seguridad (security), responsabilidad proactiva y demostrada (proactive and demonstrated accountability, including a duty to account to the data subject and the Authority), aplicación favorable al titular (interpretation most favourable to the data subject), and independencia del control (independent oversight).
- **Lawful bases** [Ley Art 7]: processing is lawful where it rests on (1) the data subject's consent for one or more specific purposes; (2) a legal obligation of the controller; (3) a judicial order, with the law's principles observed; (4) a public-interest mission or public powers derived from a competence attributed by a norm with the rank of law, subject to the applicable international human-rights standards, the law's principles, and the criteria of legality, proportionality, and necessity; (5) pre-contractual measures at the data subject's request or the performance of contractual obligations pursued by the controller, the processor, or a legally authorized third party; (6) the vital interests of the data subject or another natural person, such as their life, health, or integrity; (7) data in publicly accessible databases; or (8) a legitimate interest of the controller or a third party, unless the data subject's interests or fundamental rights prevail. Unlike the Colombian and Peruvian regimes, Ecuador therefore provides a controller legitimate-interest basis.
- **Legitimate interest**: only data strictly necessary for the purpose may be processed, the processing must be transparent to the data subject, and the Authority may require a risk report [Ley Art 9]. The Reglamento requires a balancing test (regla de ponderación) through a meticulous evaluation of the legitimate interest (which must be necessary and proportionate), the impact on data subjects, the provisional balance struck by the controller's proportionality and transparency measures, and the additional safeguards applied [Reg Art 7.3]. The Reglamento also conditions the public-interest basis on a competence attributed by a norm with the rank of law [Reg Art 7.1], limits reliance on another person's vital interest to cases where no other basis manifestly applies [Reg Art 7.2], and requires further processing of publicly sourced data to be compatible with the purpose of the publication [Reg Art 7.4].
- **Consent** must be free, specific, informed, and unequivocal [Ley Art 4, Art 8]. It may be revoked at any time without justification, through mechanisms that are quick, efficient, effective, and free of charge and a simple procedure similar to the one used to collect it; revocation has no retroactive effect, and consent for several purposes must cover each of them [Ley Art 8]. Where explicit consent is required, the controller informs the data subject in advance and in detail of the types of processing, purposes, retention period, protection measures, and consequences; consent is given by a statement, a pronouncement to unsubscribe (pronunciamiento para darse de baja), or a clear affirmative action, the controller must demonstrate it when the competent authority so requires, and silence or inaction alone does not imply consent [Reg Art 5]. The controller suspends processing once it receives notice of revocation [Reg Art 6].
- **Information duty** [Ley Art 12]: the data subject is informed of, among other items, the purposes, the legal basis, the types of processing, the retention period, the existence of the database, the source of data not obtained from the data subject, further purposes, the controller's identity and contact details (legal address, telephone, and email), where applicable the delegado's identity and contact details, intended national or international transfers with their recipients, purposes, and safeguards, the consequences of providing or refusing the data, the possibility of revoking consent, how to exercise each right, where and how to complain to the controller and the Authority, and the existence of automated assessments and decisions, including profiling. The information is given at the moment of collection where data are collected from the data subject, and otherwise within thirty (30) days or at the first communication with the data subject, whichever comes first.
- **Special categories** [Ley Art 25] are sensitive data, data of children and adolescents, health data, and data of persons with disabilities and their substitutes relating to the disability. Sensitive data cover ethnicity, gender identity, cultural identity, religion, ideology, political affiliation, judicial record (pasado judicial), migratory status, sexual orientation, health, biometric data, genetic data, and data whose improper processing may give rise to discrimination or infringe fundamental rights and freedoms [Ley Art 4]. Processing sensitive data is prohibited unless one of the following applies: the data subject has given explicit consent specifying the purposes; the processing is necessary for specific obligations and rights of the controller or the data subject in labour law and social security and protection; it is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or another natural person where the data subject is physically or legally incapable of consenting; the data subject has manifestly made the data public; a judicial authority has ordered it; it is necessary for archiving in the public interest, scientific or historical research, or statistics, in proportion to the aim pursued, respecting the essence of the right to data protection, and with adequate and specific safeguards for the data subject's interests and fundamental rights; or it is health-data processing under the law's health rules [Ley Art 26.a-26.g; health rules at Ley Arts 30-32].
- **Children and adolescents**: in addition to the Article 20 conditions on automated decisions, sensitive data or the data of children and adolescents may not be processed unless the data subject or their legal representative has given express authorization, or the processing is intended to safeguard an essential public interest, assessed against international human-rights standards, that at a minimum meets the criteria of legality, proportionality, and necessity and includes specific safeguards to protect the data subjects' fundamental rights [Ley Art 21]. Adolescents aged 15 or over may give explicit consent to the processing of their data where the purposes are clearly specified to them [Ley Art 21]. The Reglamento requires the legal representative's consent to process the data of children under 15, and the legal representative's express consent for sensitive-data processing and for decisions based on automated assessments of minors [Reg Art 19]; for those two cases it does not mention the data subject's own express authorization or the essential-public-interest alternative that Ley Article 21 provides, and an adopter confirms with counsel how the two provisions interact. Consent that undermines the child's best interests is invalid [Reg Art 20]. Adolescents over fifteen (15) and under eighteen (18) years of age may exercise their rights directly before the Authority or the controller; adolescents over twelve (12) and under fifteen (15), and children, need their legal representative to exercise them [Ley Art 24].
- **Retention**: data are kept no longer than necessary for the purpose, and the controller sets periods for their deletion or periodic review [Ley Art 10.i]. The Authority is to regulate retention periods [Reg Art 8]. Once the purpose is fulfilled and no legal or regulatory provision, legitimate interest, or legal obligation requires retention, the controller deletes, blocks, or anonymizes the data [Reg Art 9]; the database register file states the retention period [Reg Art 10]; and secure deletion follows the end of that period, except where processing remains necessary for public-interest reasons in health, State, security, labour, or education matters, or for legal claims [Reg Art 11].

## Data-subject rights

Data subjects hold the rights to information [Ley Art 12]; access, free of charge and answered within fifteen (15) days, provided it is not exercised abusively [Ley Art 13]; rectification and updating of inaccurate or incomplete data, answered within fifteen (15) days, with the recipient informed within the same period [Ley Art 14]; elimination on seven grounds (processing that breaches the principles, processing not necessary or relevant to the purpose, purpose fulfilled, retention period expired, processing that affects fundamental rights or individual freedoms, consent revoked or stated never to have been given, and legal obligation), free of charge and within fifteen (15) days [Ley Art 15]; opposition in three cases, including objection at any time to processing for direct marketing, including profiling, after which the data are no longer processed for those purposes, and objection to legitimate-interest processing justified by the data subject's concrete personal situation, unless a law provides otherwise; the controller stops unless it shows compelling legitimate grounds that prevail over the data subject's interests, rights, and freedoms, or needs the data for legal claims, and answers within fifteen (15) days [Ley Art 16]; portability in a structured, common, interoperable, machine-readable format where the processing rests on consent, is automated, involves a relevant volume of data, or is necessary for obligations and rights in labour law and social security, but not for inferred or derived data [Ley Art 17]; suspension of processing while accuracy is contested, where processing is unlawful and the data subject prefers restriction, where the controller no longer needs the data but the data subject needs them for legal claims, or, where the data subject has objected, while it is verified whether the controller's legitimate grounds prevail over the data subject's (the held text ties this case to an objection "en virtud del artículo 31", the health-data article; the objection right is in Article 16) [Ley Art 19]; not to be subject to a decision based solely or partly on automated assessments [Ley Art 20]; consultation of the Registro Nacional free of charge [Ley Art 22]; and digital education [Ley Art 23]. The rights cannot be waived, and any contrary clause is void [Ley Art 24].

The rights of rectification, updating, elimination, opposition, annulment, and portability do not apply in ten enumerated cases, including where the requester is not the data subject or a duly accredited representative, where the data are needed for a legal or contractual obligation, a judicial order, resolution, or reasoned mandate of a competent public authority, for legal claims, to avoid harm to third parties' rights or legitimate interests that the controller accredits when answering the request, to avoid obstructing duly notified judicial or administrative proceedings, for freedom of expression, for vital interests, for the public interest (subject to the applicable international human-rights standards, the law's principles, and the criteria of legality, proportionality, and necessity), or for archiving State heritage or scientific, historical, or statistical research [Ley Art 18].

**Automated decisions** [Ley Art 20]: against a decision based solely or partly on automated processing, including profiling, that produces legal effects on the data subject or infringes their fundamental rights and freedoms, the data subject may request a reasoned explanation, present observations, request the assessment criteria of the automated program, request information on the types of data used and their source, and challenge the decision before the controller or processor. The right does not apply where the decision is necessary to enter into or perform a contract between the data subject and the controller or processor; is authorized by the applicable rules, a judicial order, or a resolution or reasoned mandate of a competent technical authority, with adequate measures established to safeguard the data subject's fundamental rights and freedoms; rests on the data subject's explicit consent; or carries no serious impacts or verifiable risks for the data subject. Waiver of the right may not be required in advance through mass adhesion contracts. At the latest at the first communication with the data subject to inform them of a decision based solely on automated assessments, the right is explicitly made known to them by any suitable means.

**Procedure**: the data subject may, at any time and free of charge, submit requests, petitions, complaints, or claims directly to the controller through the physical or digital means the controller provides, and the controller has a term (término) of ten (10) days to answer and act [Ley Art 62]. The held texts do not reconcile this general ten-day term with the fifteen-day periods (plazo) stated for the individual rights in Articles 13 to 16; an adopter confirms with counsel how the two interact and answers at the earliest. The controller should provide simplified digital channels [Reg Art 12]; a request states the data subject's identity, the data concerned, the request, and the right exercised [Reg Art 13]; the controller may ask once, within five (5) days, for clarification, which the data subject supplies within ten (10) days [Reg Art 14]; and the controller records every request and its handling [Reg Art 15]. If the controller does not answer in time or refuses, or, under the Reglamento, the data subject has reason to believe the answer infringed their rights, the data subject may file an administrative complaint with the Authority under the Código Orgánico Administrativo, without prejudice to civil, criminal, or constitutional actions [Ley Art 64; Reg Art 16].

## Cross-border transfers

Ecuador operates an adequacy-plus-guarantees regime. An international transfer or communication is possible where it follows the chapter on international transfers, the law, or specialized rules [Ley Art 55]. As a general principle, personal data may be transferred or communicated to countries, organizations, and legal persons in general that offer adequate levels of protection and that conform to the obligation to comply with and guarantee internationally recognized standards, under the criteria the Reglamento sets [Ley Art 56]; the Authority declares adequacy by reasoned resolution, ex officio or on request, with general effect, so that transfers to the declared destination need no prior authorization [Ley Art 56; Reg Art 71]. Adequacy decisions are reviewed annually and may be revoked, modified, or suspended without retroactive effect, and are published in the Registro Oficial and on the Authority's website [Reg Art 72], against criteria that include the destination's data-protection and national-security legislation, its onward-transfer rules, its case law, the rights and duties it recognizes, and the existence of an independent authority with sanctioning power [Reg Art 73]. Where the Authority detects that a country or organization no longer offers an adequate level of protection, it issues a resolution of non-adequacy, after which transfers there do not proceed unless another transfer mechanism of the chapter applies; and it publishes the list of countries, organizations, companies, or economic groups that guarantee adequate levels of protection [Ley Art 61].

Absent adequacy, a transfer may proceed where the controller or processor offers adequate guarantees: compliance with the principles, rights, and obligations at a standard equal to or higher than Ecuadorian law, effective protection through permanently available administrative or judicial actions, and the right to seek full reparation where applicable; the transfer rests on a legal instrument that contains these standards and those the Authority sets, and that instrument must be binding [Ley Art 57]. The Reglamento lists the instruments: binding instruments between public authorities; Binding Corporate Rules (normas corporativas vinculantes) approved by the Authority; standard data-protection clauses adopted by international data-protection bodies and endorsed by the Authority; codes of conduct and certification mechanisms carrying binding commitments of the recipient; and non-standard contractual clauses authorized by the Authority [Reg Art 74]. Binding Corporate Rules must meet eleven statutory conditions [Ley Art 58] and be authorized by the Authority as legally binding and conferring enforceable rights on data subjects [Reg Arts 75-76].

In other cases the Authority's prior authorization is required, with documented compliance [Ley Art 59], granted only where a contract binds the recipient to comply with Ecuadorian law and either to accept the jurisdiction of the Authority and the Ecuadorian courts or to operate in a destination that guarantees data subjects' rights, including a complaint to a data-protection authority and effective judicial protection [Reg Art 77]. Without prejudice to these rules, eleven exceptional cases also permit a transfer, including the data subject's explicit consent to the proposed transfer after being informed of its possible risks arising from the absence of an adequacy resolution and of adequate guarantees; a transfer whose purpose is to fulfil a legal or regulatory obligation; a transfer necessary to perform a contract between the data subject and the controller, or to carry out pre-contractual measures taken at the data subject's request; a transfer necessary for reasons of public interest, for international judicial collaboration, or for the formulation, exercise, or defence of claims, administrative or judicial actions, and appeals; transfers in banking and stock-exchange operations; and a transfer necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of other persons where the data subject is physically or legally incapable of consenting [Ley Art 60]. Information on international transfers must be registered in advance in the Registro Nacional [Ley Art 59], stating the destination country, the data categories, the purposes, the recipient, the mechanism used, and any exception relied on [Reg Art 78]. Organizations apply the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure with the stricter-of-standards approach.

## Database registration and accountability

- **Registro Nacional de Protección de Datos Personales**: the controller reports and keeps updated, before the Authority, the identification of each database or processing, the name, legal domicile, and contact details of the controller and processor, the characteristics and purpose of the processing, the nature of the data, the recipients (including processors and third parties), the way the registered information is interrelated, the means used to implement the law's principles, rights, and obligations, the security measures, and the retention period [Ley Art 51; Ley Art 47.12]. Each database or processing is reported separately, without registering the data themselves [Reg Art 85], within ten (10) days from the day after the processing begins [Reg Art 86].
- **Controller obligations** [Ley Art 47]: the controller processes data in strict adherence to the law and the Authority's rules; implements administrative, technical, physical, organizational, and legal measures to guarantee and demonstrate compliance and periodically verifies their efficiency and effectiveness; adopts data-protection policies; uses risk-analysis methodologies; assesses the adequacy of the security level before processing; takes measures to prevent and mitigate risks and breaches; notifies the Authority and the data subject of breaches of the security implemented for the processing, in accordance with the procedure provided for that purpose; implements data protection by design and by default; signs confidentiality and proper-handling contracts with the processor and with staff; makes certain that the processor offers sufficient guarantees; registers in the Registro Nacional; designates a delegado where required; and permits audits by an auditor accredited by the Authority. The processor bears the same obligations as far as applicable [Ley Art 47, final paragraph].
- **Accountability and records**: the controller must demonstrate that it has applied the necessary measures and may use key performance indicators to do so [Reg Art 36]; the inability to bear costs is no excuse for non-compliance, with proportionality observed between the volume of processing and the controller's economic capacity [Reg Art 35]; and the measures are reviewed and updated when necessary [Reg Art 58]. Controllers with one hundred (100) or more workers keep a written or electronic record of processing activities with nine prescribed items, available to the Authority on request [Reg Art 38]; controllers with fewer workers keep one where the processing may entail a risk, is not occasional, or includes special categories [Reg Art 39]; and a processor keeps a record where its controller must [Reg Art 44]. Joint controllers define their respective tasks and responsibilities transparently by contract, to the extent that legal provisions do not already define them, and are jointly and severally liable before the Authority and data subjects [Reg Art 37].
- **Processors**: a processor's access to data to provide a service to the controller is not a transfer; the processing is governed by a contract stating that the processor processes the data only on the controller's instructions, does not use them for other purposes, and does not transfer them, even for storage; on completion the data are destroyed or returned under the Authority's supervision; and the processor is liable for its own infractions [Ley Art 34]. The contract states the object, duration, nature, purpose, data categories, data subjects, and the processor's obligations [Reg Art 41]. A processor that, for any reason, determines the purposes and means of the processing is treated as a controller as regards that processing, and a processor informs the controller without undue delay of an instruction it considers unlawful [Reg Art 43]; it subcontracts only where the contract so provides or with the controller's written authorization [Reg Art 45], returns or deletes the data, as the controller instructs, at the end of the relationship, unless a legal provision requires them to be kept [Reg Art 46], assists the controller with data-subject rights [Reg Art 42], and allows the controller to audit its records and processes [Reg Art 47].
- **Impact assessment**: the controller carries out an impact assessment where processing is likely to entail a high risk or the Authority requires it, and every impact assessment is carried out before the processing begins; the assessment is mandatory for systematic and exhaustive evaluation of personal aspects based on automated processing, including profiling, on which decisions with legal effects are based; for large-scale processing of special categories or of criminal-conviction and offence data; and for large-scale systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area [Ley Art 42]. A mandatory assessment is filed with the Authority with a prescribed minimum content [Reg Art 32], and in case of doubt the controller may consult the Authority, which answers within five (5) days [Reg Art 31]. The Reglamento sets the factors and examples for large-scale processing [Reg Art 4.7].
- **Data protection by design and by default** [Ley Art 39; Reg Arts 59-60], with optional self-regulation through codes of conduct, certifications, seals, and standard clauses that do not exempt the controller from its duties [Ley Arts 52-54; Reg Arts 61-70].
- **Data protection officer**: a delegado de protección de datos personales, a natural person [Ley Art 4; Reg Art 48], must be designated where the processing is carried out by the public sector; where the controller's or processor's activities require permanent and systematic control because of their volume, nature, scope, or purposes; where the processing involves large-scale processing of special categories; and in a fourth case worded as processing that does not refer to national-security and State-defence data lacking reserve or secrecy under specialized rules [Ley Art 48.1-48.4], whose reach the held texts do not explain. The Authority may define further cases [Ley Art 48]. The delegado advises the controller, its staff, and the processor, supervises compliance, advises on risk analysis, impact assessments, and security measures, and cooperates with and acts as contact point for the Authority; it answers administratively, civilly, and criminally for failure to perform [Ley Art 49]. The controller and processor involve the delegado appropriately and in a timely manner, give access to data and resources, train the delegado, may not dismiss or sanction the delegado for the correct performance of their functions, and provide a direct line to the highest executive level; data subjects may contact the delegado; and the delegado keeps strict confidentiality [Ley Art 50]. The Reglamento requires independence [Reg Art 48], allows employment or service contracts [Reg Art 49] and a single delegado for a business group where the delegado can carry out the role without a conflict of interest [Reg Art 50], sets the criteria for permanent and systematic control [Reg Art 53], and requires the delegado to hold political rights, be of age, hold a third-level degree in law, information systems, communication, or technology, and have at least five years' professional experience [Reg Art 55]. Members of the controller's or processor's administration and control bodies, its partners or shareholders, the spouses of its administrators, directors, or comisarios, or their relatives up to the fourth degree of consanguinity or second of affinity, and persons with conflicts of interest may not serve [Reg Art 56], and the delegado signs a confidentiality agreement that survives the relationship [Reg Art 57].

## Security and breach

- **Security duty**: controllers and processors implement all adequate and necessary security measures, accepted by the state of the art, to protect personal data against any risk, threat, or vulnerability [Ley Art 10.j]. They take account of the categories and volume of data, the state of the art, integral-security best practices, implementation costs, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, continuously verify the effectiveness of their measures, and evidence that the measures mitigate the identified risks; measures may include anonymization, pseudonymization, or encryption, measures to keep systems confidential, intact, and available and to restore access rapidly after incidents, resilience measures, and international standards for risk management and information-security systems, or codes of conduct recognized and authorized by the Authority [Ley Art 37]. The risk-analysis methodology and the selection of measures follow prescribed factors [Ley Arts 40-41]. The organization's security control set is governed operationally by the corpus security standards, with these provisions as the statutory anchor.
- **Notification to the authorities**: a breach (vulneración de la seguridad de los datos personales) is a security incident affecting the confidentiality, availability, or integrity of personal data [Ley Art 4]. The controller notifies the breach to the Autoridad de Protección de Datos Personales, to the competent regulatory and control body, and, for information and technical coordination of incident management and mitigation, to the corresponding CSIRT, as soon as possible and at the latest within a term (término) of five (5) days after becoming aware of it, unless the breach is unlikely to constitute a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons; a notification to the Authority made after five days states the reasons for the delay [Ley Art 43, first paragraph, as substituted by LOFC Art 14]. A processor notifies the controller as soon as possible and at the latest within two (2) days of becoming aware [Ley Art 43, second paragraph]. The Reglamento treats a breach as a risk where data are destroyed or unavailable, altered or incomplete, out of the controller's control, or processed without authorization or unlawfully [Reg Art 24], and prescribes the notification content: the nature and type of breach, the affected data subjects, the initial detail of the affected systems, the presumed cause, the volume and types of data exposed, the measures taken and planned, the risk assessment, and other items the Authority sets [Reg Art 26]; a processor's notice carries the same content except the risk assessment [Reg Art 27]. Reglamento Articles 24 and 25 still name the telecommunications regulator (the Agencia de Regulación y Control de Telecomunicaciones), which the original wording of Article 43 named as a recipient; an adopter confirms the current recipients against the primary sources.
- **Notification to data subjects**: where the breach entails a risk to the data subject's fundamental rights and individual freedoms, the controller notifies the data subject without delay and within a term of three (3) days from becoming aware of the risk [Ley Art 46]. No notice is needed where protective measures that can be shown to be effective were applied to the affected data or measures guarantee that the risk will not materialize, both exceptions to be qualified by the Authority, or where notice would require disproportionate effort, in which case a public communication is made instead [Ley Art 46]. The notice uses clear and simple language and carries the same content as the authority notice [Reg Art 28]. The controller's timely notification of the breach to the data subject and timely execution of response measures are to be considered mitigating (atenuante); the held text of that sentence breaks off mid-word at "atenuante de la infra" [Ley Art 46].

## Enforcement and penalties

- **Corrective measures** [Ley Arts 65-66]: on non-compliance, the Authority issues corrective measures, which may consist of, among others, cessation of processing under set conditions or deadlines, deletion of data, or technical, legal, organizational, or administrative measures. For a minor infraction by a party listed in the Registro Único de Responsables y Encargados Incumplidos, and for a very serious infraction, the Authority opens the sanctioning procedure directly; for a serious infraction it first orders corrective measures and sanctions late, partial, or defective compliance. The held text types only minor (leves) and serious (graves) infractions [Ley Arts 67-70] and sets fines only for those two tiers [Ley Arts 71-72]; it defines no very serious infraction despite the reference in Article 66.
- **Infractions**: controller minor infractions include failing to handle data-subject requests in time and failing to implement data protection by design and by default [Ley Art 67]; controller serious infractions include using data for undeclared purposes, failing to carry out a required impact assessment, failing to notify the Authority and the data subject of security breaches where they affect the data subjects' fundamental rights and individual freedoms, failing to keep the Registro Nacional updated, and failing to designate a required delegado [Ley Art 68]; processor infractions are typed separately, including failing to notify the controller of a breach or doing so with unjustified delay [Ley Arts 69-70].
- **Fines** [Ley Arts 71-72]: for a private-law entity or a public company, a minor infraction carries a fine of 0.1% to 0.7% and a serious infraction a fine of 0.7% to 1% of its volumen de negocio (business volume) for the financial year immediately preceding the fine; public servants are fined one to ten (minor) or ten to twenty (serious) salarios básicos unificados. The Authority graduates the fine by intentionality, repetition, the nature of the harm, and recidivism. Business volume is the revenue from sales of products and services in the last financial year, net of value-added tax and other directly related taxes [Ley Art 73]. Where the infringer has no domicile or legal representation in Ecuador, the resolution is notified to the data-protection authority of its principal domicile [Ley Art 72, final paragraph]. The Authority may also apply provisional or precautionary measures [Ley Art 74].
- **Procedure and registry**: the sanctioning procedure follows the Código Orgánico Administrativo, and sanctions apply without prejudice to civil or criminal liability [Reg Art 90; Ley Disposición General Primera]. Infringers with a final resolution are entered in the public Registro Único de Responsables y Encargados Incumplidos, whose data are kept for at most seven (7) years from the date the final resolution or judgment (resolución o sentencia en firme) is issued; the law gives the registry social, statistical, preventive, and training purposes, and the Reglamento confines its use to statistical, preventive, and training purposes [Ley Disposición General Sexta; Reg Arts 87-89].
- Fine amounts are expressed as a percentage of business volume for private-law entities and public companies; the public-servant fines are expressed in salarios básicos unificados, an amount the held texts do not state, which an adopter converts using the value in force for the relevant year.

## Employment and workforce monitoring

Where the organization monitors the network and device activity of workers in Ecuador, the processing is governed by the LOPDP and its Reglamento and, operationally, by the Workforce Network Monitoring Policy ([`security/policy-workforce-network-monitoring.md`](../../security/policy-workforce-network-monitoring.md)) and its supporting suite. The held texts carry no employment-specific monitoring regime; workforce monitoring is subject to the general rules.

- **Lawful footing**: the corpus's standing caution about the reliability of employment consent applies. Where consent is not a sound footing, Ecuador's controller legitimate-interest basis is available, subject to strict necessity, transparency, and the Reglamento's balancing test [Ley Arts 7.8, 9; Reg Art 7.3]; the Legitimate Interest Assessment for Employment Monitoring Annex is used as the analytic aid, adapted to the four ponderación factors of Reglamento Article 7.3 rather than to GDPR Article 6(1)(f).
- **Sensitive data**: monitoring that would capture sensitive data (broadly defined at Ley Art 4) is prohibited unless an Article 26 exception applies; the labour-law exception covers processing necessary for specific obligations and rights of the controller or the data subject in labour law and social security and protection, not monitoring generally [Ley Art 26.b].
- **Automated decisions and impact assessment**: workers hold the Article 20 rights against decisions based solely or partly on automated assessments that produce legal effects on them or infringe their fundamental rights and freedoms [Ley Art 20], and systematic and exhaustive evaluation of personal aspects by automated means on which decisions with legal effects are based requires a prior impact assessment [Ley Art 42.a].
- **Purpose, minimization, proportionality, and security** apply [Ley Art 10.d-10.f, 10.j; Art 37]. Consultation, telemetry, and presence signals are handled through the Works Council and Employee Representative Consultation Procedure, the Network Telemetry and DPI Controls Standard, and the Presence Inference Limitations Standard, with scope stated to workers mirroring the parent policy's boundaries; the Employee Monitoring Notice Template provides content adapted to the Article 12 information duty [Ley Art 12].

## Limitations

- This annex is a consolidating per-regime view of the LOPDP and its Reglamento, not a substitute for the enacted texts or legal advice; the controlling texts are the Ley Orgánica de Protección de Datos Personales (Registro Oficial, Quinto Suplemento No. 459, 26 May 2021), as amended, and the Reglamento General (Decreto Ejecutivo 904, Registro Oficial, Suplemento 435, 13 November 2023).
- **Currency.** The held texts are the LOPDP as published on 26 May 2021, the Article 43 amendment published on 22 May 2026, and the Reglamento as published on 13 November 2023. This annex could not confirm whether any of these texts has been amended since, or whether the Superintendencia is constituted and operating. The law and Reglamento leave several matters to the Authority's own rules (the Registro Nacional reporting process, retention periods, portability, adequacy resolutions, standard clauses, impact-assessment lists, delegado independence, and breach-notification forms). The implementing resolutions of the Superintendencia de Protección de Datos Personales are not held in the reference base, and this annex does not cover them. <!-- ref-absence: Superintendencia de Protección de Datos Personales resolution | SPDP resolution --> An adopter confirms the current texts, the Authority's resolutions, and the current breach-notification recipients against the primary sources before relying on a specific procedural detail.
- **Related but out-of-scope regimes.** Credit-data processing has its own rules [Ley Arts 28-29], with regulation assigned to the Junta de Política y Regulación Financiera and the Superintendencia de Bancos [Reg Art 18]; health-data processing has specific rules [Ley Arts 30-32]; and the security of personal data in the public sector is governed by a governmental information-security mechanism [Ley Art 38]. These are summarized only where they bear on private-sector processing. The Ley Orgánica para el Fortalecimiento de la Ciberseguridad (2026) is cited only for its amendment to Article 43 and is otherwise out of scope.
- **Corpus cross-references.** On any divergence for Ecuador private-sector processing between this annex and another corpus document, this annex governs the per-regime framing; the Latin America privacy annex provides the regional overview.
- Public-sector processing, including the sanctions for public servants and the public-sector delegado rules, and sectoral rules are out of scope for this private-party annex.
