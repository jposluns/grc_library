## Purpose

This annex defines the privacy regulatory requirements applicable to the processing (tratamiento) of personal data in Peru under Ley Nº 29733, Ley de Protección de Datos Personales, and its regulation, Decreto Supremo Nº 016-2024-JUS. It supplements the Privacy and Data Governance Policy and the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure. Ley 29733 applies to personal data contained in, or destined for, public- and private-administration data banks (bancos de datos personales) processed in the national territory, with sensitive data an object of special protection [Ley Art 3].

Unlike some regional regimes, Peru's framework is a fully operational one: a national data-protection authority with sanctioning power, a mandatory data-bank registration regime, an adequacy-plus-safeguards cross-border-transfer regime, a personal-data security-incident notification duty, and a Personal Data Officer requirement. The 2024 regulation (DS 016-2024-JUS) replaced the 2013 regulation (DS 003-2013-JUS) and modernized the regime; an adopter confirms current in-force versions and the current authority designation against the primary source before relying on a specific value.

## Applicable law and regulatory authority

- **Ley Nº 29733**, Ley de Protección de Datos Personales, published in El Peruano on 3 July 2011, as amended (notably by Decreto Legislativo Nº 1353, published 7 January 2017) [Ley, header + amendment tags].
- **Decreto Supremo Nº 016-2024-JUS**, Reglamento de la Ley Nº 29733, published in El Peruano on 30 November 2024 (Título Preliminar + three Títulos + 135 articles + six final and two transitory complementary provisions) [DS Art 1], in force 120 calendar days after publication [DS Primera DCF]. It expressly repealed DS 003-2013-JUS [DS Disposición Complementaria Derogatoria Única].
- **Authority**: the Autoridad Nacional de Protección de Datos Personales, exercised by the Ministerio de Justicia y Derechos Humanos through the Dirección General de Transparencia, Acceso a la Información Pública y Protección de Datos Personales (DGTAIPD) [Ley Art 32; DS Art III.9]. The authority holds sanctioning and coercive power [Ley Art 32] and administers the Registro Nacional de Protección de Datos Personales [Ley Art 33.3].
- **Territorial and extraterritorial scope**: the Law applies to processing in national territory [Ley Art 3], and the 2024 regulation extends application to controllers not established in Peru who use means located there, including offering goods or services to, or monitoring the behaviour/profiling of, persons located in Peru [DS Art VI]; such controllers must designate a representative in or for Peru as the contact point with the authority [DS Art VII].
- Public-sector processing for national defence, public security, and criminal investigation is excluded to the extent the processing is necessary for the strict fulfilment of the powers assigned to the public body by law [Ley Art 3.2; DS Art V]; purely domestic/family-life data banks are excluded [Ley Art 3.1].

## Principles, lawful basis, and consent

- **Guiding principles (Título I)**: legality, consent, purpose, proportionality, quality, security, availability of recourse, and adequate level of protection [Ley Arts 4-11]; the list is non-exhaustive [Ley Art 12]. The 2024 regulation adds transparency and proactive responsibility (accountability), the latter requiring the controller to be able to demonstrate compliance [DS Art IX].
- **Consent is the primary lawful basis** [Ley Art 5]. It must be prior, informed, express, and unequivocal [Ley Art 13.5], and additionally free [DS Arts 2-6]; the burden of proving consent lies on the controller [DS Art 9], and consent may be revoked at any time without retroactive effect [DS Art 10].
- **Exceptions to consent** are enumerated in Ley Art 14 (13 cases), including publicly accessible sources, credit/solvency data, contractual necessity, health in risk situations, anonymized/dissociated data, AML/CFT, and the legitimate interests of the data subject. (Article 14.9 concerns the legitimate interests of the data subject, not a controller legitimate-interest basis; the regime has no GDPR Article 6(1)(f) equivalent.)
- **Sensitive data** [defined at Ley Art 2.5, expanded at DS Art III.6 to include genetic, biometric, neuronal, and moral/emotional data] require written consent, or a legal authorization on important public-interest grounds [Ley Art 13.6; DS Art 8].
- **Information duty**: prior, detailed information on purposes, recipients, the data bank, controller/processor identity and domicile, transfers, retention, automated decisions/profiling, and how to exercise rights [Ley Art 18; DS Art 6]; online collection may be met by an accessible privacy policy, which does not exempt obtaining consent [Ley Art 18; DS Art 7].

## Data-subject rights

Data subjects hold the rights of information [Ley Art 18], access [Ley Art 19], updating/inclusion/rectification/deletion [Ley Art 20], preventing supply [Ley Art 21], opposition [Ley Art 22], objective treatment (not being subject to a decision based solely on automated evaluation, subject to the contractual exception and the exception for evaluation for entry into a public entity) [Ley Art 23], and indemnification [Ley Art 25]. The 2024 regulation adds data portability [DS Art 76; effective six months after the regulation entered into force] and de-indexation as a manifestation of opposition for data processed on the internet [DS Arts III.7, 86.5], and elaborates the automated-decision right [DS Art 87].

Response deadlines under the 2024 regulation: eight days for the information right, twenty days for access, and ten days for rectification, cancellation, or opposition, each counted from the day after the request; extendable once by an equal period except the information right [DS Arts 69, 71]. Exercise of these rights against private data banks is free [DS Art 67.3]. Denial or an unsatisfactory response allows the data subject to seek recourse before the authority (procedimiento trilateral de tutela) or the Judiciary via hábeas data [Ley Art 24; DS Arts 88-94].

## Cross-border transfers

Peru operates an adequacy-plus-safeguards regime. A cross-border flow (flujo transfronterizo) requires that the destination country maintain an adequate level of protection; where it does not, the exporter must provide adequate guarantees so that processing conforms to the Law [Ley Arts 11, 15; DS Arts 18-20]. The DGTAIPD determines adequacy by resolution against enumerated criteria and issues model contractual clauses as a safeguard instrument [DS Arts 19-20]. Statutory exceptions to the guarantee requirement include international treaties, judicial and intelligence cooperation, contractual necessity, banking/stock transfers, medical purposes, and the data subject's prior informed express consent [Ley Art 15]. In all cases the cross-border flow must be communicated to the authority and inscribed in the Registro Nacional de Protección de Datos Personales [DS Art 21.2]. Organizations apply the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure with the stricter-of-standards approach.

## Database registration and accountability

Peru requires registration of data banks (a key difference from regimes with no such duty). Private-sector and public entities that create, modify, or cancel personal-data banks must register those acts in the Registro Nacional de Protección de Datos Personales [Ley Art 34; DS Art 42.1]; registration is free and follows an automatic-approval procedure [DS Arts 43.3, 45]. Failure to register is a minor infraction, and grave where it persists after the authority so requires [DS Arts 132.4, 133.13].

A Personal Data Officer (Oficial de Datos Personales) must be designated by public entities, and by controllers or processors where they handle a large volume of data, where processing may affect a large number of persons or involves sensitive data, where processing may cause evident harm to other rights or freedoms, or where their core activities involve sensitive-data processing; the officer's contact is published and communicated to the authority within fifteen days [DS Art 37], with progressive entry into force by enterprise size [DS Primera DCF]. A data-protection impact assessment is available on a facultative (optional, recommended) basis, especially for sensitive data, profiling, vulnerable persons, or large-volume processing [DS Art 40].

## Security and breach

- **Security duty**: the controller must adopt technical, organizational, and legal measures preventing alteration, loss, and unauthorized processing or access; processing in non-conforming data banks is prohibited [Ley Arts 9, 16]. The 2024 regulation prescribes detailed measures, including a Documento de Seguridad (which may reference NTP-ISO/IEC 27001), access controls with periodic privilege review, interaction logs retained a minimum of two years, and backup and physical-security controls [DS Arts 46-58]. Confidentiality persists after the relationship ends [Ley Art 17].
- **Breach notification** (introduced by the 2024 regulation): a security incident is notified to the authority within 48 hours of becoming aware where it exposes large volumes of data, affects a large number of persons, involves sensitive data, or causes evident harm to other rights or freedoms; affected data subjects are separately notified within 48 hours where the incident affects them in their other rights, unless that effect did not occur and the incident was fully overcome; digital-environment incidents are also notified to the Centro Nacional de Seguridad Digital [DS Art 34]. The controller documents every incident [DS Art 35], and a processor must inform the controller immediately [DS Art 36].

## Enforcement and penalties

- Infractions are classified as leves, graves, and muy graves [Ley Art 38], typified at DS Arts 132-134; liability is objective [Ley Art 38].
- **Administrative fines** [Ley Art 39], expressed in Unidades Impositivas Tributarias (UIT): minor 0.5 to 5 UIT; serious more than 5 to 50 UIT; very serious more than 50 to 100 UIT. In no case may a fine exceed 10% of the infractor's prior-year gross annual income.
- **Coercive fines** up to 10 UIT [Ley Art 40], graduated by the 2024 regulation (minor up to 2 UIT, serious 2 to 6 UIT, very serious 6 to 10 UIT, doubling up to a 100 UIT cap) [DS Art 130]; a 40% prompt-payment reduction applies where the fine is paid in the first-instance window without appeal [DS Art 127].
- Remedies include the reclamación/tutela procedure and hábeas data [Ley Arts 24, 37], corrective measures [Ley Art 38; DS Art 122] and precautionary measures [Ley Art 33.16; DS Art 121], and indemnification [Ley Art 25].

Fine amounts are expressed in UIT because the UIT is set annually; an adopter converts to the current sol value using the UIT in force for the relevant year.

## Employment and workforce monitoring

Where the organization monitors the network and device activity of workers in Peru, the processing is governed by Ley 29733 and DS 016-2024-JUS and, operationally, by the Workforce Network Monitoring Policy and its supporting suite. The framework has no employment-specific regime; workforce monitoring is subject to the general rules.

- **Private-communications limit**: private communications, telecommunications, and IT systems may be opened, seized, intercepted, or accessed only by a motivated judicial order or with the holder's authorization, and data obtained in violation have no legal effect [Ley Art 13.4]. This governs interception-type monitoring directly.
- **Lawful footing**: consent is the primary basis [Ley Arts 5, 13.5], but the regulation treats consent conditioned on a service or benefit as not free where the data are not indispensable [DS Art 3.2], so where employment consent is not a sound footing the operative basis is a statutory authorization or an applicable Article 14 exception, not "general principles" and not a controller legitimate-interest basis (Article 14.9 concerns the data subject's legitimate interest). The Legitimate Interest Assessment for Employment Monitoring Annex is used only as an analytic aid, adapted to Ley 29733's consent-and-authorization scheme rather than to GDPR Article 6(1)(f).
- **Sensitive data**: monitoring that would capture sensitive data (expansively defined at DS Art III.6) requires written consent or a legal authorization [Ley Art 13.6].
- **Automated decisions and profiling**: workers retain the objective-treatment right against decisions based solely on automated processing that produce legal or significant effects, subject to the Article 23 exceptions [Ley Art 23; DS Art 87].
- Purpose limitation, quality, proportionality, and security apply [Ley Arts 6, 7, 8, 9, 16]. Consultation, telemetry, and presence signals are handled through the Works Council and Employee Representative Consultation Procedure, the Network Telemetry and DPI Controls Standard, and the Presence Inference Limitations Standard, with scope stated to workers mirroring the parent policy's boundaries; the Employee Monitoring Notice Template provides content adapted to the Article 18 / DS Art 6 information duty.

## Limitations

- This annex is a consolidating per-regime view of Ley 29733 and DS 016-2024-JUS, not a substitute for the enacted law, its regulation, or legal advice; the controlling texts are Ley 29733 (as amended) and DS 016-2024-JUS (in force from 120 calendar days after its 30 November 2024 publication).
- **Currency.** The 2024 regulation is recent and replaced the 2013 regulation; several duties phase in progressively (the Personal Data Officer requirement by enterprise size; portability six months after entry into force). An adopter confirms the current authority designation, the in-force regulation, and any complementary directives against the primary source before relying on a specific procedural detail.
- **Corpus cross-references.** On any divergence for Peru private-sector processing between this annex and another corpus document, this annex governs the per-regime framing; the Latin America privacy annex provides the regional overview.
- Public-sector processing excepted under Ley Art 3.2 / DS Art V, and sectoral rules, are out of scope for this private-party annex.
