## Purpose

This annex defines privacy and AI regulatory requirements applicable to the processing of personal data in South Korea under the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA). It supplements the Privacy and Data Governance Policy and the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure.

---

## Applicable laws and regulatory authorities

- **Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA)**: South Korea's primary data protection law. Most recently amended by Act No. 21445 (promulgated 10 March 2026, in force 11 September 2026); the 2023 amendment (Act No. 19234, promulgated March 2023, key provisions effective September 2023) is the version held in English translation. A further amendment, Act No. 21910 (in force 9 March 2027), is pending.
- South Korea holds an EU GDPR adequacy decision (Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/254, granted December 2021); that decision is not held in the reference base, so an adopter confirms its current scope and status directly. <!-- ref-absence: Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/254 | EU adequacy decision for the Republic of Korea -->
- **Key 2023 amendments:** Right to explanation for automated decisions; right to data portability; data-breach notification to data subjects without delay (Article 34(1)) and reporting without delay to the Protection Commission (PIPC) or to a specialized institution that Presidential Decree designates, as Presidential Decree provides, taking account of the kinds of personal information involved, how the loss, theft, or divulgence came about and how large it is, and other factors (Article 34(3)), both paraphrased below, with the timing, methods, and procedures for reporting delegated to Presidential Decree (Article 34(4)); enhanced penalty regime; mobile application and online service obligations.
- **Breach-reporting deadline:** a 72-hour reporting period is reported to be set by the PIPA Enforcement Decree; that decree is not held in the reference base, so an adopter confirms the current deadline directly. <!-- ref-absence: PIPA Enforcement Decree | Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act -->
- **Regulatory authority:** Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC).

Article 34(1), introductory text, and Article 34(3), first sentence, paraphrased with pinpoint citations from the held KLRI English translation (Act No. 19234), which is not a government publication and whose publisher's terms are not held, so it is not quoted; these provisions were not compared against Act No. 21445 (in force since 11 September 2026), so an adopter confirms the current text directly:

- **Article 34(1), introductory text (notification):** once a personal information controller learns that personal information has been lost, stolen, or divulged, it must tell the data subjects the matters that paragraph (1) lists, without delay; where it does not know a data subject's contact details, or there is other good cause, it may instead take an alternative measure that Presidential Decree prescribes.
- **Article 34(3), first sentence (reporting):** once aware of the loss, theft, or divulgence, the controller must also report the paragraph (1) matters without delay to the Protection Commission or to a specialized institution that Presidential Decree designates, as Presidential Decree provides, taking account of the kinds of personal information involved, how the loss, theft, or divulgence came about and how large it is, and other factors.

## Core PIPA obligations and data-subject rights

The Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) imposes the following core obligations on personal information controllers and grants the enumerated data-subject rights. Each is mapped to the library control that carries it, or flagged as a Korea-specific gap.

| PIPA obligation / right (held article) | Library disposition |
| --- | --- |
| **Protection principles (Art 3)**: the controller specifies processing purposes explicitly, collects personal information by lawful and fair means and only to the minimum extent those purposes need, and keeps personal information accurate, complete, and up to date. | library data-governance and data-quality controls |
| **Data-subject rights (Art 4)**: the data subject has the rights to be informed of processing, to decide whether and how far to consent, to confirm processing and access the information, to suspend, correct, erase, or destroy it, and to appropriate redress. | library data-subject-rights controls (`privacy/procedure-data-subject-rights-management.md`) |
| **Lawful bases for collection and use (Art 15)**: the controller may collect and use personal information only on one of the Art 15(1) bases (consent, statutory obligation, contract performance, and the other enumerated grounds). | library lawful-basis and consent controls |
| **Collection minimization (Art 16)**: only the minimum personal information necessary for the purpose is collected, and the controller bears the burden of proof. | library data-minimization controls |
| **Provision to third parties (Art 17) and out-of-purpose limitation (Art 18)**: provision to a third party requires consent or an Art 17 basis, and the controller must not use or provide personal information beyond the Art 15/17 scope except in the Art 18 cases. | library third-party-sharing and purpose-limitation controls |
| **Destruction when the purpose is achieved (Art 21)**: the controller must destroy personal information without delay once it is no longer necessary, unless retention is required by another statute. | library data-retention and destruction controls |
| **Consent mechanics (Art 22)**: each matter requiring consent is presented distinctly and in a clearly recognizable manner, with consent obtained separately. | library consent-management controls |
| **Sensitive information (Art 23)**: processing of sensitive information (ideology, beliefs, union or political-party membership, political opinions, health, sex life, and the prescribed categories) is prohibited absent separate consent or a statutory basis, with additional safeguards. | library special-category-data controls |
| **Unique identifiers (Art 24) and resident registration numbers (Art 24-2)**: the controller must not process the identifiers prescribed by Presidential Decree except after the Art 15(2)/17(2) notification and separate consent (given apart from consent to other processing), or where a statute specifically requires or permits it; a resident registration number must not be processed except in the narrow Art 24-2 cases (which require encryption and an alternative non-RRN sign-up route). | library identifier-handling controls; the RRN rule is Korea-specific and not carried by a dedicated control |
| **Duty of safeguards (Art 29)**: the controller takes the technical, managerial, and physical measures necessary to secure personal information. | library information-security controls (`security/`) |
| **Privacy policy (Art 30)**: the controller establishes and discloses a privacy policy covering the Art 30(1) enumerated matters. | library privacy-notice controls (`privacy/template-privacy-notice.md`; adopter maps the Korea-specific policy-content items) |
| **Privacy officer / CPO (Art 31)**: the controller designates a privacy officer responsible for personal-information processing. | library accountability and privacy-officer controls |
| **Data-breach notification (Art 34)**: once aware that personal information has been lost, stolen, or divulged, the controller tells the affected data subjects the Art 34(1) matters without delay, and also reports without delay to the Protection Commission (PIPC) or to a specialized institution that Presidential Decree designates, as Presidential Decree (the PIPA Enforcement Decree, not held) provides, taking account of the kinds of personal information involved, how the loss, theft, or divulgence came about and how large it is, and other factors (Art 34(3)); for the status of that decree and the reporting period, see the Breach-reporting deadline line above. | library incident-response and breach-notification controls |
| **Access, correction/erasure, suspension (Arts 35, 36, 37)**: a data subject may request access (Art 35), correction or erasure of accessed information (Art 36), and suspension of processing (Art 37), subject to the statutory exceptions. | library data-subject-rights controls (`privacy/procedure-data-subject-rights-management.md`, `privacy/template-dsar-workflow.md`) |
| **Compensation and statutory damages (Arts 39, 39-2)**: a data subject suffering damage from a violation may claim compensation unless the controller proves no intent or negligence (Art 39(1)); where the controller's intent or negligence causes a data subject's own personal information to be stolen, lost, divulged, altered, forged, or damaged and the data subject suffers damage as a result, the court may set compensation at no more than five times that damage, except where the controller proves that it acted with neither intent nor negligence (Art 39(3)); for those same compromise events the data subject may instead elect statutory damages up to the Art 39-2 cap. | *(Korea-specific liability; the library's disclosure-accuracy and security controls reduce the exposure)* |

## AI and privacy obligations

- **Right to explanation:** Data subjects may request an explanation of any decision made solely through automated means that significantly affects their rights or interests. The data controller must explain the criteria and logic applied and provide human review upon request.
- **Purpose limitation and data minimization:** Apply to AI training on personal data. Consent must be specific to the AI processing purpose.
- **High-risk processing:** PIPC guidance addresses higher-risk processing such as CCTV, biometric, and credit-assessment systems (proportionality and human oversight); that guidance is not held in the reference base, so an adopter confirms the current PIPC requirements directly. <!-- ref-absence: PIPC high-risk processing guidance | PIPC CCTV -->
- **Employment and profiling:** PIPC guidance is reported to address AI use in employment screening and credit decisions (proportionality and human oversight); that guidance is not held in the reference base, so an adopter confirms the current PIPC position directly. <!-- ref-absence: PIPC employment screening guidance | PIPC profiling -->
- **AI Basic Act:** South Korea's AI statute is covered in [`ai/jurisdictions/annex-ai-south-korea.md`](../../ai/jurisdictions/annex-ai-south-korea.md); among its basic principles, the held English translation of the AI Basic Act provides that affected persons are to be "provided with a clear and meaningful explanation of the key criteria and principles, used to derive the final AI outcomes, to the extent technically and reasonably possible" (Article 3(2)). In the held translation Article 3 is headed "Basic Principles and Obligations of the Nation"; the operator-level explanation duty is a separate article, Article 34(1)2, under which AI business operators providing high-impact AI or AI-based products and services establish and implement, as prescribed by Presidential Decree and to the extent technically feasible, a plan to provide explanations for AI-generated outputs, including the key criteria used to derive such outputs and an overview of the learning data used in the development and utilization of AI.

## Cross-border transfer mechanisms

Article 28-8, paraphrased with pinpoint citations from the held KLRI English translation (Act No. 19234), which is not quoted for the reason given above, with no substantive difference found against Article 28-8 of the consolidated Act No. 21445 in force since 11 September 2026 (law.go.kr, checked 3 October 2026):

- **Article 28-8(1) (transfer grounds):** a personal information controller must not transfer personal information across borders (provision, including access by inquiry, entrusted processing, or storage) unless one of these grounds applies:
  1. the data subject has given separate consent;
  2. a treaty that the Republic of Korea is party to, another international convention, or a statute makes special provision for the transfer;
  3. entrusting the processing and storage of the information is needed for entering into and carrying out a contract with the data subject, and either (a) the paragraph (2) matters are disclosed in the Article 30 privacy policy or (b) they are communicated to the data subject by a means that Presidential Decree prescribes, such as email;
  4. the recipient holds a certification that the Protection Commission determines and publicly notifies (such as the Article 32-2 personal information protection certification) and takes every measure that subparagraph 4 lists: under item (a), both the safety measures needed to protect the personal information and the measures needed to guarantee data subjects' rights; and under item (b), the measures needed to implement the certified matters in the destination country;
  5. the Protection Commission recognizes that the destination country's or international organization's protection system, scope of guarantee of data-subject rights, and damage-relief procedures are substantially equivalent to the level of protection under the Act.
- **Article 28-8(2) (advance information for consent under paragraph (1)1):** before obtaining that consent, the controller must tell the data subject what personal information is to be transferred; the destination country and the date and method of transfer; the recipient's name (for a corporation, its name and contact information); the recipient's purpose of use and the period for which it retains and uses the information; and how the data subject can refuse the transfer, by what procedure, and with what effect.
- **Article 28-8(3) (changes):** to change any paragraph (2) matter, the controller must inform the data subject of the change and obtain consent to it.
- **Article 28-8(4) (other provisions and protective measures):** a controller transferring under the paragraph (1) proviso must comply with the Act's other provisions on cross-border transfer, including Articles 17 to 19 and Chapter V, and take the protective measures that Presidential Decree prescribes.
- **Article 28-8(5) (contracts):** a controller must not enter into a cross-border transfer contract on terms that violate the Act.
- **Article 28-8(6) (further criteria and procedures):** Presidential Decree prescribes the other criteria and procedures for cross-border transfer that paragraphs (1) to (5) do not cover.

**Pending amendment (Act No. 21910, promulgated 8 September 2026, in force 9 March 2027):** new Article 28-15 allows the PIPC, after deliberation and resolution, to disapply Article 28-8 to the extent of overseas outsourced processing (entrusted processing abroad) where personal information is used under the Article 28-12 special case for artificial intelligence development. The Article 28-8 transfer grounds above otherwise continue to apply.

## Enforcement and fines

Figures below are taken, and provisions paraphrased, from the held KLRI English translation (Act No. 19234), which is not quoted for the reason given above; these provisions were not compared against Act No. 21445 (in force since 11 September 2026), so an adopter confirms the current text directly.

- **Penalty surcharges (Article 64-2):** the PIPC may levy on the controller a penalty surcharge of no more than 3/100 of total sales (Article 64-2(1)), worked out from gross sales after deducting the sales that have no connection with the violation (Article 64-2(2)); in cases that Presidential Decree prescribes where the controller has made no sales or its sales cannot practicably be calculated, the ceiling is two billion won instead (Article 64-2(1) proviso). Any of nine events can lead to a surcharge (Article 64-2(1)1 to 9): processing contrary to Articles 15(1), 17(1), 18(1), 18(2), or 19; processing the personal information of a child younger than 14 without the consent of the child's legal representative, contrary to Article 22-2(1); processing sensitive information without the data subject's consent, contrary to Article 23(1)1; processing personally identifiable information or resident registration numbers contrary to Articles 24(1) and 24-2(1); a failure by the controller to carry out the education, management, or supervision that Article 26(4) requires, as a result of which the person entrusted breaches the Act; processing information with the aim of identifying a particular individual, contrary to Article 28-5(1); transferring personal information across borders contrary to Article 28-8(1); not complying with an order under Article 28-9(1) to suspend a cross-border transfer; and personal information that the controller processes being stolen, lost, divulged, altered, forged, or damaged, unless the controller had taken all the measures needed under Article 29 to keep the information safe from such events. If the controller, without good cause, refuses to submit the data for calculating its sales or submits false data, the PIPC may base the sales figure on the controller's gross sales (Article 64-2(3)), or may instead estimate the sales by reference to product and service prices, financial statements and other accounting data, the amount of personal information held, and other business data of a controller comparable in size (Article 64-2(3) proviso). The PIPC may decline to impose a surcharge in four cases: the person liable, judged objectively, cannot pay it because of capital impairment, a suspension of payments, insolvency, or a similar cause (Article 64-2(5)1); the person had good cause to believe, wrongly, that the conduct in question was not unlawful (Article 64-2(5)2); the violation is minor in substance and degree, or the surcharge as assessed is small (Article 64-2(5)3); or a ground that Presidential Decree sets applies, under which the data subject has suffered little or no damage (Article 64-2(5)4). Presidential Decree sets the detailed criteria and procedures for the calculation (Article 64-2(6)); that decree is not held in the reference base, so an adopter confirms the current calculation criteria directly. <!-- ref-absence: PIPA Enforcement Decree | Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act -->
- **Criminal penalties (Articles 70 to 73):** the highest tier, in Article 70, is imprisonment with labour for up to 10 years or a fine of up to 100 million won. It reaches a person who, to disrupt a public institution's processing of personal information, alters or erases personal information that the institution processes and so causes the suspension or paralysis of, or other severe hardship to, the institution's work; and a person who obtains, through fraud or other improper means, personal information that third parties process and passes it on to a third party for profit or for an unjust purpose, together with anyone who abets or arranges that conduct. Article 71 sets imprisonment with labour for up to five years or a fine of up to 50 million won for violations including giving a third party personal information without the data subject's consent, contrary to Article 17(1)1, where Article 17(1)2 does not apply, together with anyone who knowingly receives that information (Article 71, subparagraph 1), out-of-purpose use or provision (Article 18(1) and (2)), and processing sensitive information (Article 23(1)) or personally identifiable information (Article 24(1)) in violation of the Act. For lower-tier offences, Article 72 provides imprisonment with labour for up to three years or a fine of up to 30 million won, and Article 73(1) provides imprisonment with labour for up to two years or a fine of up to 20 million won. Under the joint penalty provisions of Article 74, where a corporation's representative, or an agent, an employee, or anyone else employed by a corporation or individual, commits an offence under Articles 70 to 73 in connection with that corporation's or individual's business, the corporation or individual is punished as well as the offender: by a fine of up to 70 million won for an Article 70 offence (Article 74(1)), and by the fine that the relevant Article sets for an Article 71 to 73 offence (Article 74(2)); but the corporation or individual is not punished if it was not negligent in exercising due care and supervision over the affairs concerned to prevent the offence (Article 74(1) and (2) provisos).
- **Administrative fines (Article 75):** Article 75 sets administrative fines in four tiers, not exceeding 50 million won, 30 million won, 20 million won, and 10 million won (Article 75(1) to (4)).

Article 26(8) applies Articles 34 and 64-2, among others, mutatis mutandis to the person entrusted with processing, and each Article 64-2(1) trigger other than subparagraph 5 (the entrusting controller's own Article 26(4) management, supervision, or education failure) states that it includes application mutatis mutandis under Article 26(8), with subparagraphs 7 and 8 also extending through Article 28-11:

- **Article 26(8):** Articles 15 to 18, 21, 22, 22-2, 23, 24, 24-2, 25, 25-2, 27, 28, 28-2 to 28-5, 28-7 to 28-11, 29, 30, 30-2, 31, 33, 34, 34-2, 35, 35-2, 36, 37, 37-2, 38, 59, 63, 63-2, and 64-2 apply mutatis mutandis to a person entrusted with processing, with each reference in them to the personal information controller read as a reference to the person entrusted.
