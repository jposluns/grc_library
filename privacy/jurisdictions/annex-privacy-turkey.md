## Purpose

This annex defines privacy and AI regulatory requirements applicable to the processing of personal data in Turkey under the Law on the Protection of Personal Data (KVKK). It supplements the Privacy and Data Governance Policy and the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure.

---

## Applicable laws and regulatory authorities

- **Law on the Protection of Personal Data (KVKK) No. 6698**: Effective April 2016; substantially amended by Law No. 7499 (enacted March 2024, in force June 2024), harmonizing the KVKK with GDPR in several respects.
- **Key 2024 amendments:** Law No. 7499 revised the processing conditions for special-category personal data (Article 6) and the cross-border transfer regime (Article 9). It amended the administrative-fine provisions in Article 18, including new Article 18(1)(d) for failure to notify a standard contract under Article 9(5). Under Provisional Article 3, the former Article 9(1) continued to apply alongside amended Article 9 until 1 September 2024. Under the same provision, applications pending before criminal judgeships of peace as of 1 June 2024 must continue to be heard by those judgeships.
- **Regulatory authority:** Personal Data Protection Authority (KVKK Board / Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurumu).

## AI and privacy obligations

- The KVKK has published recommendations on personal-data protection addressing transparency in automated processing that significantly affects individuals; the recommendations are not held in the reference base and are guidance rather than binding law, so an adopter confirms the current KVKK position directly. <!-- ref-absence: KVKK recommendations | KVKK artificial intelligence -->
- Data subjects have the right to object to decisions made solely through automated means.
- Turkey does not have standalone AI legislation as of 2025; KVKK principles and guidance apply to AI processing of personal data.

## Cross-border transfer mechanisms

Article 9 of Law No. 6698, as amended by Article 34 of Law No. 7499 in 2024, provides the following transfer routes:

- **Adequacy:** A transfer must satisfy an applicable processing condition under Articles 5 or 6 and be covered by a Board adequacy decision for the destination country, a sector within that country, or an international organization (Article 9(1)). The Board must publish its adequacy decisions in the Official Gazette and must consult the relevant institutions and organizations when it considers this necessary. The Board must review adequacy decisions at least every four years. Following a review or whenever else it considers necessary, the Board may amend, suspend or revoke an adequacy decision with prospective effect (Article 9(2)). When deciding adequacy, the Board must primarily consider: reciprocity with Turkey on personal-data transfers; the destination's legislation and practice, or the rules an international organization is subject to; an independent and effective data-protection authority and administrative and judicial remedies; participation in international data-protection conventions or membership of international organizations; membership of global or regional organizations to which Turkey belongs; and international conventions to which Turkey is a party (Article 9(3)).
- **Appropriate safeguards:** Where no adequacy decision exists, a transfer must satisfy an applicable processing condition under Articles 5 or 6, and the data subject must be able to exercise rights and seek effective legal remedies in the destination country. The parties must provide one of the safeguards in Article 9(4): an agreement that is not an international convention, between public bodies or international organizations abroad and public bodies or professional organizations with public institution status in Turkey, with Board permission for the transfer (a); Board-approved binding corporate rules containing personal-data protection provisions with which companies within a group engaged in joint economic activities must comply (b); a standard contract published by the Board (c); or a written undertaking containing provisions providing adequate protection, with Board permission for the transfer (ç). The Board's standard contract covers matters such as data categories, transfer purposes, recipients and recipient groups, the technical and administrative measures the recipient will take, and the additional measures taken for special-category personal data (Article 9(4)(c)).
- **Standard-contract notification:** The controller or processor must notify the Personal Data Protection Authority of the standard contract within five business days of signing (Article 9(5)). This notification requirement is distinct from the Board permission required for a transfer using a written undertaking under Article 9(4)(ç).
- **Incidental-transfer derogations:** To use Article 9(6), no adequacy decision must exist, none of the Article 9(4) safeguards can be provided, and the transfer must be incidental and satisfy one of the following conditions:
  - **(a)** The data subject must give explicit consent to the transfer after being informed of the potential risks.
  - **(b)** The transfer must be necessary to perform a contract between the data subject and the controller or to implement pre-contractual measures requested by the data subject.
  - **(c)** The transfer must be necessary to conclude or perform a contract between the controller and another natural or legal person in the data subject's interest.
  - **(ç)** The transfer must be necessary for an overriding public interest.
  - **(d)** The transfer must be necessary to establish, exercise or protect a right.
  - **(e)** The transfer must be necessary to protect the life or bodily integrity of a person unable to express consent because of factual impossibility or whose consent lacks legal validity, or of another person.
  - **(f)** The transfer must be from a register open to the public or to persons with a legitimate interest, the access conditions in the applicable legislation must be met, and a person with a legitimate interest must request the transfer.

Article 9(7) excludes Article 9(6)(a), (b) and (c) from public-law activities of public institutions and organizations; Article 9(6)(ç), (d), (e) and (f) remain available for those activities, subject to their conditions and the Article 9(6) prerequisites.

Controllers and processors must ensure that the safeguards under Law No. 6698 and the requirements of Article 9 also apply to onward transfers and transfers to international organizations (Article 9(8)).

Under every transfer route, where a transfer would seriously harm Turkey's interests or the data subject's interests, the transfer must receive Board permission after the Board obtains the opinion of the relevant public institution or organization, subject to the provisions of international agreements (Article 9(9)).

Provisions of other laws governing transfers of personal data abroad remain applicable (Article 9(10)).

## Enforcement and fines

Administrative fines are recalibrated annually for inflation. As of 2025:

- Up to approximately TRY 7.9 million for failure to implement adequate security measures.
- Up to approximately TRY 22.7 million for unlawful international transfer.
- Amounts are published annually by the KVKK Board.
- Criminal penalties: imprisonment from 1 to 4.5 years for unlawful processing of special categories of personal data.
