## Purpose

This annex defines privacy and AI regulatory requirements applicable to the processing of personal data in Turkey under the Law on the Protection of Personal Data (KVKK). It supplements the Privacy and Data Governance Policy and the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure.

---

## Applicable laws and regulatory authorities

- **Law on the Protection of Personal Data (KVKK) No. 6698**: Effective April 2016; substantially amended by Law No. 7499 (enacted March 2024, in force June 2024), harmonizing the KVKK with GDPR in several respects.
- **Key 2024 amendments:** Explicit consent requirements aligned with GDPR standards; legitimate interest introduced as a lawful basis; revised Article 9 transfer framework distinguishing standard contracts published by the Board from written undertakings requiring Board permission for the transfer; enhanced rights including data portability; strengthened DPO-type obligations for large data processors.
- **Regulatory authority:** Personal Data Protection Authority (KVKK Board / Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurumu).

---

## AI and privacy obligations

- The KVKK has published recommendations on personal-data protection addressing transparency in automated processing that significantly affects individuals; the recommendations are not held in the reference base and are guidance rather than binding law, so an adopter confirms the current KVKK position directly. <!-- ref-absence: KVKK recommendations | KVKK artificial intelligence -->
- Data subjects have the right to object to decisions made solely through automated means.
- Turkey does not have standalone AI legislation as of 2025; KVKK principles and guidance apply to AI processing of personal data.

## Cross-border transfer mechanisms

Article 9 of Law No. 6698, as amended by Article 34 of Law No. 7499 in 2024, provides the following transfer routes:

- **Adequacy:** A transfer must satisfy an applicable processing condition under Articles 5 or 6 and be covered by a Board adequacy decision for the destination country, a sector within that country, or an international organization. The Board issues adequacy decisions and publishes them in the Official Gazette (Article 9(1) and (2)).
- **Appropriate safeguards:** Where no adequacy decision exists, a transfer must satisfy an applicable processing condition under Articles 5 or 6, and the data subject must be able to exercise rights and access effective legal remedies in the destination country. The parties must provide one of the safeguards in Article 9(4): an agreement that is not an international convention, between public bodies or international organizations abroad and public bodies or professional organizations with public institution status in Turkey, with Board permission for the transfer (a); Board-approved binding corporate rules for companies within a group engaged in joint economic activities (b); a standard contract published by the Board (c); or a written undertaking providing adequate protection, with Board permission for the transfer (ç).
- **Standard-contract notification:** The controller or processor must notify the Personal Data Protection Authority of the standard contract within five business days of signing (Article 9(5)). This notification requirement is distinct from the Board permission required for a transfer using a written undertaking under Article 9(4)(ç).
- **Incidental-transfer derogations:** To use Article 9(6), no adequacy decision must exist, none of the Article 9(4) safeguards can be provided, and the transfer must be incidental and satisfy a condition in Article 9(6). Under Article 9(6)(a), the data subject must give explicit consent to the transfer after being informed of the potential risks. Article 9(7) excludes this consent derogation, and the derogations in Article 9(6)(b) and (c), from public-law activities of public institutions and organizations.

Controllers and processors must ensure that the safeguards under Law No. 6698 and the requirements of Article 9 also apply to onward transfers and transfers to international organizations (Article 9(8)).

## Enforcement and fines

Administrative fines are recalibrated annually for inflation. As of 2025:

- Up to approximately TRY 7.9 million for failure to implement adequate security measures.
- Up to approximately TRY 22.7 million for unlawful international transfer.
- Amounts are published annually by the KVKK Board.
- Criminal penalties: imprisonment from 1 to 4.5 years for unlawful processing of special categories of personal data.
