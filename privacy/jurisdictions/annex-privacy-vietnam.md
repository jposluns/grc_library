## Purpose

This annex defines privacy requirements applicable to the processing of personal data in Vietnam under the Law on Personal Data Protection No. 91/2025/QH15 (PDPL) and Decree No. 356/2025/ND-CP, both in force from 2026-01-01, including the AI-related provisions those two instruments themselves contain. It supplements the Privacy and Data Governance Policy and the Privacy Impact and Cross-Border Transfer Procedure.

**Sourcing rule for this annex.** Every statement below rests on the held English text of the PDPL or of Decree 356/2025. Where a proposition is not in those two texts, it is either omitted or explicitly marked as not held and not relied upon.

## Applicable laws and regulatory authorities

- **Law on Personal Data Protection No. 91/2025/QH15 (PDPL)**: "passed by the 15th National Assembly of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam at its 9th session on June 26, 2025"; "This Law comes into force from January 1, 2026" (Art. 38(1)). The held text runs to 5 chapters and 39 articles. On the prior regime: PDPL Art. 39 preserves consents and dossiers made "as prescribed in Decree No. 13/2023/ND-CP dated April 17, 2023 of the Government before the effective date of this Law", and Decree 356 Art. 42(2) repeals that Decree.
  - *Not asserted:* the held texts do not describe the PDPL as Vietnam's first personal data protection statute, nor characterize the shift from decree to statute. That framing is dropped rather than sourced.
- **Decree No. 356/2025/ND-CP**: dated "Hanoi, December 31, 2025"; "This Decree comes into force as of January 1, 2026" (Art. 42(1)). Its title is "DECREE ELABORATING ON CERTAIN ARTICLES AND IMPLEMENTATION MEASURES OF LAW ON PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION", and Art. 1 enumerates the PDPL clauses it elaborates (including Clauses 2 and 3 Art. 2; Clause 5 Art. 9; Clause 7 Art. 20; Clause 7 Art. 21; Clause 4 Art. 22; Clause 5 Art. 23; Clause 6 Art. 30; Clause 3 Art. 33; Clause 4 Art. 38) plus implementation measures for the personal data protection authorities, the National Information Portal, and ministry/local responsibilities. Under Art. 42(2), Decree No. 13/2023/ND-CP ceases to have effect from the effective date of Decree 356, **Decree 13/2023 is repealed**.
  - *Not asserted:* the held texts do not state that Decree 356 is the sole or principal implementing instrument. It is described here only by its own title and Art. 1 scope.
- **Transitional continuity (PDPL Art. 39):** personal data processing activities already being carried out on the consent or agreement of the data subject given under Decree 13/2023 before 2026-01-01 continue without having to seek re-consent or re-agreement (Art. 39(1)); impact-assessment dossiers received by the agency in charge of personal data protection before the effective date of the PDPL continue to be used, but updating of those dossiers after that date must be implemented in accordance with the provisions of the PDPL (Art. 39(2)).
- **Data categories.** The PDPL itself does not enumerate the categories: Art. 2(2) defines basic personal data as data "included in the list issued by the Government" and Art. 2(3) defines sensitive personal data as data "listed by the Government." The enumerations are in Decree 356/2025:
  - **Basic personal data (Decree 356 Art. 3)**, 11 items, including name, date of birth, gender, places of birth/residence, **nationality (Art. 3(5): "Nationality")**, images of the individual, phone/identification/passport/driver's-licence/vehicle-registration-plate numbers, marital status, family relationships, digital accounts, and a residual catch-all for "Other information associated with a specific individual or capable of identifying a specific individual, other than the information prescribed in Article 4 of this Decree" (Art. 3(11)).
  - **Sensitive personal data (Decree 356 Art. 4(1))**, racial or ethnic origin; opinions on politics, religion and belief; private life and personal/family secrets; health status; biometric data and genetic characteristics; sexual life or sexual orientation; crime and law-violation data "collected and stored by law enforcement agencies"; "Location data of individuals determined through positioning services"; electronic-identification account login names and passwords and "images of ID cards, citizen ID cards, or 9-digit ID cards"; bank account credentials, bank card data, transaction history and financial, credit, securities and insurance information; "Data monitoring behavior and activities related to the use of telecommunications services, social networks, online communication services, and other services in cyberspace"; and other data that law requires to be kept confidential.
  - **Nationality is BASIC data, not sensitive**, Decree 356 Art. 3(5) lists "Nationality" among basic personal data, and it does not appear in the Art. 4(1) sensitive list. Any control design that treated Vietnamese nationality data as a sensitive category requires re-baselining. (The held texts use the term "nationality"; "citizenship" is not used in this sense and is not adopted here.)
  - In the course of processing sensitive personal data, Decree 356 Art. 4(2) requires agencies and organizations to establish regulations on access authorization and restriction, processing procedures, and confidentiality measures.
- **Lawful basis.** PDPL Art. 11(1): "Personal data collected must have the consent of the personal data subject before collection, unless otherwise provided by law." Art. 9 governs the validity, form and principles of consent (voluntariness, informedness, "Express consent to each purpose" (Art. 9(4)(a)), and the rule at Art. 9(4)(d) that silence or non-response is not deemed to constitute consent). PDPL Art. 19(1) sets out the cases of "Processing of personal data without the consent of the personal data subject", and Art. 19(2) requires a monitoring mechanism where consent is not required.
  - *Not asserted:* the held texts do not rank lawful bases or describe consent as the "primary" basis. The operative held position is the Art. 11(1) default (consent before collection) subject to "unless otherwise provided by law" and to Art. 19.
- **Notification of violations.** PDPL Art. 23(1) requires notification to the competent personal data protection authority "no later than 72 hours from the time of discovery of the violation", the duty is triggered by a violation "that may harm national defense, national security, social order and safety, or infringe upon the life, health, honor, dignity, or property of the personal data subject", rather than by any personal data breach. "In case the personal data processor discovers a violation, it must promptly notify the personal data controller or the personal data controller and processor." Decree 356 Art. 28(1) prescribes the notification content, and Art. 28(2) requires submission "to the personal data protection authority or via the National Information Portal for Personal Data Protection using Form No. 08".
- **Location and biometric data violations (Decree 356 Art. 29).** On "a personal data violation incident involving location data or biometric data", the controlling party or processing-and-controlling party must "Notify the affected personal data subject matter within no more than 72 hours from the time the violation is detected" (Art. 29(1)(a)), report to the competent state authority under Art. 28 (Art. 29(1)(b)), and "retain violation dossiers for a minimum period of **5 years from the date the incident is fully remedied**" (Art. 29(1)(c)), the retention clock starts on full remediation, not on detection. Art. 29(2) lists the minimum contents of the subject notification; Art. 29(3) applies where all affected subjects cannot be notified within the Clause-1 time limit for technical or emergency reasons; the party must then do BOTH (a) "Make a public notification via the organization's official electronic means, including its website or application" and (b) "Send notifications to the relevant personal data subject matters immediately once technical conditions permit", a mandatory two-part duty, not an optional public-notice permission. Art. 29(1) and the retention duty at Art. 29(1)(c) are unaffected.
- **Regulatory authority.**
  - PDPL Art. 36(2): "The Ministry of Public Security is the focal agency responsible to the Government for implementing state management of personal data protection, **except for content under the management scope of the Ministry of National Defense**." PDPL Art. 36(3): the Ministry of National Defense "is responsible to the Government for implementing state management of personal data protection within its scope of management." The MPS focal role is therefore carved out, not universal.
  - Operative authority: PDPL Art. 33(1)(a), "The agency responsible for personal data protection under the Ministry of Public Security"; Decree 356 Art. 39(1), "Personal data protection authorities are units under the Ministry of Public Security of Vietnam, responsible for assisting the Minister of Public Security of Vietnam in performing the state management function concerning personal data protection."
  - Decree 356 allocates responsibilities article by article, with these headers: Art. 34 "Responsibilities of Ministry of Public Security of Vietnam"; Art. 35 "**Responsibilities of Ministry of National Defense of Vietnam**"; Art. 36 "Responsibilities of Ministry of Science and Technology of Vietnam"; Art. 37 "Responsibilities of ministries, ministerial agencies, and governmental agencies"; Art. 38 "Responsibilities of People's Committees of provinces". Art. 38 is a provincial-authority provision, not a sector-ministry provision.
  - **Filing channels, the Portal is not a general dossier-filing channel.** Decree 356 Art. 39(2) describes the National Information Portal for Personal Data Protection as "a platform for providing information to disseminate the CPV's guidelines and policies and the State's personal data protection laws; supporting guidance, enhancing awareness, and improving personal data protection skills…; receiving and handling feedback and petitions from relevant agencies, organizations, and individuals; and carrying out other activities in accordance with the law on personal data protection." It does not name dossier filing. Impact-assessment dossiers are submitted "online, in person, or by post to the personal data protection authority" (Decree 356 Arts. 18(4) and 19(4)). The Portal is a named channel for (a) **updates** to impact-assessment dossiers, PDPL Art. 22(3): updating "is carried out on the National Personal Data Protection Information Portal or at the personal data protection authority", and (b) **violation notifications**, Decree 356 Art. 28(2).
  - *Flagged, unreconciled:* PDPL Art. 22(3) names the Portal as an update channel, while Decree 356 Art. 20(3) states that updates are made using Form No. 03a/03b "and submitted online, in person, or by post to the personal data protection authority" without naming the Portal. The two held texts are not aligned on this point; adopting organizations should confirm the operative update channel with the authority rather than rely on either wording alone.

## Personal data protection function (DPO equivalent)

- **PDPL Art. 33(2):** agencies and organizations "are responsible for designating departments and personnel with the capacity to protect personal data or hiring organizations and individuals to provide personal data protection services." There is no single-officer "DPO" construct; the obligation is a designated unit, designated personnel, or an outsourced provider.
- **Designation formality and competency (Decree 356 Art. 13):** designation of personal data protection personnel or a personal data protection unit must be made through an official written document of the relevant agency or organization, specifying the assignment, functions, tasks, entitlements, and other requirements relating to personal data protection within that agency or organization (Art. 13(1)). Designated personnel must hold "at least a college-level degree or higher"; have "at least 2 years of working experience (from the time of graduation)" in one of legal affairs, information technology, cybersecurity, data security, risk management, compliance control, or personnel management/organization; and have "received training and advanced training in legal knowledge and professional skills relating to personal data protection" (Art. 13(2)). Agencies and organizations must enter into agreements on responsibility for confidentiality with personal data protection personnel (Art. 13(5)).
  - Where the function is outsourced to an **individual**, Decree 356 Art. 15(2) requires a higher bar: at least 3 years' relevant experience. An **organization** providing the service must have "at least 3 personnel satisfying the competency conditions specified in Clause 2 Article 15" (Art. 16(1)(b)).
- **Tasks (Decree 356 Art. 14):** policy, procedure, regulation and form development; organizing the exercise of data subject rights; periodic compliance assessment reporting; preparing cross-border transfer and processing impact assessment dossiers; receiving and reporting violations; training; technical confidentiality measures, standards, and "emergency response plans for personal data protection incidents".
- **Scale-based relief (PDPL Art. 38(2), (3), as applied by Decree 356 Art. 41):** small enterprises and start-ups "have the right to choose whether or not to implement the provisions in Article 21, Article 22 and Clause 2, Article 33 of this Law within 05 years from the effective date of this Law" (PDPL Art. 38(2)); business households and micro-enterprises "are not required to comply" with the same provisions (PDPL Art. 38(3)). Both carve-outs are **disapplied** for entities "providing personal data processing services, directly processing sensitive personal data, or processing personal data from the time their scale reaches **100 thousand or more personal data subject matters, based on the accumulated total volume of personal data processed**" (Decree 356 Art. 41(1) and 41(2)). The PDPL's phrase "a large number of personal data subjects" is given this numeric threshold by Decree 356 Art. 41, and the threshold bites **from the time the accumulated total reaches 100,000**.

## Impact assessments

- **Who must hold a processing impact assessment dossier.** Decree 356 Art. 19(1) requires the personal data controlling party, the personal data processing and controlling party, **and the personal data processing party** to prepare and retain their dossier for the impact assessment of personal data processing from the time personal data processing commences. The duty therefore extends to the processing party, not only to controllers and controller-processors. PDPL Art. 21(3) makes the processor's version contract-dependent: the personal data processor must establish and maintain a record of the impact assessment of personal data processing **in accordance with the agreement** with the personal data controller and the personal data controller and processor, except in the case specified in Art. 21(6).
- **Processing impact assessment filing (PDPL Art. 21(1); Decree 356 Art. 19(4)):** one original copy to the personal data protection authority "within 60 days from the first day of processing personal data" (PDPL Art. 21(1)); Decree 356 Art. 19(4) adds that the dossier must be kept readily available for inspection and assessment and that the original is "submitted online, in person, or by post to the personal data protection authority together with Form No. 02a/02b … within 60 days from the date personal data processing is carried out". Conducted "once for the entire period of operation" and updated per Art. 22 (PDPL Art. 21(2)). Dossier components and report contents are in Decree 356 Art. 19(2), (3) (report on Form No. 10; copy of the processing contract or agreement; policies, procedures, regulations and forms).
  - Authority review: under Decree 356 Art. 19(5) the authority must assess and issue results indicating whether the dossier meets or fails to meet requirements **within 15 days**; under Art. 19(6), where the dossier is incomplete or non-compliant the authority must assess and request completion **within 30 days**, and on failure to complete must consider applying regulations on penalties for administrative violations in the field of personal data protection.
  - **Exemption:** PDPL Art. 21(6), "Competent state agencies are not required to comply with the provisions on assessment of the impact of personal data processing specified in this Article."
- **Cross-border transfer impact assessment (PDPL Art. 20(2)):** one original to the authority "within 60 days from the first day of transferring personal data across borders", except the Art. 20(6) cases. Dossier components are specified in Decree 356 Art. 18(2) (report on Form No. 09; a copy of the transfer contract or document evidencing binding obligations; and the transferring party's personal data protection policies, procedures, regulations and forms). See the cross-border section below for the Art. 18(3), 18(5) and 18(6) requirements.
- **Updating (PDPL Art. 22; Decree 356 Art. 20):**
  - **6-month clock and its start.** PDPL Art. 22(1) requires updating "periodically every 6 months when there is a change". Decree 356 Art. 20(1) fixes the clock start: dossiers must be updated periodically every 6 months **from the date of initial submission** in the cases of new processing/transfer purposes (Art. 20(1)(a)) or where the controlling, processing-and-controlling, processing or third parties "arise or are changed" (Art. 20(1)(b)).
  - **Immediate updates are bounded by a 10-day period.** PDPL Art. 22(2) lists the immediate-update triggers (reorganization, cessation, dissolution or bankruptcy; change of personal data protection service provider; change in the registered business lines, professions or services related to personal data processing). Decree 356 Art. 20(2) requires that the changes it lists be updated **immediately within 10 days**, "immediately" is operationalized as a 10-day outer limit, not an open-ended obligation.
  - **Channel.** PDPL Art. 22(3): updating "is carried out on the National Personal Data Protection Information Portal or at the personal data protection authority". Decree 356 Art. 20(3): updating is carried out using Form No. 03a/03b "and submitted online, in person, or by post to the personal data protection authority". See the unreconciled flag under "Filing channels" above.

## AI and privacy obligations

- **Notification, explanation and opt-out for automated processing (Decree 356 Art. 10(3)):** the controlling party and the processing-and-controlling party are "responsible for notifying the personal data subject matter of automated personal data processing, explaining the operating principles of the algorithms and their impacts on the legitimate rights and interests of the personal data subject matter, and providing options enabling the personal data subject matter to exercise the right to opt out." This is materially broader than a bare disclosure duty.
- **AI inference outputs are personal data (Decree 356 Art. 10(2)):** data derived from artificial intelligence inference results, where such data can be used to identify or assist in identifying a specific individual, is subject to personal data protection measures in accordance with the law.
- **Risk classification (PDPL Art. 30(4)):** "The processing of personal data by artificial intelligence must be classified according to the level of risk in order to have appropriate measures to protect personal data."
- **Purpose and scope limits (PDPL Art. 30(1), (2)):** personal data in "big data, artificial intelligence, blockchain, virtual universe and cloud computing" must be "processed for the right purpose and limited to the necessary scope", and processing must be "in accordance with ethical standards and Vietnamese customs and traditions".
- **Security and access controls (PDPL Art. 30(3)):** such systems and services "must integrate appropriate personal data security measures; must use appropriate authentication, identification and access authorization methods to process personal data."
- **Prohibition (PDPL Art. 30(5)):** do not use or develop big data, AI, blockchain, virtual universe or cloud computing systems using personal data "to harm national defense, security, social order and safety or infringe upon the life, health, honor, dignity, or property of others."
- **Required AI measures (Decree 356 Art. 10(5)):** cybersecurity and comprehensive data protection standards for AI systems "with particular emphasis on information confidentiality, algorithm reliability, system stability, and cyberattack prevention capacity"; supervision mechanisms "in two aspects: supervision by competent state authorities, and accountability toward the personal data subject matter of the personal data controlling party and the personal data processing and controlling party"; "supervision systems and early-warning mechanisms for cybersecurity threats"; mechanisms "to control and prevent the abuse of artificial intelligence systems and the metaverse for activities infringing on national security or social order and safety"; and "**Conducting periodic assessments of compliance with personal data protection regulations once per year**" (Art. 10(5)(dd)).
- **Profile rights (Decree 356 Art. 10(6)):** the data subject has the right to modify, anonymize, and delete identification profiles, including in cases where platforms store behavioral history.
- **Research and development permitted (Decree 356 Art. 10(1)):** organizations and individuals "may use personal data for the research and development of self-learning algorithms, artificial intelligence systems, and other automated systems, provided that compliance with personal data protection regulations is ensured."
- **Separate AI legislation, not held, not relied upon.** Secondary sources report that Vietnam has also adopted a standalone law on artificial intelligence; its text is not held in the reference library, so this annex asserts nothing about its number, dates, scope, structure or operative provisions and places no reliance on it, organizations with AI systems in Vietnam must assess that instrument separately against its own text.

## Cross-border transfer mechanisms

- **Covered transfers (PDPL Art. 20(1)):** transferring "personal data stored in Vietnam to a data storage system located outside the territory"; transfers by agencies, organizations and individuals in Vietnam "to organizations and individuals abroad"; and use by parties "in Vietnam or abroad using platforms outside the territory … to process personal data collected in Vietnam". Decree 356 Art. 17(1) restates these three cases and expressly includes transfer "to cloud computing services of foreign service providers" (Art. 17(1)(a)).
- **Mechanism, filing, not approval.** The PDPL does **not** condition transfer on consent plus a recipient-adequacy test, a bilateral agreement, or prior approval. The mechanism is a **cross-border transfer impact assessment dossier filed with the authority within 60 days of the first transfer** (PDPL Art. 20(2)), "conducted once for the entire duration of the operation of that agency, organization or individual" and updated per Art. 22 (PDPL Art. 20(3)). Decree 356 Art. 18(4) confirms the dossier is "kept readily available for inspection and assessment by the personal data protection authority" and that "1 original complete dossier" is submitted "online, in person, or by post to the personal data protection authority, together with Form No. 01a/01b … within 60 days from the date of commencement of the cross-border transfer".
- **Recipient protection level must nonetheless be assessed in the dossier (Decree 356 Art. 18(3)(i)).** Although there is no adequacy or approval regime, the impact assessment report must contain "**Assessment of the level of personal data protection of the personal data receiving party**; the degree of impact and risks arising from the cross-border transfer and processing of personal data; potential adverse consequences or damages; and measures to mitigate or eliminate such risks." This is a mandatory dossier component, not an optional annex. Art. 18(3) also requires, among other contents, a description of onward-transfer procedures by the recipient (Art. 18(3)(g)), system diagrams for the receiving systems (Art. 18(3)(e)), post-transfer safety plans (Art. 18(3)(dd)), and self-assessment results (Art. 18(3)(h)).
- **Authority determination and completion window.**
  - Decree 356 Art. 18(5): the personal data protection authority must assess and issue results indicating whether the dossier for the impact assessment of the cross-border transfer of personal data **meets or fails to meet requirements within 15 days**.
  - Decree 356 Art. 18(6): where the dossier for the impact assessment of the cross-border transfer of personal data is incomplete or non-compliant, the personal data protection authority must assess and request the cross-border personal data transferring party to **complete the dossier within 30 days**. Where the transferring party fails to complete the dossier in accordance with regulations, the authority must consider applying regulations on **penalties for administrative violations** in the field of personal data protection in accordance with the law.
  - Practical effect: the regime is a filing regime carrying a meets/fails determination and a dossier-completion window, plan for a 15-day determination and a 30-day cure period rather than treating filing as the end of the process.
- **Exemptions.**
  - **PDPL Art. 20(6):** cross-border transfer by competent state agencies (a); storage by agencies and organizations of "personal data of employees of those agencies and organizations on cloud computing services" (b); transfer by the data subject of their own personal data (c); and "Other cases as prescribed by the Government" (d).
  - **Decree 356 Art. 17(3) fills in Art. 20(6)(d).** "In cases other than those specified in Points a, b, and c Clause 6 Article 20 of the Law on Personal Data Protection, the following cases are not required to conduct an impact assessment of cross-border transfer of personal data: a) Journalism and communication activities in accordance with the law; b) Cross-border transfer of personal data that has been disclosed in accordance with the law; c) Emergencies where it is genuinely necessary to provide personal data across borders to protect life, health, or property safety of individuals or to perform tasks and obligations as prescribed by law; d) Cross-border transfer of personal data for cross-border personnel management in accordance with labor rules and regulations, and collective labor agreements as prescribed by law; dd) Provision of personal data across borders for contract conclusion or for carrying out procedures related to cross-border transportation, logistics, remittance, payment, accommodation, visa applications, or scholarship applications."
- **Supervision and suspension.** PDPL Art. 20(4): the authority may inspect cross-border transfers periodically "no more than once a year" or conduct surprise inspections "when detecting violations … or when incidents of personal data disclosure or loss occur". PDPL Art. 20(5): the authority "decides to request the suspension of cross-border transfer of personal data … when it discovers that the personal data transferred for use in activities may harm national defense and security." Decree 356 Art. 17(2) specifies the two cessation grounds: where transferred data "is discovered to be used for activities infringing on national defense or national security" (a), and where violations occur "that may cause harm to national defense or national security" (b).
- **Data localization.** Neither the PDPL nor Decree 356 imposes an in-country storage requirement or a minimum in-country retention period for personal data, including sensitive personal data of Vietnamese citizens; searches of both held texts return no such duty. Where an in-country storage obligation exists in Vietnamese law, it arises under instruments **outside this annex's scope whose texts are not held in the reference library**, this annex therefore does not identify those instruments or assert their content. Adopting organizations must assess any localization duty separately against the applicable source texts.
  - One interaction is held: Decree 356 Art. 42(3) amends Clause 2 Art. 16 of Decree No. 165/2025/ND-CP so that the protection of core data and important data constituting personal data must be implemented in accordance with the Law on Personal Data Protection and documents elaborating that Law, and so that, in cases of cross-border transfer or processing of such data, the governing body of that data must prepare dossiers for personal data processing impact assessment and cross-border personal data transfer impact assessment in accordance with the law on personal data protection, and is not required to conduct risk assessment or impact assessment for cross-border data transfer or processing as prescribed in Decree 165/2025. PDPL Art. 5(4) states the same substitution at statute level.

## Enforcement and fines

- **PDPL Art. 8(1):** violations may attract administrative sanctions or criminal prosecution, "if causing damage, they must compensate according to the provisions of law".
- **PDPL Art. 8(3):** maximum administrative fine for **buying and selling personal data** is "10 times the amount of revenue from the violation", falling back to the Art. 8(5) cap where there is no such revenue or the calculated fine is lower.
- **PDPL Art. 8(4):** maximum administrative fine for organizations violating **cross-border transfer** rules is "5% of the organization's revenue of the previous year", with the same fallback.
- **PDPL Art. 8(5):** maximum administrative fine for **other** personal data protection violations is **VND 3 billion**.
- **PDPL Art. 8(6):** the maxima apply to organizations, and an individual committing the same violation faces a maximum fine equal to half of the fine for organizations.
- **PDPL Art. 8(7):** the PDPL delegates to the Government the method for calculating the revenue obtained from committing violations of the law on personal data protection, the calculation method is not in either held text.
- **Dossier-completion failure** is separately routed to administrative penalties by Decree 356 Arts. 18(6) and 19(6).
