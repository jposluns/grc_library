## 1. Purpose

This policy governs the monitoring of workforce network activity: the collection, analysis, and use of telemetry, traffic signals, and presence data generated by personnel and their devices on organization systems and networks. It exists to keep monitoring lawful, proportionate, transparent, and confined to legitimate security and compliance aims, and to place explicit limits on how monitoring signals may be used.

Monitoring is a necessary security capability and a significant intrusion on workforce privacy at the same time. This policy holds both facts together: it authorizes the monitoring the organization genuinely needs, and it forbids the uses that monitoring most readily drifts into. It complements the [Acceptable Use Policy](policy-acceptable-use.md) (which reserves the organization's right to monitor) by defining the conditions, safeguards, and prohibitions that govern how that right is exercised.

---

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 In scope

1. Applies to all employees, contractors, consultants, and any other party authorized to access organization systems, networks, or data.
2. Covers monitoring of workforce network activity on organization-owned or managed devices, networks, and services, including:
   - network traffic metadata (source, destination, volume, timing, protocol);
   - connection, session, and authentication telemetry;
   - endpoint, network, and cloud security signals generated in the course of work;
   - presence and availability signals derived from network connection or system sign-in; and
   - device and network telemetry collected for security and compliance purposes; operational-health and capacity telemetry (metrics, latency, saturation) is governed by the operations observability and telemetry standard, not this policy.
3. Applies to access from any location, including offices, home, and travel, where activity traverses or connects to organization systems or networks.

### 2.2 Out of scope

1. Personal devices, personal accounts, and personal premises, except where a personal device connects to organization resources, in which case only the organization-resource interaction is in scope and only to the extent permitted by the applicable device-access terms.
2. Legally privileged or protected communications, whose content is never inspected under this policy (Section 7). The content of other personal communications is not inspected except under the exceptional, defined-approval path in Section 4.
3. Routine HR and performance management, which is governed by the organization's HR management processes and is expressly not a purpose of the monitoring this policy authorizes; the [Human Capital and Ethical Conduct Framework](../governance/framework-human-capital-and-ethical-conduct.md) governs the ethical-conduct dimension of workforce oversight.
4. Investigative monitoring conducted under the [Insider Risk Programme Framework](framework-insider-risk-programme.md), which operates under that framework's own lawful-basis, proportionality, and due-process safeguards; this policy governs the general monitoring baseline, and the insider-risk framework governs targeted, signal-triggered investigation.

## 3. Proportionality and necessity

1. **Legitimate aim.** Monitoring is conducted only in pursuit of a specified, legitimate aim: security, integrity, availability, legal or regulatory compliance, or the protection of organization and personnel from harm. An aim that cannot be stated does not justify monitoring; a stated legitimate aim is necessary but not sufficient, and each monitoring capability additionally requires a documented lawful basis under Section 4.
2. **Necessity.** A monitoring capability is deployed only where the aim cannot reasonably be achieved by a less intrusive means. Necessity is assessed before deployment and re-assessed at each material change.
3. **Least intrusive means.** Where an aim can be met with metadata rather than content, aggregate signals rather than individual signals, or targeted rather than blanket collection, the less intrusive option is used.
4. **No blanket content inspection.** Continuous or blanket inspection of communication content is prohibited absent a specific, documented justification approved under Section 4. Baseline monitoring operates on metadata, security signals, and telemetry, not on the content of workforce communications.
5. **Data minimization.** Each monitoring capability collects the minimum data necessary for its stated aim. Collection "in case it is useful later" is not a legitimate basis.
6. **Proportionality of retention and access.** The intrusiveness of a capability is bounded not only at collection but through restricted access (Section 9) and minimized retention (Section 8).

---

## 4. Lawful-basis posture and impact assessment

1. **Documented lawful basis.** The Data Protection Officer documents the lawful basis for each monitoring capability that processes personal data, by reference to applicable privacy and employment law and to the [Privacy and Data Governance Policy](../privacy/policy-privacy-and-data-governance.md). Where the basis relied on is a legitimate interest, that interest is balanced against the rights, freedoms, and reasonable privacy expectations of the affected personnel, and the balancing is documented.
2. **Impact assessment.** A privacy impact assessment (or the applicable data protection impact assessment) is completed before a new or materially changed monitoring capability is deployed where required by law or by the organization's assessment threshold. The assessment records the aim, the necessity and proportionality analysis, the data collected, the retention period, the access controls, and the residual risk.
3. **Consultation and codetermination.** Where applicable law or agreement requires consultation with, or the agreement of, workforce representatives (for example works councils or trade unions), that consultation is completed before deployment and its outcome recorded. The organization operates to the strictest applicable employment, privacy, and consultation rules across its footprint.
4. **Cross-jurisdiction posture.** Where the workforce spans jurisdictions with differing monitoring rules, monitoring is designed to the strictest applicable standard unless a jurisdiction-specific carve-out is documented and approved.
5. **Stricter-is-safer.** Where two applicable rules conflict on what monitoring is permitted, the more protective of worker privacy governs unless a specific legal obligation requires otherwise.
6. **Approval of exceptional content inspection.** Inspection of communication content beyond metadata and security signals is an exceptional measure. It is never conducted on legally privileged or protected communications. For other content, inspection requires a specific, documented, and lawful justification approved in advance by the Chief Information Security Officer and Legal Counsel, with the Data Protection Officer's independent advice; it is time-bound, narrowly scoped, and recorded.

## 5. Transparency and notice

1. **Workforce is informed.** Personnel are informed of the nature, purpose, and scope of monitoring before it applies to them, and on an ongoing basis, through this policy, the [Acceptable Use Policy](policy-acceptable-use.md), and the applicable privacy notice.
2. **Accessible policy.** This policy is published and accessible to all personnel. Material changes are communicated, not merely posted.
3. **What is disclosed.** The categories of data collected, the purposes, the broad retention approach, and the roles with access are disclosed. Specific security detection logic and thresholds may be withheld where disclosure would defeat the security purpose, consistent with the insider-risk framework's notice safeguard; withholding operational detail does not permit withholding the existence, nature, or purpose of monitoring.
4. **No covert baseline monitoring.** Baseline monitoring under this policy is not covert, and covert monitoring is prohibited (Section 7).
5. **Onboarding and awareness.** Awareness of monitoring is included in joiner orientation and periodic awareness activities, coordinated with the [Human Capital and Ethical Conduct Framework](../governance/framework-human-capital-and-ethical-conduct.md).

---

## 6. Conduct of monitoring

1. Monitoring capabilities are approved, inventoried, and owned before deployment, with a named owner and a documented aim, lawful basis, and retention period.
2. Baseline monitoring operates on network metadata, telemetry, and security signals as defined in the [Logging and Monitoring Standard](standard-logging-and-monitoring.md).
3. Access to monitoring data is role-restricted, logged, and reviewed (Section 9).
4. Signals that warrant investigation are handled through the applicable procedure: security events through the security incident response process, and insider-risk signals through the [Insider Risk Programme Framework](framework-insider-risk-programme.md), which requires validation before escalation and human, due-process handling of any employment consequence.
5. Monitoring configurations, coverage, and integrity are periodically reviewed so that monitoring remains fit for its stated aim and no broader than it.

---

## 7. Prohibited uses

The following uses of workforce monitoring signals are prohibited.

1. **No automated discipline.** Monitoring signals must not trigger disciplinary, performance, or employment action automatically. Any employment consequence arising from a monitoring signal requires human review, validation of the signal against context, and handling under the applicable due-process procedure. A monitoring system must not, by itself, sanction, rank, restrict, or penalize a worker.
2. **Presence is not proof of work.** Network presence, absence, connection duration, sign-in timing, or activity volume must not be used as a productivity metric or as the basis for a performance judgement about an individual. These signals indicate connection, not contribution; treating them as a measure of work is prohibited. Availability and presence data collected for security or operational purposes must not be repurposed to assess or compare individual output.
3. **No monitoring of protected or private activity.** Monitoring must not target lawful protected activity, including trade-union or workforce-representation activity, whistleblowing, protected concerted activity, or the exercise of a legal right. The content of legally privileged or protected communications is not inspected.
4. **No secondary-purpose creep.** Data collected for a stated monitoring purpose must not be repurposed for an unrelated purpose. Purpose limitation applies across the lifecycle: security telemetry is not silently reused for HR, performance, marketing, or commercial purposes. A new purpose requires its own lawful basis, assessment, and notice.
5. **No covert monitoring.** Covert or undisclosed monitoring is prohibited, consistent with the [Insider Risk Programme Framework](framework-insider-risk-programme.md), which prohibits covert techniques. Where an investigation is genuinely compelled by law to proceed without notice, it is conducted outside this policy's monitoring programme and outside the insider-risk programme, under the direction of Legal Counsel and only to the extent the law requires; it is never a standing capability of this policy.
6. **No monitoring beyond scope.** Monitoring must not extend to personal devices, personal accounts, or personal premises beyond the narrow organization-resource interaction defined in Section 2, and must not be used to build a general profile of a worker's lawful off-duty conduct.

## 8. Retention and minimization

1. Monitoring and telemetry data is retained only for as long as necessary for its stated purpose, then destroyed or de-identified, consistent with the principles in the [Data Retention Schedule](../governance/register-data-retention-schedule.md) and the [Privacy and Data Governance Policy](../privacy/policy-privacy-and-data-governance.md).
2. Retention periods are organization-defined and recorded in the [Data Retention Schedule](../governance/register-data-retention-schedule.md); they are not prescribed by this policy or attributed to any external standard. The register is the authority for the applicable period of each data category.
3. Data retained for an active investigation is kept for the period necessary for that investigation plus any documented limitation period, per the insider-risk framework's retention safeguard, and is then destroyed unless a legal hold applies.
4. Legal holds override retention schedules until lifted.
5. Where monitoring data can serve its purpose in aggregate or de-identified form, it is minimized to that form as early as the purpose allows.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)** | Owns this policy; approves monitoring capabilities and their aims; accountable for proportionate, lawful conduct of monitoring; joint approver, with Legal Counsel, of any exceptional content inspection. |
| **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** | Independently advises on and reviews the lawful basis, balancing test, and impact assessment for each capability, and reviews monitoring for proportionality, purpose limitation, retention, and subject rights. Acting as an independent adviser and monitor, the DPO does not determine or approve monitoring measures. |
| **HR / People Operations** | Prevents monitoring signals from being used for automated discipline or as a productivity metric; leads any due-process handling where a validated signal reaches employment action; coordinates workforce notice and consultation. |
| **Line managers** | Do not request, receive, or act on individual monitoring signals as a performance or presence measure; route security or conduct concerns through the defined channels rather than direct surveillance. |
| **Insider Risk function** | Conducts targeted, signal-triggered investigation only under the [Insider Risk Programme Framework](framework-insider-risk-programme.md), with validation before escalation and due process on any consequence. |
| **Legal Counsel** | Advises on lawful basis and consultation obligations; joint approver, with the CISO, of any exceptional content inspection; directs any legally compelled no-notice investigation. |
| **Internal Audit** | Provides independent assurance that monitoring is proportionate, disclosed, access-restricted, minimally retained, and free of the prohibited uses in Section 7. |
| **Workforce representatives** | Consulted where law or agreement requires, before deployment or material change. |

---

## 10. Oversight and assurance

1. Monitoring capabilities are inventoried and reviewed at least annually against this policy, the necessity and proportionality tests, and the prohibited-use list.
2. Access to monitoring data is logged and reviewed; unauthorized access or use is treated as a policy violation and, where applicable, a security incident.
3. Internal Audit reviews the monitoring programme independently at least annually, including for over-collection, secondary-purpose creep, automated-discipline drift, and misuse of presence data.
4. Findings are recorded and remediated through the organization's risk and corrective-action processes.

---

## 11. Violations

Misuse of workforce monitoring, including monitoring outside an approved capability, use of monitoring signals for a prohibited purpose (Section 7), unauthorized access to monitoring data, or covert monitoring, is a serious violation of this policy. Violations are handled under the applicable security disciplinary process and HR disciplinary framework, with due process, and may be reported to regulators or law enforcement where required by law. Monitoring itself is never a substitute for that due process.
