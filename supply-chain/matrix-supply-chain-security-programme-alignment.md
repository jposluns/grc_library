## Purpose

This matrix provides a consolidated cross-reference of the supply chain security and trusted-trader programmes applicable to the organization's operations. It does not treat every listed programme as internationally recognized: the Mutual Recognition column records what the held sources confirm for each programme, and BASC (a private certification run by the World BASC Organization) and NEEC are not recorded in the WCO SAFE Framework (2025 edition) or the WCO AEO Compendium (2020). It maps the security domains and key requirements of each programme to identify overlaps, shared evidence opportunities, and coverage gaps across all active and target programme memberships.

## Security domain alignment matrix

The following matrix maps the common supply chain security domains against each programme's requirements. ✓ = required by this programme; ~ = partially or conditionally required (for the WCO SAFE column, the rule below governs); N/A = not addressed by this programme's published criteria; Unverifiable = ISO 28000 requirement content cannot be confirmed from the reference base. For AEO-S (UK), the held HMRC criteria (UK HMRC AEO security and safety criteria handbook, section 11) name the computer system only as an area the security and safety risk assessment is to address (§11.2), but the record keeping criterion, which §1.4 lists among the AEO-S criteria, asks for appropriate information technology security measures to protect computer systems from unauthorized intrusion and to secure documentation and data (§8.1), with detail in §8.9 to §8.12, so IT and Cybersecurity is marked ✓ (§1.4, §8.1). On incidents, §11.4 expects security procedures to be reviewed and amended after an incident, §11.8 (building and boundary security) asks how security incidents are reported and dealt with, not limited to cargo units and with no recipient named, and §11.12 (cargo security) expects procedures for cargo units that say how incidents are reported and recorded and what action is to be taken, including reporting to law enforcement or senior management. Reporting to HMRC rests on the handbook's section on maintaining the standards: businesses must notify HMRC of all changes that may affect their AEO status, including disclosure of security incidents, and should notify the AEO Central Site as soon as the change is known or, at the latest, within 14 calendar days of the change, using form C122 (§12.4), so Incident Reporting to Authority is marked ✓ for AEO-S (UK). The AEO-S (UK) marks for Documentation and Manifest Accuracy, Customs Compliance Record and Financial Solvency rest on other held sections of the HMRC Customs Technical Handbook (AEO). Its introduction says AEO-S status is issued to a business established in the UK that fulfils the criteria of customs compliance, appropriate record keeping standards, financial solvency, and appropriate security and safety standards directly related to the activity carried out (§1.4). HMRC examines the applicant's record of compliance with customs and tax requirements over the 3 years before the application (§7.1), so Customs Compliance Record is marked ✓, and the financial solvency requirements can be met if the business can prove good financial standing (§9.1), so Financial Solvency is marked ✓. The record keeping criteria ask for robust procedures for verifying the accuracy of customs declarations submitted by third parties, where appropriate (§8.1), and for documented procedures for verifying the accuracy of customs declarations (§8.7); no held section names manifests, so Documentation and Manifest Accuracy is marked ~. The back-up, recovery, fallback and archiving section asks for satisfactory procedures for archiving records and information and for protection against the loss of information (§8.9), which is not business continuity, and a business continuity or disaster recovery plan is among the actions businesses should consider for the protection of computer systems (§8.10) and the actions documentation security should normally include (§8.12), so Business Continuity is marked ~ (§8.9, §8.10, §8.12). For the WCO SAFE column, each domain's mark follows the element of the WCO SAFE Framework (2025 edition) Annex IV that the domain mainly rests on, and Annex IV states each element cited here as a requirement ("This element requires that"). Customs Compliance Record and Risk Assessment are marked ~ because the elements they mainly rest on are qualified by the national AEO programme: element A (a) sets the compliance period as one "determined by the national AEO programme", and element M (a) requires security risk assessments "as specifically outlined in the national AEO programme". Incident Reporting to Authority, a ✓ domain whose main element is listed after the table, also contains a qualified sub-item, element H (d), under which the AEO is to "require operators, as specifically detailed in national AEO programmes, to report any actual or suspicious" incident to designated security department staff of both the AEO and Customs. The other WCO SAFE domains are marked ✓ because the elements they mainly rest on state the requirement without that qualifier. Of these, IT and Cybersecurity rests on element F (b) in the list for the AEO alone, under which the AEO is to "have a documented information security policy and procedures and/or security-related controls" in place "to protect the AEO’s electronic systems from unauthorized access", Security Assessment / Audit rests on element M (b), under which the AEO is to "establish and conduct regular self-assessments of its security management system", and Business Continuity rests on element L (a), under which the AEO and Customs are to develop and document "contingency plans for emergency security situations and for disaster or terrorist incident recovery" in conjunction with the appropriate authorities "where advisable or necessary", a phrase that limits only the coordination with the authorities, not the requirement for the plans. Financial Solvency rests on element C (a), under which the AEO "is required to be in good financial standing which is sufficient to fulfil its commitments"; the element adds that financial indicators can be used to establish ratio measurements that help identify the operator's financial standing and that "This process falls within the vetting and validation procedures of Members’ AEO programmes", so its programme reference covers that assessment process (the ratio measurements), not the requirement, which the element states without a programme reference, and Financial Solvency is marked ✓. A ✓ domain can still contain a qualified sub-item: Cargo and Conveyance Security includes element G (g), under which the AEO is to "regularly examine, through particular mechanisms set forth in the national AEO programme, its security" and control procedures, and element H (d). Only the national AEO programme qualifier moves a WCO SAFE mark to ~. Other conditions, such as "if necessary" in element K (a) and, in element I (a), "in accordance with its business model and risk analysis", "If access control is not possible" and "as appropriate and based on risk", do not. The ✓ domains whose main element is not named above rest on the elements listed after the table.

## IT and cybersecurity requirements comparison

The comparison below indicates whether each programme addresses a control area, not that each publishes an identically enumerated technical requirement. Programmes vary in specificity: PIP, for example, states its cybersecurity expectations at a high level within its corporate security area rather than as a numbered IT standard. The held HMRC security and safety criteria for AEO-S (UK) (handbook section 11) set out no IT control: they name the computer system only as an area the security and safety risk assessment is to address (§11.2). The IT security measures the held handbook names sit under the record keeping criterion, which §1.4 lists among the AEO-S criteria: appropriate information technology security measures to protect computer systems from unauthorized intrusion and to secure documentation and data (§8.1); back-up, recovery, fallback and archiving (§8.9); protection of computer systems, where there should be appropriate security measures, for example a firewall, an antivirus programme and password protection, and where businesses should consider details of firewalls, a business continuity or disaster recovery plan, back-up routines, logs of each user and their actions and periodic vulnerability management (§8.10); access rights procedures, including the format for setting passwords (§8.11); and documentation security (§8.12). The AEO-S (UK) column marks each row a held section holds with that section, ✓ where the section says there should be such a measure or procedure and ~ where it covers the row in part or lists it among the actions businesses should consider, so security awareness training cites the security and safety training of section 11.15 and third-party IT security the external service providers of section 11.14, and it records the organization's own scoping of the HMRC criteria, not HMRC text, in the other rows: no held section names multi-factor authentication, encryption or an asset inventory. Of the eight cited ~ rows, vulnerability management and audit logging are marked ~ because periodic vulnerability management and logs of each user and their actions are among the actions businesses should consider (§8.10), vulnerability management also because testing systems against unauthorized access and recording the results, which covers it in part, is among the actions documentation security should normally include (§8.12), and six are marked ~ because their sections cover the row in part: least privilege, since access rights procedures should include how authorization for access is issued and the level of access to the computer systems, with access to sensitive information limited to staff authorized to apply changes and additions to it (§8.11), but no held section states least privilege; network security, since firewalls are named among the security measures there should be and among the actions businesses should consider, but intrusion detection is not (§8.10); incident response plan, since the actions documentation security should normally include cover dealing with incidents which compromise document security, not incident response as a whole (§8.12); security awareness training, since applicants are required to provide their staff with training on security and safety, not IT security awareness specifically (§11.15); third-party IT security, since software providers are among the external service providers to which appropriate security and safety measures should apply, with the business's requirements stated in contracts, but IT security is not named for them (§11.14); and business continuity or IT recovery, since §8.9 covers archiving records and information and protection against the loss of information, not business continuity, while a business continuity or disaster recovery plan is among the actions businesses should consider (§8.10) and the actions documentation security should normally include (§8.12). In the Notification to customs authority cell, the handbook's section 12.4 says businesses must notify HMRC of all changes that may affect their AEO status, including disclosure of security incidents and changes to accounting or computer systems, and should notify the AEO Central Site as soon as the change is known or, at the latest, within 14 calendar days of the change, using form C122, so that cell is marked ✓ (§12.4).

## Mutual recognition arrangements

Mutual recognition means that qualifying members of one programme receive facilitation benefits from a partner country's customs authority. Evidence collected for one programme may also support another, but only where that programme accepts it, requirement by requirement; the shared evidence shown below marks candidate overlaps, not established reuse.

| Programme Pair | Mutual Recognition Basis | Shared Evidence Possible |
|---|---|---|
| CTPAT ↔ AEO-S (UK) | Not confirmed by a held source: UK-US Trade and Investment Working Group; post-Brexit bilateral arrangement (the WCO AEO Compendium (2020) lists US-United Kingdom as under negotiation) | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source: most domains; UK legal supplements required |
| CTPAT ↔ AEO (EU) | US-EU Mutual Recognition Agreement (2012, a concluded MRA in the WCO AEO Compendium (2020)); the 2019 update is not confirmed by a held source | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source: most domains; EU legal supplements required |
| CTPAT ↔ PIP | Canada-US CBSA-CBP mutual recognition (2008, a concluded MRA in the WCO AEO Compendium (2020); confirm current scope against the CBSA's published arrangements) | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source; subject to jurisdiction-specific supplements |
| PIP ↔ AEO (Mexico) | Canada-Mexico AEO mutual recognition arrangement, signed Cancun 11 May 2016 (CBSA / SAT) | Facilitation benefits (fewer inspections, priority processing); the held source does not state evidence reuse |
| AEO-S ↔ AEO (EU) | Not confirmed by a held source: mutual recognition between UK AEO-S and EU AEO authorizations. The legal basis is held: since 1 January 2021, UK AEO-S authorizations have been governed by the Union Customs Code (UCC) Regulation (EU) No 952/2013, Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/2446 and Commission Implementing Regulation EU 2015/2447 as assimilated law under the European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018, amended by the Customs Safety and Security Procedures (EU Exit) Regulations 2019, and in relation to Northern Ireland the UCC and those two Regulations remain applicable under the Windsor Framework and the European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018 (HMRC Customs Technical Handbook (AEO), section 1.7) | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source; whether separate UK AEO-S and EU AEO applications are required is not confirmed by a held source (the held HMRC handbook says a business applying for UK AEO status must be established in Great Britain or Northern Ireland: HMRC Customs Technical Handbook (AEO), section 2.1) |
| AEO-S ↔ OEA (Brazil) | Not confirmed by a held source: UK-Brazil Trade Working Group framework | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source; any facilitation would depend on a UK-Brazil arrangement, which no held source records |
| AEO (EU) ↔ OEA (Brazil) | Not confirmed by a held source: EU-Brazil mutual recognition agreement (the WCO AEO Compendium (2020) lists no EU-Brazil MRA, concluded or under negotiation) | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source |

The WCO SAFE Framework is not a programme pair: it provides baseline technical guidance for the implementation of AEO programmes at the global level and is designed to serve as a starting point for national AEO programme implementation. Its 2025 edition lists among the benefits to an Authorized Economic Operator "Recognition by other Customs administrations via MRAs as a low-risk trader in the Customs automated risking system, which may lead to fewer examinations", so recognition by other Customs administrations can come through mutual recognition arrangements or agreements (MRAs). MRAs are not the only route: its Section VII, 2.1, on regional Customs union AEO programmes, says that under that concept "it is presumed that AEO status granted by the Customs administration of one Member is recognized by the Customs administrations of the other Members within the Customs union", so businesses "could benefit from AEO status across the whole region without the need for separate or additional MRAs". Confirm recognition and any evidence reuse against the applicable arrangement and each programme's requirements before relying on them. The table above lists no mutual recognition pair for BASC, NEEC, ISO 28000:2022 or ISO 28001:2007, so no mutual recognition is established here for them, nor for the pairs whose basis is marked not confirmed by a held source. AEO (Mexico) in the PIP row is not counted as NEEC's pair: the held Canada-Mexico arrangement guide names the Mexican programme Authorized Economic Operator and does not name NEEC. The CTPAT (US) recognition shown for NEEC in the programme summary and the application priority below has no held arrangement text; confirm it before relying on it.
