## Purpose

This matrix provides a consolidated cross-reference of the supply chain security and trusted-trader programmes applicable to the organization's operations. It does not treat every listed programme as internationally recognized: the Mutual Recognition column records what the held sources confirm for each programme, and BASC (a private certification run by the World BASC Organization) and NEEC are not recorded in the WCO SAFE Framework (2025 edition) or the WCO AEO Compendium (2020). It maps the security domains and key requirements of each programme to identify overlaps, shared evidence opportunities, and coverage gaps across all active and target programme memberships.

## Security domain alignment matrix

The following matrix maps the common supply chain security domains against each programme's requirements. ✓ = required by this programme; ~ = partially or conditionally required; N/A = not addressed by this programme's published criteria; Unverifiable = ISO 28000 requirement content cannot be confirmed from the reference base. For AEO-S (UK), the held HMRC criteria (UK HMRC AEO security and safety criteria handbook, section 11) name the computer system only as an area the security and safety risk assessment is to address (§11.2), so IT and Cybersecurity is marked ~. They name no incident reporting to HMRC: §11.4 expects security procedures to be reviewed and amended after an incident, §11.8 (building and boundary security) asks how security incidents are reported and dealt with, not limited to cargo units and with no recipient named, and §11.12 (cargo security) expects procedures for cargo units that say how incidents are reported and recorded and what action is to be taken, including reporting to law enforcement or senior management. Of these, only the cargo security provision names a recipient, so Incident Reporting to Authority is marked ~ for AEO-S, limited to cargo units and to those recipients.

## Mutual recognition arrangements

Mutual recognition means that qualifying members of one programme receive facilitation benefits from a partner country's customs authority. Evidence collected for one programme may also support another, but only where that programme accepts it, requirement by requirement; the shared evidence shown below marks candidate overlaps, not established reuse.

| Programme Pair | Mutual Recognition Basis | Shared Evidence Possible |
|---|---|---|
| CTPAT ↔ AEO-S (UK) | Not confirmed by a held source: UK-US Trade and Investment Working Group; post-Brexit bilateral arrangement (the WCO AEO Compendium (2020) lists US-United Kingdom as under negotiation) | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source: most domains; UK legal supplements required |
| CTPAT ↔ AEO (EU) | US-EU Mutual Recognition Agreement (2012, a concluded MRA in the WCO AEO Compendium (2020)); the 2019 update is not confirmed by a held source | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source: most domains; EU legal supplements required |
| CTPAT ↔ PIP | Canada-US CBSA-CBP mutual recognition (2008, a concluded MRA in the WCO AEO Compendium (2020); confirm current scope against the CBSA's published arrangements) | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source; subject to jurisdiction-specific supplements |
| PIP ↔ AEO (Mexico) | Canada-Mexico AEO mutual recognition arrangement, signed Cancun 11 May 2016 (CBSA / SAT) | Facilitation benefits (fewer inspections, priority processing); the held source does not state evidence reuse |
| AEO-S ↔ AEO (EU) | Not confirmed by a held source: UK HMRC aligned criteria to EU AEO post-Brexit; separate national authorizations | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source; separate applications required |
| AEO-S ↔ OEA (Brazil) | Not confirmed by a held source: UK-Brazil Trade Working Group framework | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source; any facilitation would depend on a UK-Brazil arrangement, which no held source records |
| AEO (EU) ↔ OEA (Brazil) | Not confirmed by a held source: EU-Brazil mutual recognition agreement (the WCO AEO Compendium (2020) lists no EU-Brazil MRA, concluded or under negotiation) | Candidate overlap, not confirmed by a held source |

The WCO SAFE Framework is not a programme pair: it provides baseline technical guidance for the implementation of AEO programmes at the global level and is designed to serve as a starting point for national AEO programme implementation. Its 2025 edition lists among the benefits to an Authorized Economic Operator "Recognition by other Customs administrations via MRAs as a low-risk trader in the Customs automated risking system, which may lead to fewer examinations", so recognition by other Customs administrations can come through mutual recognition arrangements or agreements (MRAs). MRAs are not the only route: its Section VII, 2.1, on regional Customs union AEO programmes, says that under that concept "it is presumed that AEO status granted by the Customs administration of one Member is recognized by the Customs administrations of the other Members within the Customs union", so businesses "could benefit from AEO status across the whole region without the need for separate or additional MRAs". Confirm recognition and any evidence reuse against the applicable arrangement and each programme's requirements before relying on them. The table above lists no mutual recognition pair for BASC, NEEC, ISO 28000:2022 or ISO 28001:2007, so no mutual recognition is established here for them, nor for the pairs whose basis is marked not confirmed by a held source. AEO (Mexico) in the PIP row is not counted as NEEC's pair: the held Canada-Mexico arrangement guide names the Mexican programme Authorized Economic Operator and does not name NEEC. The CTPAT (US) recognition shown for NEEC in the programme summary and the application priority below has no held arrangement text; confirm it before relying on it.
